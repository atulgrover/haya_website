CUTM/AGR/Q1301: Soil and Water Conservation Supervisor
Brief Job Description
The individual will get detailed insights on watershed processes and hydro-climatic data processing
technique, scientific methodology of maintaining data records, maintenance of equipment, design
consideration of different soil and water conservation structures for minimizing soil erosion and improving
crop production.
Personal Attributes
A Soil and Water Conservation Supervisor should be environmentally conscious, responsible, and
committed to sustainable resource management. They must possess strong observation and analytical
skills to monitor soil health, water flow, and conservation measures. Leadership and decision-making
abilities are essential for guiding field teams and implementing projects effectively. The role requires
adaptability to diverse terrains and climatic conditions, along with problem-solving skills for addressing
erosion, water scarcity, and land degradation. Good communication and planning skills are important for
coordinating with farmers, communities, and government agencies. Patience, attention to detail, and
dedication to environmental protection are key personal attributes.
Applicable National Occupational Standards (NOS)
Compulsory NOS:

CUTM/AGR/N1301: Understand About Various Watersheds Processes
Description
This NOS focuses on imparting knowledge of watershed dynamics and their role in soil and water
conservation. Learners will study hydrological cycles, soil erosion patterns, land-use interactions, and the
significance of groundwater recharge. It covers collection and interpretation of agricultural, climatic, and
environmental data, as well as identification of critical watershed issues. The NOS also emphasizes
prioritization of watersheds, problem analysis, and preparation of evaluation reports to support sustainable
land and water resource management.
Scope
The scope covers the following :
This NOS focuses on developing knowledge and skills to understand the functioning of watershed
systems and their impact on soil and water resources. It covers collection of localized information,
hydrological and climatic data, agricultural practices, and interpretation of topographic and
geological maps. Learners will identify critical watershed issues, such as soil erosion hazards and
groundwater recharge potential, and prioritize watersheds accordingly. The scope also includes
preparing evaluation reports that support sustainable watershed management and conservation
practices.
Elements and Performance Criteria
Understand about various watersheds processes
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC1. Collection of localized information related to selected watershed.
PC2. Understand various processes involved in a watershed.
PC3. Collect agricultural practice information, climactic data, hydrological data, environmental data,
aerial photos, topographic maps, and geological maps from relevant sources.
PC4. Identify critical problems of various watersheds based on review of literatures.
PC5. Prioritization of Watersheds based on soil erosion hazard and quality of groundwater recharge.
PC6. Make a report based on critical evaluation of the watershed.
Knowledge and Understanding (KU)
The individual on the job needs to know and understand:
KU1. Concept of Watersheds - Understanding the definition, components, and importance of a
watershed, including its role in soil and water conservation, agriculture, and ecosystem
balance.
KU2. Hydrological Cycle & Processes - Knowledge of precipitation, infiltration, runoff,
evapotranspiration, and groundwater recharge, and how these processes interact within a
watershed.

KU3. Data Collection for Watershed Analysis - Familiarity with sources and methods of collecting
agricultural, climatic, hydrological, environmental, and geological data, including use of

KU4. Watershed Characterization - Understanding land use, soil types, topography, vegetation
cover, and socio-economic conditions that influence watershed behavior.
KU5. Critical Problem Identification - Ability to identify and analyze issues such as soil erosion,

KU10. Sustainability & Conservation Linkages - Understanding how watershed management
contributes to soil fertility, water availability, climate resilience, and sustainable agricultural
practices.

User/individual on the job needs to know how to:
GS1. explain the definition, components, and functions of a watershed, including hydrological
cycle and linkages between land, soil, and water systems.
GS2. collect localized information such as agricultural practices, climatic data, hydrological data,
environmental parameters, aerial photos, and topographic maps for watershed studies.
GS3. identify and assess critical watershed problems like soil erosion, runoff intensity,
groundwater depletion, and waterlogging, using literature reviews and field observations.
GS4. prioritize watersheds based on soil erosion hazards, water recharge potential, and socio-
economic importance for effective conservation planning.
GS5. conduct ground-truth surveys, site inspections, and verification of secondary data to ensure
accuracy in watershed studies.
GS6. prepare structured technical reports, integrating scientific data and field findings to support
watershed management decisions.
GS7. Use the Scientific Tools & Techniques - Familiarity with basic watershed-related instruments
and analysis methods (e.g., soil augers, hydrometers, pH meters, GIS-based mapping tools).
GS8. evaluate multiple solutions for watershed-related challenges and adopt the most sustainable
and cost-effective measures.
GS9. communicate watershed-related findings clearly with farmers, local communities, and
government agencies, and to collaborate with multidisciplinary teams.
GS10. understand watershed processes contribute to sustainable agriculture, soil conservation,
water resource management, and climate resilience.

CUTM/AGR/N1302: Learn Data acquisition and processing techniques for
watershed management
Description
This NOS focuses on equipping learners with skills to systematically collect, analyze, and process data
critical for effective watershed management. It covers meteorological, soil, and satellite data collection,
ground-truthing, and the use of GIS/remote sensing tools for trend analysis and land use change detection.
Learners will also gain proficiency in software such as QGIS and ArcGIS to process and interpret data. The
outcome ensures the ability to prepare actionable reports for farmers and stakeholders, enabling informed
decision-making for sustainable soil and water conservation practices.
Scope
The scope covers the following :
The scope includes gathering field data from local and regional sources, performing soil sampling
and analysis, using GIS and remote sensing tools (QGIS, ArcGIS) for change detection and land-use
studies, and preparing analytical reports. This NOS ensures supervisors can integrate scientific data
with practical watershed management strategies, supporting sustainable soil and water
conservation practices.
Elements and Performance Criteria
Data Acquisition and Processing Techniques for Watershed Management
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC1. Collect meteorological data at local scale from nearby meteorological laboratory (e.g.
Agricultural University, Govt. agricultural dept., Govt. Irrigation dept. etc.)
PC2. Trend analysis of collected meteorological data.
PC3. Collect soil data at local scale by sampling technique.
PC4. Soil sample analysis (mention if any assumption made regarding grid size).
PC5. Collect soil data at regional scale from National Bureau of Soil Survey & Land use Planning.
PC6. Collect satellite data from USGS Earth explorer websit.
PC7. Change detection analysis of land use land cover of the watershed.
PC8. Process the collected satellite data by using software like QGIS and ArcGIS etc.
PC9. Make a report based on critical assessment and suggest farmers to act on that.
Knowledge and Understanding (KU)
The individual on the job needs to know and understand:
KU1. methods to collect local-scale meteorological data (rainfall, temperature, humidity, wind
speed, etc.) from relevant government or agricultural meteorological stations.
KU2. how to perform trend analysis on collected hydro-meteorological data to understand climate
variability, rainfall intensity patterns, and seasonal changes.

CUTM/AGR/N1303: Perform the process of delineation of watershed
Description
This NOS involves applying scientific and geospatial techniques to delineate watershed boundaries
accurately. It covers the use of Digital Elevation Models (DEM), hydro-meteorological data, and GIS tools to
analyze flow direction, accumulation, and stream order. The individual is expected to interpret maps,
conduct soil and land use analysis, and prepare thematic layers for effective watershed planning. By
performing delineation, the supervisor ensures better management of soil and water resources, enabling
sustainable agricultural practices and improved conservation outcomes.
Scope
The scope covers the following :
It covers the study of hydro-meteorological data, application of Digital Elevation Models (DEM), use
of GIS and remote sensing techniques, and interpretation of map features such as flow direction,
accumulation, and stream order. This scope ensures accurate watershed delineation, which is critical
for effective planning, resource management, and implementation of soil and water conservation
measures.
Elements and Performance Criteria
Delineation of Watershed
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC1. Understand the process of Watershed delineation.
PC2. Learn Digital Elevation Model data.
PC3. Be familiar with various processes involved in watershed delineation.
PC4. Study about various map features (flow direction, flow accumulation, stream order etc.)
Knowledge and Understanding (KU)
The individual on the job needs to know and understand:
KU1. what watershed delineation means, its importance in hydrology, and its role in soil and water
conservation planning.
KU2. Digital Elevation Model (DEM) Data - Knowledge of DEMs, their sources (e.g., satellite
imagery), and how elevation data is used to define watershed boundaries.
KU3. Processes in Watershed Delineation - Familiarity with steps such as identifying ridgelines,
drainage patterns, catchment areas, and defining watershed outlets.
KU4. what watershed delineation means, its importance in hydrology, and its role in soil and water
conservation planning.
KU5. what watershed delineation means, its importance in hydrology, and its role in soil and water
conservation planning.

CUTM/AGR/N1304: Study About the Processes to Improve Soil Quality
Description
This NOS focuses on enhancing and maintaining soil health to ensure sustainable agricultural productivity.
It covers techniques to increase soil organic matter, monitor and balance nutrient levels, manage soil pH,
and improve soil structure through aggregation and porosity management. Trainees will also learn
methods to assess microbial activity, control soil erosion, and track contaminant levels. By applying these
practices, soil quality can be improved for better water retention, crop yield, and long-term ecological
balance.
Scope
The scope covers the following :
It covers improving soil organic matter, maintaining optimal pH and nutrient levels, assessing soil
structure (aggregation, porosity, compaction), monitoring erosion and sediment loss, and evaluating
microbial activity. The scope also includes identifying soil contaminants, applying soil amendments,
and adopting organic and conservation practices. These processes help improve fertility, crop
productivity, and long-term soil sustainability, supporting effective watershed and land
management.
Elements and Performance Criteria
Improve Soil Quality
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC1. Improve Soil Organic Matter (SOM) Content.
PC2. Monitor and improve nutrient levels.
PC3. Maintain optimal soil pH levels.
PC4. Assessing soil aggregation, porosity, and compaction by implementing improved soil
structure.
PC5. Monitor soil erosion rates and sediment loss.

conservation practices to maintain aquifer health and ensure sustainable water availability.
Scope

It covers monitoring and reducing contaminants like nitrates, pesticides, and heavy metals, ensuring

Description
This unit is about employability skills, Constitutional values, becoming a professional in the 21st Century,
digital, financial, and legal literacy, diversity and Inclusion, English and communication skills, customer
service, entrepreneurship, and apprenticeship, getting ready for jobs and career development.
Scope
The scope covers the following :
Introduction to Employability Skills
Constitutional values - Citizenship
Becoming a Professional in the 21st Century
Basic English Skills
Career Development & Goal Setting
Communication Skills
Diversity & Inclusion
Financial and Legal Literacy
Essential Digital Skills
Entrepreneurship
Customer Service
Getting ready for Apprenticeship & Jobs
