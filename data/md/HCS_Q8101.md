HCS/Q8101: Coir Fibre Artisan
Brief Job Description
A Coir Fibre Artisan combines traditional skills with a modern touch, creating environmentally friendly
products. They are meticulous, innovative, and dedicated to quality, blending age-old techniques with
contemporary design. Their work reflects both cultural heritage and modern aesthetic sensibilities.
Personal Attributes
A Coir Fibre Artisan is meticulous and innovative, blending traditional craftsmanship with modern design
sensibilities. They are patient, detail-oriented, and adaptable, committed to producing high-quality,
sustainable products that meet contemporary market demands.

HCS/N8105: Carry out the process of Making Coir fibre
Description
The unit focuses on arranging raw materials, producing coir fibre, and ensuring the quality of the prepared
coir fibre.
Scope
The scope covers the following :
Collection and Preparation of Raw Materials and Tools
Coir Fibre Extraction Using Traditional Methods
Coir Fibre Extraction Using Modern Machines
Spinning and Twisting of Coir Fibre
Tool Maintenance and Workplace Safety
Quality Control and Record Maintenance
Elements and Performance Criteria
Collection and preparation of raw materials and tools
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC1. Gather raw materials such as coconuts and husk for coir fibre production.
PC2. Gather traditional tools, such as iron rods and hand-beaters, as well as modern machines
such as Dehusking machines, Defibering machines, and Refining machines.
PC3. Organise and arrange raw materials and tools at the workplace, ensuring accessibility and
safety.
PC4. Transport any extra materials to the designated storage area to maintain workplace order.
Coir fibre extraction using traditional methods
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC5. Dry coconut shells under sunlight for several days to aid in husk separation.
PC6. Soak dried coconut shells in water for over six months to soften the husk.
PC7. Remove the coconuts from the water and repeatedly beat them with an iron rod to remove
the fibres.
PC8. Manually peel off the outer layer to separate the husk.
PC9. Bundle the husk and soak it again for further softening.
PC10. Use husk bundles in a water tank for two to four days for refinement.
PC11. Extract fibres by manually beating the soaked husk or using traditional tools.
Coir fibre extraction using modern machines
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC12. Put the dried coconut shells into a Dehusking machine to separate the husk.
PC13. Transfer the husk to a Defibering machine to break it down into finer fibres.
PC14. Use a refining machine, locally known as 'kudu,' to remove unwanted particles and extract
clean coir fibres.

Spinning and Twisting of Coir fibre
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC16. Strengthen fibres by twisting two strands together using a traditional spinning wheel or a

PC17. Extend the fibre length by joining twine ends through friction or using a mechanised splicing

PC18. Bundle the spun coir fibres and prepare large spools for further product manufacturing.
Tool maintenance and workplace safety
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC19. Regularly clean traditional tools such as iron rods and hand-beaters to prevent rusting.
PC20. Perform routine checks on modern machines such as Dehusking and Defibering machines to

PC21. Lubricate moving parts of machines to minimise wear and tear.
PC22. Report any mechanical issues immediately to avoid production delays.
PC23. Follow safety protocols, such as wearing protective gear and ensuring proper machine
handling practices.
Quality control and record maintenance
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC24. Inspect raw materials to ensure they meet quality standards before processing.
PC25. Monitor each stage of the production process to maintain consistent quality.
PC26. Record the quantity of materials used and fibres produced to track productivity.
PC27. Minimise wastage through careful handling and efficient processing techniques.
PC28. Prepare inspection reports and submit them to the supervisor for review.
Knowledge and Understanding (KU)
The individual on the job needs to know and understand:
KU1. Understand the types and quality of coconuts used for coir fibre production.
KU2. Recognise the significance of soaking time and conditions required for husk softening.
KU3. Understand traditional methods like manual beating and peeling of husk.
KU4. Gain knowledge of modern machinery such as Dehusking machines, Defibering machines,
refining machines (kudu), and spinning machines.
KU5. Comprehend each step in the coir fibre extraction process, from drying and soaking to
spinning and bundling.
KU6. Understand the importance of fibre refinement and methods for achieving better quality.
KU7. Identify the correct usage of tools like iron rods, hand-beaters, and spinning wheels.
KU8. Learn the proper handling, maintenance, and troubleshooting of modern machines to
prevent breakdowns.
KU9. Understand quality parameters for assessing coir fibres, such as strength, texture, and
colour.
KU10. Understand safety protocols when handling sharp tools and operating machinery.

HCS/N8106: Carry out the Process of making Coir products
Description
This unit covers the process of crafting coir products from raw fibre to finished goods, ensuring quality and
sustainability at every stage.
Scope
The scope covers the following :
Preparing the Coir Fibres
Spinning and Yarn Making
Weaving and Braiding Techniques
Product Shaping and Assembly
Product Specification in Coir Product Making
Product Detailing and Design Enhancement
Quality Check and Finishing Touches
Tool Maintenance and Upkeep
Elements and Performance Criteria
Preparing the Coir Fibres
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC1. Select coir fibres based on the product type, ensuring suitable colour, texture, and durability.
PC2. Clean the fibres to remove impurities and unwanted remains and Soften the fibres through
soaking and proper drying to improve flexibility.
PC3. Separate fibres into uniform strands for consistency in product production.
Spinning and Yarn Making
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC4. Spin coir fibres into yarn using a traditional hand spindle or mechanised spinning wheel.
Make sure that even twisting and tension while spinning to create a strong, uniform thread.
PC5. Test yarn strength and elasticity to ensure durability for different product applications. Wind
the finished yarn into spools for quick handling during product construction.
Weaving and Braiding Techniques
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC6. Select the appropriate weaving technique (handloom, power loom, or manual braiding)
based on product design.
PC7. Set up the loom or workstation, ensuring that the fibres and tools are properly connected.
Interlace coir fibres are designed to create even, tight weaves, ensuring strength and
durability.
PC8. Maintain tension across the weave to prevent sagging or loose areas. Examine the weave
periodically to ensure uniformity and address any irregularities.
Product Shaping and Assembly
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:

Remove any loose fibres, uneven knots, or other defects.
PC24. Perform a final quality inspection, ensuring the product meets established standards before
packaging.
Tool Maintenance and Upkeep
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC25. Clean tools such as spindles, looms, cutting shears, and moulds after each use to eliminate
coir dust and fibres.
PC26. Inspect tools regularly for wear and damage, ensuring smooth operation and safety.
PC27. Lubricate rotating parts of spinning wheels and looms to maintain efficiency.
PC28. Sharpen cutting tools and replace worn-out components to ensure accurate cutting and
shaping.
PC29. Store tools in a dry area to prevent rust and damage.
PC30. Maintain records of maintenance activities and replace tools as necessary to avoid
production delays.

HCS/N8526: Entrepreneurship Skills for Coir Fibre Artisan
Description
This NOS covers the key entrepreneurship skills required for a coir fibre artisan to establish, manage, and
grow a coir-based enterprise.
Scope
The scope covers the following :
Opportunity identification and market research
Business planning and goal setting
Product development and design innovation
Branding and Marketing strategy
Financial management
Quality assurance and production efficiency
Legal compliance and ethical practices
Scaling and Sustainability
Elements and Performance Criteria
Opportunity identification and market research
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC1. Identify various applications of coir fibre in products like mats, ropes, handicrafts, and other
eco-friendly items.
PC2. Analyse market demand, consumer preferences, and eco-friendly trends.
PC3. Research competitors and find niche markets for unique coir products.
PC4. Explore collaborative activities with local artisans and cooperatives.
Business planning and goal setting
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC5. Develop a clear vision, mission, and long-term goals for the coir industry.
PC6. Create a business plan, including product selection, pricing, and target customers.
PC7. Create initial investment, working capital, and revenue streams.
PC8. Identify risks and develop strategies to manage uncertainties.
Product development and design innovation
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC9. Explore new designs and patterns for coir products.
PC10. Utilise traditional craftsmanship with modern techniques.
PC11. Create eco-friendly products by using natural dyes and minimising waste.
PC12. Ensure product durability, aesthetics, and functionality are aligned with the market trends.
Branding and Marketing strategy
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
