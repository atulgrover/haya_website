#### TEL/Q2502: Line Assembler - Telecom Products
Brief Job Description
A Line Assembler -Telecom Products is responsible for assembling handsets and telecom equipment at
various stages of the production or assembly line, following predefined processes and industry standards.
The role involves assembling or fitting pre-assembled electronic PCB boards, along with relevant
mechanical and electrical components, onto a metal or plastic chassis within an enclosure. This includes
attaching the top cover and securing all related hardware components, such as screws, using appropriate
tools like electric or pneumatic screwdrivers. The individual is also responsible for following health and
safety standards like ESD-safe procedures and applicable sustainability practices.
Personal Attributes
The individual should have a strong technical understanding of electronic components, PCB boards,
hardware components, and assembly processes, along with keen attention to detail and strict adherence
to ESD safety, sustainability, health & safety standards. The ideal candidate should be **focused, process-
oriented, and capable of maintaining both speed and precision to ensure the efficiency of the assembly
line.
Applicable National Occupational Standards (NOS)
Compulsory NOS:
1. TEL/N2506: Preparing Workspace for Assembly Operations
2. TEL/N2507: Assembly Operations in Production Line
3. TEL/N2510: Assembly of Mobile Phone Accessories
4. TEL/N2508: ESD Safe Procedures and Practices
5. TEL/N9107: Follow sustainability practices in telecom production and assembly line processes
6. DGT/VSQ/N0101: Employability Skills (30 Hours)

#### TEL/N2506: Preparing Workspace  for Assembly Operations
Description
This unit focuses on the preparation of the workplace for assembly operations, incorporating modern
manufacturing technologies and best practices.
Scope
The scope covers the following :
Arrange components/parts and assembly bench
Arrange tools and equipment required for assembly
Prepare workspaces for advanced assembly techniques
#### Elements and Performance Criteria
Arrange components/parts and assembly bench
#### To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
- PC1. retrieve assembled electronic PCB boards, parts of enclosures like metal or plastic chassis,
cover, accessories, hardware components like screws, cables and the other related hardware components from stores as per work instructions and verify against inventory records
- PC2. interpret and confirm understanding of work and assembly instructions
- PC3. arrange the required assembly parts ergonomically for smooth assembly
- PC4. check all the assembly parts, including electronics assembled PCB boards, metal enclosures
and other hardware items for defects, mismatches, or non-conformance and report the identified issues
- PC5. identify and segregate 5G-specific hardware for assembly tasks
- PC6. update inventory records using the ERP system to ensure material availability
Arrange tools and equipment required for assembly
#### To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
- PC7. collect required tools and equipment as per work instructions
- PC8. check the calibration and compliance of measuring instruments and critical tools
- PC9. test tools and equipment for functionality and report malfunctions
- PC10. use IoT-enabled tools to monitor tool compliance and performance
- PC11. set up 3D printing workstations for prototyping and small-batch production
Prepare workspaces for advanced assembly techniques
#### To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
- PC12. set up and calibrate 3D printing equipment for operation
- PC13. ensure 3D-printed parts meet specifications and are defect-free
- PC14. follow best practices for using 3D-printed components in assembly

#### TEL/N2507: Assembly  Operations in Production Line
Description
This unit is about undertaking assembly operations in a telecom handset production line, integrating
automation, smart manufacturing, and advanced quality control measures.
Scope
The scope covers the following :
Assemble telecom devices/products
Use automation and smart manufacturing methods
Use digital tools for inventory and production management
Perform post assembly activities
#### Elements and Performance Criteria
Assemble telecom devices/products
#### To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
- PC1. check the availability of all the parts required for the assembly of Electronic PCB boards,
metal/plastic enclosures, hardware components and all the other components, and tools as per assembly specifications
- PC2. ensure all required tools and equipment are operational and properly calibrated
- PC3. conduct pre-assembly safety compliance checks before starting operations
- PC4. arrange parts and sub-assemblies in the correct sequence for efficient workflow
- PC5. interpret technical diagrams, specifications, and schematics accurately
- PC6. handle components carefully, ensuring proper fitment using appropriate tools
- PC7. assemble components such as RFID tags, Wi-Fi, and Bluetooth modules using correct
procedures, sequences, and tools for precision
- PC8. perform basic quality checks to verify correct assembly before moving to the next stage
Use automation and smart manufacturing methods
#### To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
- PC9. utilize IoT sensors to track all the items used in the assembly process to ensure placement
accuracy and assembly line efficiency
- PC10. operate Robotic Process Automation (RPA) tools for automated pick-and-place assembly
- PC11. use machine vision systems for real-time quality inspection and defect detection
- PC12. use Augmented Reality (AR) tools for interactive training and troubleshooting of assembly
processes
Use digital tools for inventory and production management
#### To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
- PC13. use ERP systems to track materials and schedule production
- PC14. log component usage accurately to maintain stock levels
- PC15. coordinate inventory management with production teams

#### TEL/N2508: ESD Safe Procedures  and Practices
Description
This unit covers ESD safety procedures during assembly operations and the safe handling of ESD-sensitive
components, sub-assemblies, and products. It ensures compliance with best practices to prevent ESD-
related failures by integrating automation, smart manufacturing, and updated compliance measures.
Scope
The scope covers the following :
Comply with ESD processes and procedures
#### Elements and Performance Criteria
Comply with ESD processes and procedures
#### To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
- PC1. follow safe work practices as per the ESD process and protocol
- PC2. ground all components in the work area
- PC3. use ESD tools/equipment (static voltage checker, wrist straps, shoe grounders, air ionizers,
ionized air guns)
- PC4. remove non-essential items and equipment carrying electrostatic generating potential
- PC5. pack/unpack electronic components in compliance with ESD processes
- PC6. handle all semi-finished products after assembly operations using ESD-free trays and conveyor
lines
- PC7. utilize machine vision systems to identify ESD safety violations and ensure compliance
- PC8. implement advanced grounding techniques to mitigate electrostatic risks
- PC9. ensure compliance with ISO 27001 standards for information security in ESD-sensitive
operations
#### Knowledge and Understanding (KU)
The individual on the job needs to know and understand:
- KU1. the necessity of following ESD policies, procedures, and safety guidelines to protect electronic
components and ensure product quality
- KU2. the basics of Electrostatic Discharge (ESD), its sources in the workplace, and its impact on
electronic components and product performance
- KU3. the methods for identifying ESD-sensitive parts, packages, and areas, including the use of
precautionary labels, packaging, and handling instructions
- KU4. classification and properties of ESD-safe materials, appropriate PPEs, and the use of storage/packaging solutions like trays and bags
- KU5. voltage buildup during routine activities (walking, soldering, cleaning), discharge paths, and
associated risks in non-ESD safe areas
