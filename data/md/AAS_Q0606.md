AAS/Q0606: Airline First Officer- Ground Level
Brief Job Description
An Airline First Officer (Ground Level) assists in pre-flight planning, operational coordination, and aircraft
readiness checks to support efficient and safe flight operations. They collaborate with flight crews, ground
staff, and dispatch teams to ensure compliance with safety protocols and regulations. This role serves as a
preparatory stage for full in-flight responsibilities as a licensed First Officer.
Personal Attributes
A first officer attributes would include good communication skills , responsibility. He/She should be meet
the medical and attitude standards set forth by the regulator/organisation
Applicable National Occupational Standards (NOS)

Description
This unit is about the pre-flight tasks to be performed by the First officer
Scope
The scope covers the following :
Perform Pre Flight Check
Prepare flight Plan
Provide Pre flight briefing
Elements and Performance Criteria
Perform pre-flight checks
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC1. peform visual check of the external of the aircraft in accordance with organisation
procedures, aircraft manufacturer procedures and recommendations and regulatory
procedures
PC2. perform cabin inspection and ensure all required documentations are updated and available
in the cabin (according to company policy and regulatory requirements)
PC3. communicate with the engineers on ground and check for any maintenance logs which can
impact the aircraft operations
PC4. ensure if any Minimum Equipment Lists (MEL's) required are in place and appropriate action
for the MEL has been factored in during flight operations
PC5. assist the commander as requested
PC6. carry out pre-flight checks of instruments, engines, fuel and safety systems
Prepare flight plan
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC7. gather and interpret all the required data about weather from MET and ground crew
PC8. gather and interpret all the required data from ATC regarding the route to be followed during
the flight
PC9. calculate the fuel required for the flight
PC10. provide necessary instructions for fuelling of the aircraft in lines with the calculated fuel
PC11. prepare the flight plan
PC12. assist the commander as required
PC13. provide information to ATC as an when required
Provide pre-flight briefing
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC14. provide a preflight briefing to the cabin crew wih details of the flight

AAS/N0636: Air Navigation and Aviation Meteorology
Description
This unit is about applying the principles of air navigation and interpreting meteorological information
essential for flight planning and operation
Scope
The scope covers the following :
Understanding navigation principles
Interpreting navigation charts and instruments
Applying meteorological knowledge to flight planning and safety
Elements and Performance Criteria
Apply Principles of Air Navigation
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC1. Interpret different types of aeronautical charts (e.g., enroute charts, approach charts)
PC2. Identify and use navigation aids such as VOR, NDB, ADF, DME, and GPS
PC3. Calculate headings, bearings, tracks, and estimated time of arrival using flight computer or
manual methods.
PC4. Understand and apply the principles of dead reckoning and radio navigation.
PC5. Determine position using radar, GNSS, and other navigation systems.
PC6. Identify airspace structures, restrictions, and regulations.
Interpret Meteorological Data
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC7. Read and interpret aviation weather reports and forecasts (METAR, TAF, SIGMET, AIRMET).
PC8. Analyze upper wind charts, surface analysis charts, and prognostic charts.
PC9. Understand weather patterns, cloud formations, visibility, turbulence, and icing conditions.
PC10. Correlate meteorological information with route planning and aircraft performance.
PC11. Identify hazardous weather conditions that may affect flight operations.
Integrate Navigation and Meteorology into Flight Planning
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC12. Integrate wind and weather data to adjust flight path and fuel calculation.
PC13. Liaise with ATC and meteorological units for real-time updates
PC14. Update the commander on any weather-related advisories or route deviations.
PC15. Ensure compliance with safety margins based on navigational and meteorological inputs.
Knowledge and Understanding (KU)
The individual on the job needs to know and understand:

AAS/N0614: Perform- Post flight tasks of Pilot
Description
This unit is about the pre-flight checks to be performed by the First officer
Scope
The scope covers the following :
Post flight tasks
Elements and Performance Criteria
Post flight tasks
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC1. communicate with the ground staff to update the nature of flight undertaken
PC2. provide additional information as deemed necessary to theengineering team on any of the
maintenance related events during the flight
PC3. perform a debriefing session with the cabin crew
PC4. provide inputs to the flight dispatch team on any visual weather conditions or air traffic as
noticed during the flight
PC5. update the pilot report for the flight
