SGJ/Q3301: Apiculturist (wild bee) NTFP (Non-Timber Forest Produce)
Brief Job Description
NTFP includes all the Non- Timber Forest Products that are harvested from trees, shrubs and other plants
in the forests. The course on (I) Value Addition & Marketing of Non-Timber Forest Products (NTFPs)-
(Animal Origin): Wild Beekeeping & processing A Beekeeper is responsible for carrying out beekeeping
operations which include preparing for and setting up beehives procuring and introducing bees in hives,
and maintaining the bees and beehives. The individual is also responsible for harvesting, processing and
marketing honey and related produce.
Personal Attributes
Apiculturist focusing on wild bees and Non-Timber Forest Produce (NTFP) include a passion for nature,
environmental stewardship, adaptability, patience, observational skills, collaborative spirit, innovative
thinking, respect for indigenous knowledge, effective communication skills, and resilience.

floral calendar

SGJ/N0205: Introduce Animal based NTFP management
Description
This unit tells about Animal based NTFP management
Scope
The scope covers the following :
For animal-based NTFP management encompasses ecological understanding, sustainable harvesting
techniques, processing methods, and adaptive monitoring to distinguish and achieve sustainable
development goals and understanding of indigenous traditional knowledge and its role in NTFP
management.
The history of beekeeping and its economic importance entails examining the evolution of apiculture
practices, background of wild beekeeping & processing and its role in livelihood generation.
Elements and Performance Criteria
Animal based NTFP management (Common Module)
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC1. introduction to NTFPs- Resources, potential in economy and livelihood generation.
PC2. explain conservation Awareness and approaches.
PC3. discuss indigenous traditional knowledge and its role in NTFP management.
PC4. discuss sustainable and harvesting: Principle and practices.
PC5. discuss intellectual Property Rights and the Patent Regime.
PC6. understanding of NTFP Market dynamics.
PC7. overview of NTFPs: Resources, Economic Potential, and Generation of Livelihoods;
Conservation Awareness and Methods.
PC8. demonstrate native American customary knowledge and its function in managing the NTFP
PC9. perform harvesting and sustainability: Concept and methods.
PC10. illustrate rights to Intellectual Property and the Patent System.
PC11. demonstrate knowledge of the dynamics of the NTFP Market.
History of Beekeeping, Its economic importance (Theory)
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC12. explain type of Bee Products (Honey, Royal jelly, pollen and Bee colony)
PC13. discuss demand of consumer related to bee products.
PC14. explain wild Honey Bee species -Apis cerana Indica,, Apis dorsata, Apis florae and Trigona
species.
PC15. explain life cycle of Honey Bee.
PC16. discuss apis cerana Indica-External Morphology and Internal Morphology, Behaviour and
Ecology
PC17. explain type of Beekeeping Equipments for Wild honey bee-A. cerana.
PC18. Illustrate about Bee Products: pollen, honey, royal jelly, and bee colonies.

#### united and Bee handling and management
Description
This unit tells about Basics of Colony organization/Colony division, Colony united and Bee handling and
management.
Scope
The scope covers the following :
Entails comprehensive training in the principles, methods, and best practices for effectively
managing honeybee colonies, encompassing colony assessment, swarm retrieval, colony splitting,
and colony integration techniques to optimize hive health and productivity.
Equipping individuals with the knowledge and skills necessary to safely and effectively handle bees,
manage hive activities, and implement seasonal management practices to support colony
development, health, and productivity throughout the year.
Elements and Performance Criteria
Colony organization/ Colony capturing/ Colony division, Colony united (Theory and Practical)
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC1. discuss queen, New Queen cell formation, fertilized virgin, Queen mating, drone, etc.
PC2. swarm colony and Natural colony.
PC3. queen less colony control and apply of queen cell, control of worker laying, etc.
PC4. showcase queen, New Queen cell formation, fertilized virgin, Queen mating, drone, etc.
PC5. identify swarm colony and Natural colony
PC6. showcase queen less colony control and apply of queen cell, control of worker laying, etc.

SGJ/N0207: Identify Methods for Apiary & Grafting, Honey/Wax
Extraction, Pollination and other natural requirements
Description
This unit tells about Methods for Apiary & Grafting, Honey/Wax Extraction, Pollination and other natural
requirements
Scope
The scope covers the following :
Enhancing agricultural productivity by promoting pollination and facilitating the propagation of
desirable plant traits, respectively, thereby contributing to sustainable farming practices and
improved crop yields.
Encompasses the sustainable utilization of bee products, fostering economic opportunities for
beekeepers while promoting environmental conservation through the production of natural,
renewable resources.
Safeguarding ecosystems, preserving biodiversity, and ensuring the reproductive success of flora
and fauna, thereby supporting food security, ecosystem resilience, and understand the honey bee in
relation with agriculture and pesticide poisoning, honey production in relation to water.
Elements and Performance Criteria
Apiary & Grafting method
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC1. discuss site selection, bee flora, seasonal flower, water, shade.
PC2. explain bee colony migration.
PC3. explain type and uses of grafting equipment.
PC4. define artificial Queen rearing, etc.
PC5. show importance of site selection, Bee flora, seasonal flower, water, shade.
PC6. perform bee colony migration and management.
PC7. demonstrate type and Uses of grafting equipment.
PC8. ensure understanding about Artificial Queen rearing, etc.
Honey/Wax Extraction
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC9. explain uses of honey, Honey bee wax and its uses.
PC10. explain quality control of honey, extraction-filtration, indirect heating and storage.
PC11. explain wax extraction.
PC12. discuss about bee Enemies/ Pest/Diseases and management.
PC13. ensure practical understanding about the uses of honey, Honey bee wax and its uses, quality
control of honey, extraction-filtration, indirect heating and storage.
PC14. ensure practical understanding about Bee Enemies/Pest/Diseases and management.

Knowledge and Understanding (KU)
The individual on the job needs to know and understand:
KU1. choose a sunny, sheltered location with access to water and diverse foraging sources (flowers,
trees).
KU2. decide on hive type (Langstroth, Top Bar, etc.) based on your experience and goals.
KU3. acquire essential tools like hive tools, smokers, protective suit, frames, and extractors.
KU4. regularly check colony health, brood pattern, queen presence, and potential issues like
swarming or disease.
KU5. raising new queens to replace old ones or create specific breeding stock.
KU6. maintaining optimal queen cell development within hives or queen rearing setups.
KU7. honeybees are crucial pollinators for crops, fruits, and flowers, ensuring plant reproduction
and biodiversity.
KU8. avoid using harmful pesticides near apiaries to protect bee health and foraging success.
KU9. ensure a clean and accessible water source near the hives for bees' hydration and hive
cooling.
Generic Skills (GS)
User/individual on the job needs to know how to:
GS1. knowledge of bee biology and behavior.
GS2. hive inspection and monitoring skills.
GS3. disease and pest identification and control methods.
GS4. safe and gentle bee handling techniques.
GS5. record-keeping and data analysis.
GS6. understanding local regulations and best practices.
GS7. advanced knowledge of hive management techniques (e.g., swarm control, queen rearing).
GS8. understanding queen bee biology and development & basic beekeeping skills and equipment
