LSC/Q2117: Claims Coordinator - Warehouse
Brief Job Description
Claims coordinator - Warehouse is also known as Warehouse Claims Processor. Individuals in this role are
responsible for receiving claims, assessing the goods concerned to ensure the claim is genuine and
estimating the reimbursable amount. They are also required to verify all the documents, send the claims to
the insurance companies and provide detailed reports to the management.
Personal Attributes
This job requires the individual to work well with his/her team and achieve joint goals. The individual must
be able to prioritise and execute tasks within scheduled time limits. The individual should be able to
maintain high concentration levels throughout his/her shift. The individual must perform all functions
without having any bias.
Applicable National Occupational Standards (NOS)
Compulsory NOS:
- 1. LSC/N0324: Prepare for Claims Processing
- 2. LSC/N0325: Inspect Goods and Validate Claims

LSC/N0324: Prepare for Claims Processing
Description
This unit is about preparing for processing claims.
Scope
The scope covers the following :
Analyse the schedule and receive claim forms
Gather the required equipment to perform the inspection
Elements and Performance Criteria
Analyse the schedule and receive claim forms
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC1. Receive the work schedule for the day from the manager.
PC2. Obtain the list of claims, individual claim forms, claims processing, and inspection checklists
for the day from the Warehouse manager.
PC3. Analyse any pending claims and check how they are included in the schedule.
PC4. Review all the claims received and identify priorities (if any) among claims.
PC5. Categorise the claims received according to the incident, crtiticality, priority, location, value,
etc.
PC6. Acquire all the documents submitted along with the claims, such as invoices, transportation
receipts, dispatch lists, etc.
Gather the required equipment and perform the inspection
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC7. Collect and wear all the necessary Personal Protective Equipment (PPE) as required by the
product or the environment.
PC8. Gather the camera and testing equipment required, such as vernier callipers, screw gauges,
densimeters, etc.
PC9. Inspect the camera and testing equipment to ensure that they are in good working condition.
PC10. Perform a trial run and make configuration adjustments to ensure the testing equipment is
ready.
PC11. Check for any existing CCTV footage captured during the incident and collect it from relevant
security personnel/department.
Knowledge and Understanding (KU)
The individual on the job needs to know and understand:
KU1. Knowledge of organisational products and procedures.

LSC/N0325: Inspect Goods and Validate Claims
Description
This OS unit is about inspecting goods and validating the claims.
Scope
The scope covers the following :
Inspect the quarantined goods
Conduct interviews, get expert opinions and segregate false claims
Clean up after inspection
Elements and Performance Criteria
Inspect the quarantined goods
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC1. Check the reason in the claim form and visually inspect the quarantined goods.
PC2. Use the testing equipment to verify the accuracy of the claim or refer it to the quality
assurance team if required.
PC3. Note down the results of the tests and fill out inspection checklists.
PC4. Analyse the claim in detail and perform Root cause analysis as required.
PC5. Form a hypothesis on the reason for the damage and the accuracy of the claim and estimate
the reimbursable amount.
PC6. Take photographs to use as evidence.
Conduct interviews, get expert opinions and segregate false claims
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC7. Interview the claimants, witnesses (if any), clients, suppliers or insurance companies to
check the genuineness and the reason for the claim.
PC8. Validate the documents received against the claims.
PC9. Follow up with relevant stakeholders/ departments if any additional documents, such as
shipping documents or inventory logs, are required.
PC10. Discuss the cause of damage with workers who are experienced in the area or
industry/subject matter experts to validate the hypothesis.
PC11. Check whether the claims were filed within the allowable period and are not outdated.
PC12. Refer to earlier cases and previous similar claims.
PC13. Investigate each claim based on the documentation, interviews conducted and testing
results.
PC14. Segregate genuine claims from false claims based on rationale.
PC15. Escalate false or outdated claims to the manager along with the supporting documentation.
PC16. Update the client/claimant on the claim status throughout the claim’s analysis process.
Clean up after inspection
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:

LSC/N0326: Process claim requests
Description
This unit is about processing claim requests and coordinating with insurance companies to resolve claims.
Scope
The scope covers the following :
Fill out forms and forward them to insurance companies
Coordinate for claims resolution
Elements and Performance Criteria
Fill out forms and forward them to insurance companies
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC1. Ensure that all the documents required (assessment certificate, invoice/bill, claims and
insurance forms, etc.) are in the claims processing checklist.
PC2. Verify the genuineness of the documents received from external sources.
PC3. Confirm that the claim amounts are within the maximum legally permitted range.
PC4. Inform the claimant whether the claim will be processed or not, explain the maximum
permissible claim amounts and resolve any other issues.
PC5. Process the claim forms and forward them to the concerned insurance companies, along with
evidence and recommendations.
Coordinate for claims resolution
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC6. Prepare detailed reports about the inspection results, claim amounts, etc.
PC7. Prepare a report with reasons why certain claims were considered false.
PC8. Update the stakeholders on difficulties or delays due to formalities or interaction with
claimants, government departments, or other agencies.
PC9. Coordinate with the insurance companies with the list of claims still being processed and the
reasons for the delay.
PC10. Resolve any clarifications or issues the insurance company raises.
PC11. Escalate the pending cases to the manager if required.
PC12. Follow up with the insurance companies till the claims are settled.
Knowledge and Understanding (KU)
The individual on the job needs to know and understand:
KU1. Knowledge of organisational products and procedures.
KU2. Procedures for dealing with loss/damage to goods/missing goods from intact
packaging/damaged outer packaging but intact inside material/both outer packaging and
inside material in damaged condition etc.

LSC/N0327: Compliance and Record Keeping
Description

Scope

Record Keeping

insights for management.
PC7. Analyse data from processed claims to identify patterns or recurring issues.
PC8. Suggest improvements to warehouse practices/operations, inventory management, or
training to reduce future claims.
Compliance in Claims Management

PC9. Adhere to regulatory laws and policies governing insurance claims, such as consumer
protection laws, industry standards, and data privacy regulations (e.g., GDPR or General
Data Protection Regulation).
PC10. Comply with internal policies for claims processing, including criteria for claim validity,
required documentation, and timelines for processing.
PC11. Ensure policy enforcement among internal stakeholders to maintain consistency and fairness
in claims handling.
PC12. Prepare reports summarising compliance issues in claims processing and share them with
management for oversight.
PC13. Conduct regular reviews of the claims processed to ensure ongoing compliance with policies
and regulations, adjusting as necessary.

DGT/VSQ/N0101: Employability Skills (30 Hours)
Description
This unit is about employability skills, Constitutional values, becoming a professional in the 21st Century,
digital, financial, and legal literacy, diversity and Inclusion, English and communication skills, customer
service, entrepreneurship, and apprenticeship, getting ready for jobs and career development.
Scope
The scope covers the following :
Introduction to Employability Skills
Constitutional values - Citizenship
Becoming a Professional in the 21st Century
Basic English Skills
Communication Skills
Diversity & Inclusion
Financial and Legal Literacy
Essential Digital Skills
Entrepreneurship
Customer Service
Getting ready for Apprenticeship & Jobs
Elements and Performance Criteria
Introduction to Employability Skills
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC1. understand the significance of employability skills in meeting the job requirements
Constitutional values – Citizenship
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC2. identify constitutional values, civic rights, duties, personal values and ethics and
environmentally sustainable practices
Becoming a Professional in the 21st Century
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC3. explain 21st Century Skills such as Self-Awareness, Behavior Skills, Positive attitude, self-
motivation, problem-solving, creative thinking, time management, social and cultural
awareness, emotional awareness, continuous learning mindset etc.
Basic English Skills
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC4. speak with others using some basic English phrases or sentences
Communication Skills
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC5. follow good manners while communicating with others
PC6. work with others in a team

Assessment Guidelines and Assessment Weightage
Assessment Guidelines
- 1. Criteria for assessment for each Qualification Pack will be created by the Sector Skill Council. Each
Element/ Performance Criteria (PC) will be assigned marks proportional to its importance in NOS. SSC will
also lay down proportion of marks for Theory and Skills Practical for each Element/ PC.
- 2. The assessment for the theory part will be based on knowledge bank of questions validated and
approved by the SSC.
- 3. Individual assessment agencies will create unique question papers for theory part for each candidate at
each examination/training center (as per assessment criteria below).
- 4. Individual assessment agencies will create unique evaluations for skill practical for every student at
each examination/ training center based on these criteria.
- 5. To pass the Qualification Pack, every trainee should score a minimum of 70% for NSQF level 4 & above
job roles and 50% for NSQF level 1 to 3 job roles.
- 6. In case of unsuccessful completion, the trainee may seek reassessment on the Qualification Pack.
