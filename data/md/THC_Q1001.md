THC/Q1001: Foundation Course in Hospitality Operations
Brief Job Description
The Foundation Course in Hospitality Operations introduces learners to the basics of the hospitality
industry, covering food production, food and beverage service, front office, housekeeping, customer
service, and tourism, sanitation and hygiene practices. It emphasizes hands-on training, communication
skills, and teamwork to prepare participants for entry-level roles. This course serves as a steppingstone for
careers in hospitality or further advanced studies.
Personal Attributes
Traits to become a Hospitality Professional Communication Skills Interpersonal skills Critical and creative-
thinking skills Practical skills and knowledge Problem solving skills Digital Literacy

Electives(mandatory to select at least one):
Elective 1: Tourism Products of India (Semester 1)
Tourism Products of India outlines the competencies required to understand and promote India's diverse
tourism offerings, including heritage, culture, adventure, wellness, and eco-tourism. It focuses on
equipping professionals with skills to effectively communicate, curate, and deliver enriching experiences
for domestic and international tourists.
- 1. THC/N4569: Tourism Products of India
Elective 2: Tourism & Hospitality Marketing (Semester 1)
Tourism & Hospitality Marketing defines the competencies required to effectively promote tourism and
hospitality services, focusing on market research, branding, and customer engagement strategies. It
equips professionals with skills to design targeted marketing campaigns, utilize digital platforms, and drive
customer satisfaction and business growth in the tourism and hospitality sectors
- 1. THC/N4570: Tourism & Hospitality Marketing
Elective 3: Destination Management (Semester 2)
This NOS outlines the competencies required for effective destination management, covering key aspects
such as tourism concepts, destination marketing, planning, sustainability, crisis management, and the role
of DMOs and DMCs. It also includes destination monitoring, evaluation, and the development of tourist
circuits to enhance visitor experiences
- 1. THC/N4572: Destination Management (Semester 2)
Elective 4: Cargo Operations and Management (Semester 2)
This NOS focuses on the efficient handling, documentation, and transportation of cargo. It covers
stakeholder coordination, safety protocols, and regulatory compliance to ensure smooth operations. The
role includes overseeing staff, optimizing cargo flow, and ensuring cost-effectiveness
- 1. THC/N4573: Cargo Operations and Management (Semester 2)

THC/N0462: Foundation Course in Food Production - I
Description
The Foundation Course in Food Production - I is to introduce learners to basic culinary skills, kitchen
operations, ingredient usage, and food preparation techniques, emphasizing hygiene, safety, and
foundational knowledge for professional kitchen environments.
Scope
The scope covers the following :
The scope of the Foundation Course in Food Production - I includes building fundamental culinary
skills, understanding kitchen operations, mastering basic cooking techniques, ensuring hygiene and
safety, and preparing learners for entry-level roles in professional kitchens or further specialized
training in culinary arts.
Elements and Performance Criteria
Introduction to Professional Cooking
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC1. Explain the history and origin of Modern cooking
PC2. Describe the attributes required for kitchen personnel

Description
This NOS outlines the competencies required for effective destination management, covering key aspects
such as tourism concepts, destination marketing, planning, sustainability, crisis management, and the role
of DMOs and DMCs. It also includes destination monitoring, evaluation, and the development of tourist
circuits to enhance visitor experiences.

Strategic planning and sustainability.

THC/N4573: Cargo Operations and Management (Semester 2)
Description
This NOS focuses on the efficient handling, documentation, and transportation of cargo. It covers
stakeholder coordination, safety protocols, and regulatory compliance to ensure smooth operations. The
role includes overseeing staff, optimizing cargo flow, and ensuring cost-effectiveness.
Scope
The scope covers the following :
Managing transport, coordination, and regulatory compliance.
Overseeing storage, documentation, and risk management.

Knowledge and Understanding (KU)
The individual on the job needs to know and understand:
KU1. Evolution and the current state of air cargo, including its major players and business models.
KU2. Service and network attributes in air cargo and the benefits it offers to the industry.
KU3. Impact of major international air regulations and conventions on air cargo operations.
KU4. Functions of international organizations such as ICAO and IATA in air cargo management.
KU5. Classification and handling of dangerous goods in air cargo, including packing and
documentation.
KU6. Importance of air cargo rates, charges, and the role of IATA and AAI in formulating them.
KU7. Aircraft payload and volume weight concepts in airfreight operations.
KU8. Role of airlines and cargo handling staff in ensuring smooth cargo operations at airports.
KU9. Challenges faced by the Indian aviation industry and opportunities in the air cargo sector.
KU10. Issues and trends in global and Indian cargo handling operations, focusing on efficiency and
innovation.
KU11. Emerging trends in the air cargo industry, including technological advancements and future
airports.
KU12. Functions of cargo organizations like IATA, ICAO, FIATA, and others in supporting air cargo
operations.
KU13. Global scenario of cargo handling operations and the position of Indian airports in this
context.
KU14. Latest trends in air cargo industry, including advancements in digitalization and
sustainability.
KU15. Importance of air cargo complexes (ACC) in facilitating efficient cargo operations in India.
KU16. Benefits of efficient air cargo services in India, improving trade and logistics capabilities.
KU17. Future trends in air cargo, emphasizing automation, data analytics, and enhanced
infrastructure.
KU18. Various cargo handling regions in airports and the roles of staff within them.
Generic Skills (GS)
User/individual on the job needs to know how to:
GS1. Analyze and manage international air cargo regulations and conventions effectively.
GS2. Develop and implement best practices for handling dangerous goods in air cargo.
GS3. Coordinate air cargo operations across various regions and airports with efficiency.
GS4. Utilize technology and innovation to improve cargo handling and management processes.

Assessment Guidelines
- 1. Criteria for assessment for each Qualification Pack will be created by the Sector Skill Council. Each
Performance Criteria (PC) will be assigned marks proportional to its importance in NOS. SSC will also lay
down the proportion of marks for Theory and Skills Practical for each PC.
- 2. The assessment for the theory part will be based on the knowledge bank of questions created by the
SSC.
- 3. Assessment will be conducted for all compulsory NOS, and where applicable, on the selected
elective/option NOS/set of NOS.
- 4. Individual assessment agencies will create unique question papers for the theory part for each
candidate at each examination/training center (as per assessment criteria below).
- 5. Individual assessment agencies will create unique evaluations for skill practical for every student at
each examination/ training center based on these criteria.
- 6. To pass the Qualification Pack assessment, every trainee should score a minimum of 70% of aggregate
marks to successfully clear the assessment.
