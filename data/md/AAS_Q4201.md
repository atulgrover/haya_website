AAS/Q4201: Airport Safety Officer
Brief Job Description
The Airport Safety Officer is responsible for maintaining a safe and secure environment within the airport
premises, including airside and landside areas. Their duties involve regular inspections, hazard
identification, monitoring compliance with safety procedures, and responding to emergencies such as
accidents, spills, fires, or security breaches. They ensure that airport operations comply with aviation
safety regulations, organizational protocols, and environmental standards. The crew coordinates ground
handling teams, air traffic control, emergency response units, and maintenance personnel to proactively
manage risks and uphold operational safety standards
Personal Attributes
An Airport Safety Officer member should be observant, physically fit, and quick to respond to hazards.
They must have strong communication skills, integrity, and the ability to work well in a team. A calm
attitude under pressure and knowledge of safety procedures are essential for maintaining a secure airport
environment.
Applicable National Occupational Standards (NOS)
Compulsory NOS:

AAS/N4202: Ensure Airside Operations Safety through Inspections and
Hazard Reporting
Description
This unit is about performing regular inspections, identifying and reporting safety hazards, and ensuring
the safe functioning of airside operations.
Scope
The scope covers the following :
Conduct routine inspections of airside infrastructure and operational areas
Identify and report safety hazards like FOD, surface damage, or non-compliant markings
Ensure compliance of personnel, vehicles, and ongoing works with airside safety protocols
Maintain accurate documentation and promptly escalate safety concerns
Participate in safety briefings and support emergency response and hazard mitigation efforts
Elements and Performance Criteria
Conducting Airside Inspections
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC1. conduct regular inspections of airside areas including runways, taxiways, aprons, signage,
lighting, and markings
PC2. identify foreign object debris (FOD), surface damage, and other hazards that could affect
aircraft and vehicular safety
PC3. ensure all safety equipment and markings are present, functional, and compliant with
regulatory standards
PC4. verify that airside construction or maintenance works are cordoned off and compliant with
safety protocols
PC5. ensure safety procedures are followed by all personnel and vehicles operating in airside
areas
Reporting and Hazard Mitigation
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC6. accurately document inspection findings in accordance with organizational protocols
PC7. report hazards or breaches in safety protocols to relevant authorities promptly.
PC8. follow up on reported issues to ensure timely rectification and closure.
PC9. participate in safety meetings and briefings, and share updates related to airside safety.
PC10. support emergency response procedures in case of safety-related incidents.
Knowledge and Understanding (KU)
The individual on the job needs to know and understand:
KU1. airport layout and classification of airside operational areas.

AAS/N4203: Implementation and Maintenance of Safety Management
System
Description
This module provides foundational knowledge and practical skills for implementing, maintaining, and
evaluating a Safety Management System (SMS) within the aviation environment. It covers core
components, regulatory compliance, risk management processes, and continuous improvement practices.
Scope
The scope covers the following :
Understand the purpose, structure, and regulatory framework of SMS in aviation
Assist in implementing SMS components, including safety policies, risk management, and reporting
systems
Participate in monitoring, documenting, and evaluating safety performance
Support internal audits, training, and continuous improvement of SMS processes
Contribute to building and sustaining a positive safety culture within the organization
Elements and Performance Criteria
Understand the Safety Management System (SMS)
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC1. Comprehend the purpose and importance of SMS in aviation.
PC2. Understand the four components of an SMS: Safety Policy, Safety Risk Management, Safety
Assurance, and Safety Promotion.
PC3. Identify the regulatory requirements related to SMS (e.g., ICAO, DGCA).
PC4. Recognize the roles and responsibilities of personnel in maintaining SMS.
PC5. Understand the integration of SMS with organizational functions.
Implement and operate the SMS
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC6. Assist in developing safety policies and objectives.
PC7. Participate in hazard identification and risk assessment activities.
PC8. Help establish safety reporting systems and encourage safety reporting culture.
PC9. Monitor safety performance indicators.
PC10. Maintain documentation and records related to SMS implementation.
Maintain and Improve SMS
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC11. Support internal safety audits and inspections.
PC12. Identify areas for improvement in SMS processes.
PC13. Participate in safety training and communication initiatives.
PC14. Assist in updating SMS procedures in response to operational changes or audit findings.

AAS/N4204: Manage Risks, and Conduct Compliance Audits
Description
This module equips individuals with the knowledge and skills required to identify, evaluate, and manage
operational and safety risks in aviation settings, along with planning and conducting compliance audits in
alignment with industry regulations and organizational standards.
Scope
The scope covers the following :
Identify and assess various types of risks in aviation operations
Implement and monitor risk control and mitigation strategies
Plan, prepare, and conduct aviation compliance audits as per regulations and standards
Report audit findings and support corrective and preventive actions (CAPA)
Promote continuous improvement and organizational learning through audit processes
Elements and Performance Criteria
Identify and Manage Risks
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC1. Understand types of risks in aviation operations (safety, operational, financial, legal).
PC2. Identify potential hazards using systematic methods.
PC3. Conduct risk assessments and evaluate consequences and likelihood.
PC4. Implement appropriate risk control measures.
PC5. Monitor and review the effectiveness of risk mitigation actions.
PC6. Document and report risk-related findings in compliance with organizational procedures.
Plan and Conduct Compliance Audits
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC7. Understand the objectives and scope of aviation compliance audits.
PC8. Prepare audit checklists and plans based on relevant regulations and internal policies.
PC9. Conduct compliance audits in an organized and impartial manner.
PC10. Collect and verify audit evidence through observations, interviews, and document review.
PC11. Identify non-conformities and assess their impact on operations.
PC12. Prepare audit reports with findings, root causes, and recommendations.
Ensuring Follow-up and Continuous Improvement
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC13. Support the development of corrective and preventive action plans (CAPA).
PC14. Monitor implementation of corrective actions and validate closure.
PC15. Contribute to organizational learning and continuous improvement through audit feedback.
Knowledge and Understanding (KU)
