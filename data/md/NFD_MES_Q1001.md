NFD/MES/Q1001: Theatrical Makeup Artist
Brief Job Description
A Theatrical Makeup Artist applies a variety of makeup techniques to create realistic or stylized looks for
theatrical productions. They collaborate with directors, costume designers, and actors to achieve the
desired visual impact, ensuring makeup complements the overall character portrayal and stage
presentation.
Personal Attributes
They collaborate with directors, costume designers, and actors to achieve the desired visual impact,
ensuring makeup complements the overall character portrayal and stage presentation.
Applicable National Occupational Standards (NOS)
Compulsory NOS:
- 1. NFD/MES/N0304: Follow Workplace Health & Safety Protocols
- 2. DGT/VSQ/N0102: Employability Skills (60 Hours)
- 3. NFD/MES/N1001: Prepare for theatrical makeup applications.
- 4. NFD/MES/N1002: Apply advanced makeup techniques.
- 5. NFD/MES/N1003: Collaborate with actors and the production team
Electives(mandatory to select at least one):
Elective 1: Makeup for Period Productions
This OS is about makeup for Period Productions
- 1. NFD/MES/N1004: Makeup for Period Productions

This OS is about Hair Styling for Theatrical Productions
- 1. NFD/MES/N1005: Hair styling for Theatrical Productions

NFD/MES/N0304: Follow Workplace Health & Safety Protocols
Description
This NOS is designed to follow workplace health & safety protocols
Scope
The scope covers the following :
Follow the health, safety and security risks prevalent in the workplace
Elements and Performance Criteria
Follow the health, safety and security risks prevalent in the workplace
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC1. Adhere to established safety protocols, promptly reporting any identified hazards to relevant
authorities and consistently avoiding unsafe practices.
PC2. Advocate for and maintain a healthy posture during work activities, incorporating regular
breaks to mitigate the risk of work-related injuries.
PC3. Prioritize proper eye care by taking regular breaks to prevent eye strain while working on
screens, ensuring sustained productivity and well-being.
PC4. Comply with government health and safety norms and policies, including familiarity with
emergency procedures such as evacuation protocols for illness, accidents, or fires.
PC5. Engage in relevant health and safety training programs to remain updated on workplace best
practices, ensuring a proactive approach to safety measures.
PC6. Implement effective stress management techniques to sustain personal well-being and
optimize productivity amidst workplace demands.
PC7. Recognize and respond to security signals, such as fire alarms, and familiarize oneself with
designated safety locations like staircases, fire warden stations, and medical rooms.
PC8. Execute emergency procedures swiftly in the event of an accident or fire, ensuring personal
and collective safety.
PC9. Take proactive measures to promptly address safety concerns as they arise, contributing to a
secure work environment for oneself and others.
PC10. Uphold personal health and safety, as well as that of colleagues, through consistent
adherence to precautionary measures and safety guidelines.
PC11. Demonstrate awareness of and responsiveness to safety-related protocols and instructions,
maintaining a vigilant approach to workplace safety.
PC12. Contribute to a culture of safety by actively promoting and adhering to established safety
practices, fostering a secure and conducive work environment.

NFD/MES/N1004: Makeup for Period Productions
Description
This OS is about how to apply makeup for Period Productions.
Scope
The scope covers the following :
Researching historical makeup styles and techniques
Selecting and applying period-appropriate makeup products
Creating realistic aging effects
Adapting makeup designs for different social classes
Elements and Performance Criteria
Researching historical makeup styles and techniques
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC1. Identify key resources for researching historical makeup trends, such as paintings, fashion
plates, and historical texts.
PC2. Distinguish between makeup styles for different social classes, genders, and age groups
within a specific historical period.
PC3. Utilize research findings to identify the most appropriate makeup techniques for the
characters in the production.
PC4. Critically assess the historical accuracy and theatrical effectiveness of potential makeup
design choices for the period.
PC5. Develop a comprehensive research document or mood board that visually represents the
target historical makeup styles and informs design decisions.
Selecting and applying period-appropriate makeup products
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC6. Identify the types of makeup products commonly used during different historical periods
(e.g., natural pigments, kohl, greasepaint).
PC7. Analyze the ingredients and functionalities of period-appropriate makeup products to select
the safest and most effective options for the production.
PC8. Apply period-appropriate makeup techniques using knowledge of historical application
methods and tools.
PC9. Assess the compatibility of modern and period-specific makeup products to ensure desired
effects and actor safety.
PC10. Develop customized makeup blends using modern products to accurately replicate the look
and texture of historical makeup.
Creating realistic aging effects
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC11. Identify the key anatomical and facial changes associated with aging.

PC14. Assess the realism and consistency of the aging effects from various viewing angles and

NFD/MES/N1006: Create and Maintain Character Prosthetics
Description
This OS is about Creating and Maintaining Character Prosthetics
Scope
The scope covers the following :
Selecting and sculpting prosthetic materials
Applying and adhering prosthetics to actors
Blending prosthetics seamlessly with actors' skin
Maintaining and repairing prosthetics throughout the production
Elements and Performance Criteria
Selecting and sculpting prosthetic materials
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC1. Identify different types of prosthetic materials (e.g., latex, silicone, foam latex) and their
properties.
PC2. Analyze the desired prosthetic effect and select the most suitable material based on factors
like flexibility, durability, and skin-safe properties.
PC3. Sculpt prosthetic pieces using appropriate tools and techniques to achieve the desired shape
and detail for the character.
PC4. Evaluate the sculpted prosthetic for accuracy, symmetry, and adherence to the makeup
design concept.
PC5. Apply mold-making techniques to create reusable molds for prosthetics that require multiple
applications.
Applying and adhering prosthetics to actors
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC6. Recall the importance of proper skin preparation before prosthetic application to ensure
optimal adhesion.
PC7. Analyze the actor's facial features and apply prosthetics with appropriate placement and
blending techniques.
PC8. Select and utilize different adhesives (spirit gum, skin adhesives) based on the prosthetic
material and desired wear time.
PC9. Assess the security and comfort of the applied prosthetic, making adjustments as necessary.
PC10. Develop creative solutions for adhering prosthetics to actors with unique facial features or
allergies.
Blending prosthetics seamlessly with actors' skin
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC11. Recognize the importance of color matching between the prosthetic and the actor's skin
tone.
