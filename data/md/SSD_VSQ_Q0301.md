SSD/VSQ/Q0301: Basic Rigger
Brief Job Description
Basic Rigger is responsible for carrying rigging work, aligning, and anchoring the machinery, using correct
rigging technique, attaching loads, controlling the movement of heavy equipment, and ensuring that the
rigging is safe for use.
Personal Attributes
He should be physically & mentally fit and should be able to ensure compliances required as per safety
and health requirements. He should also be able to provide correct & timely advice on the safety and
health issues of construction workers to the management.

SSD/VSQ/N0301: Introduction to Basic Rigging
Description
The NOS covers the knowledge & skills required by the professional to familiarize with terms & definitions,
concepts used in rigging, material & hardware, slings, working load limit and type of slings and their
advantages.
Scope
The scope covers the following :
Familiarization with the terms & definitions used in rigging applications and inspections and
principles behind basic rigging theory.
Different pieces of rigging hardware & applications for various pieces of rigging equipment, sling
types and proper use conditions.
Advantages of different pieces of rigging hardware in unique use cases.
Working load limit (WLL) load weight calculation, center of gravity for diverse types of slings.
Safety standards and regulations governing rigging activities.
Elements and Performance Criteria
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC1. Define key terminologies and basic concepts in rigging. (e.g., hitches, slings, loads).
PC2. Explain the fundamental principles of rigging, including weight distribution, load angles, and
center of gravity.
PC3. Describe the roles and responsibilities of a Basic Rigger.
PC4. Identify different types of rigging tools and their applications.
PC5. Calculate the weight and center of gravity of a load using basic formulas and estimations.
PC6. Identify different types of lifting equipment and their appropriate use for various load types.
PC7. Explain the impact of load angles on lifting and rigging safety.
PC8. Identify the different types of slings (wire rope, chain, synthetic) and their properties.
PC9. Select appropriate slings and rigging hardware for specified lifting tasks.
PC10. Demonstrate the correct use of basic hitch configurations for securing loads.
PC11. Outline the safety standards and regulations applicable to basic rigging operations.
PC12. Identify potential hazards in rigging operations and the appropriate measures to mitigate
them.
PC13. Apply safety protocols during rigging tasks, including the use of personal protective
equipment (PPE).
Knowledge and Understanding (KU)
The individual on the job needs to know and understand:
KU1. Rigging terminologies and definitions, including types of slings, hitches, and hardware.

SSD/VSQ/N0302: Safety Standards and Regulations
Description
The NOS describes the knowledge & skills required by the professional to understand the safety
requirements and regulations that govern rigging and lifting operations. It aims to ensure that the Basic
Riggers are equipped with the knowledge and skills required to perform rigging tasks in compliance with
legal and safety standards. The unit covers various safety regulations, hazard identification, risk
management, and compliance procedures specific to rigging and lifting activities.
Scope
The scope covers the following :
Rigging activities performed by basic Riggers, focusing on adherence to safety standards and
regulatory guidelines.
Understanding industry-specific safety protocols, implementing hazard prevention strategies, and
maintaining a safe work environment.
Use of personal protective equipment (PPE) and ensuring compliance with occupational health and
safety (OHS) regulations.
Elements and Performance Criteria
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC1. Identify and describe the key safety standards and regulations applicable to rigging
operations, including OSHA, ANSI, and other relevant standards.
PC2. Explain the legal requirements for workplace safety and the responsibilities of a Basic Rigger
in maintaining safety compliance.
PC3. Demonstrate knowledge of the company’s safety policies and procedures related to rigging
activities.
PC4. Identify potential hazards in rigging and lifting operations, such as load instability, equipment
failure, and environmental factors.
PC5. Evaluate risks associated with identified hazards and implement strategies to control or
mitigate them.
PC6. Develop and implement a risk management plan that addresses common hazards in rigging
environments.
PC7. Ensure proper use of personal protective equipment (PPE) and inspect PPE for compliance
with safety standards before use.
PC8. Adhere to safety protocols and procedures when performing rigging tasks, including load
handling, lifting, and securing activities.
PC9. Report safety issues or non-compliance with safety regulations to supervisors or safety
officers.
PC10. Maintain accurate records of safety inspections, equipment inspections, and incident reports
as per regulatory requirements.
PC11. Complete safety checklists, hazard assessments, and compliance reports in accordance with
industry standards.

SSD/VSQ/N0303: Rigging Inspections
Description
The NOS describes the knowledge & skills required by the professional to understand the essential
competencies required to conduct thorough inspections of rigging equipment, components, and
configurations to ensure safe and compliant lifting operations. Identifying, evaluation, and documentation
of the condition of various rigging tools and materials to prevent accidents and maintain operational
integrity. This unit encompasses pre-use, periodic, and post-use inspections, as well as the application of
safety standards for different types of rigging equipment.
Scope
The scope covers the following :
Inspection of rigging equipments used in lifting and material handling operations, including slings,
chains, wire ropes, hooks, and other hardware.
Identification of defects, assessment of equipment wear and tear, understanding inspection
frequency requirements, and documenting inspection results.
Compliance with industry standards, such as OSHA and ANSI.
Elements and Performance Criteria
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC1. Identify potential hazards in rigging and lifting operations, such as load instability, equipment
failure, and environmental factors.
PC2. Identify the operational limits and specifications of different rigging tools & devices and
develop risk management plan that addresses common hazards in rigging environments.
PC3. Identify and describe the key safety standards and regulations applicable to rigging
operations, including OSHA, ANSI, and other relevant standards.
PC4. Conduct comprehensive risks & hazards identification for rigging tasks and take safety
measures.
PC5. Perform precise load calculations considering factors such as weight, center of gravity, and
balance.
PC6. Identify and document any signs of wear, damage, or defects in rigging gear.
PC7. Coordinate with team members to ensure synchronized and safe rigging operations.
PC8. Develop and practice emergency response plans specific to rigging operations.
PC9. Adhere to safety protocols and procedures when performing rigging tasks, including load
handling, lifting, and securing activities.
PC10. Ensure proper use of personal protective equipment (PPE) and inspect PPE for compliance
with safety standards before use.
PC11. Raise all the hooks to upper limit switch. Place all controls in the off position. Visually check
for dangerous conditions.
PC12. Ensure measures that no personnel stand under suspended loads.
Knowledge and Understanding (KU)

Description
The NOS describes the knowledge & skills required by the professional about "Rigging Equipment and
Sling", various rigging equipment and sling types used in lifting and material handling operations. This unit
equips Basic Riggers with the knowledge and skills required to select, use, and maintain different types of

properties, applications, and limitations of wire rope slings, chain slings, synthetic slings, and metal mesh
slings, as well as rigging hardware such as hooks, shackles, and clips, use of different types of knots, such
as bowline, clove hitch, and square knot, with various rope types, including manila, fiber, and synthetic

Scope

Selection and safe use of rigging equipment and sling types in lifting operations.

environmental factors.

turnbuckles, to enhance load stability and control.

for effective load handling.
PC7. Select and tie the appropriate knot, such as bowline, clove hitch, square knot, or double half

rigging configurations, and ensure load stability throughout lifting operations with understanding of load

accidents during rigging and lifting activities.
Scope
The scope covers the following :
Application of rigging techniques and configurations used in lifting and material handling operations
to ensure load stability.
Selection of appropriate rigging methods based on load characteristics, employing different types of
hitches, and managing load angles to maintain balance and control.
Identification of potential hazards related to load stability and implementing risk management
strategies to prevent tipping, slipping, or other stability-related issues during lifting operations.
Elements and Performance Criteria

stability during lifting operations.
PC3. Identify different rigging techniques such as vertical, choker, and basket hitches, and
describe their impact on load handling and stability.
PC4. Select and apply the appropriate hitch configurations (vertical, choker, basket) based on load
characteristics and lifting requirements.
PC5. Calculate and manage sling angles to minimize stress and ensure balanced load lifting.
PC6. Use multiple slings, spreader beams, or lifting beams to distribute the load evenly and
prevent tipping or swinging.
PC7. Assess load stability by determining the center of gravity and applying rigging techniques to
keep the load level during lifting.
PC8. Monitor load movement and make necessary adjustments to sling tension and configurations
to maintain load control.
PC9. Implement safety measures, such as tag lines or secondary rigging, to control load
movement and prevent unwanted load shifts.
PC10. Identify potential hazards associated with load stability, such as imbalanced loads, swinging,
and load tipping, and assess their impact on lifting operations.
PC11. Apply risk management strategies to mitigate identified hazards, including adjusting rigging
configurations and using additional rigging equipment.

Description
The NOS describes the knowledge & skills required by the professional to respond effectively to emergency
situations that may arise during rigging and lifting operations. This NOS focuses on developing contingency

protect personnel, equipment, and the work environment. It ensures that riggers are prepared to handle
unexpected events such as equipment failure, load instability, or environmental hazards.
Scope

strategies, and execution of emergency response plans.

with safety personnel and first responders.

ensure preparedness and safety.

emergency response.
