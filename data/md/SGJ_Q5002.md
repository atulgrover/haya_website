SGJ/Q5002: Waste Optimisation Professional
Brief Job Description
India is facing major environmental challenges associated with waste generation and inadequate waste
collection, transport, treatment and disposal. Current systems in India cannot cope with the volumes of
waste generated by an increasing urban population, and impact on the environment and public health. The
course will facilitate management of various categories of waste (e.g., solid waste, plastic, biomedical
waste, e-waste, C&D waste etc.) including source segregation, collection, disposal, processing and
recycling.
Personal Attributes
This job requires the individual to be diligent, organised, punctual, attentive and accountable to complete
the work safely and timely.
Applicable National Occupational Standards (NOS)
Compulsory NOS:
- 1. SGJ/N4072: Introduction to Solid Waste
- 2. SGJ/N4073: Identify characteristic, collection , transfer and transport and Separation and
processing of solid waste
- 3. SGJ/N4074: Perform Composting and recycling, waste to energy and sanitary landfilling
- 4. SGJ/N4075: Introduce climate change in context to waste.
- 5. SGJ/N4076: Plan and develop strategy for waste management focusing circular economy and
plastic waste management
- 6. SGJ/N4077: Perform E-Waste Management
- 7. SGJ/N4078: Perform Construction and Demolition activities within waste management frameworks,
alongside addressing Bio-Medical Waste Management concerns and explores the fundamental
principles of Geographic Information Systems applied to waste management practices.
- 8. DGT/VSQ/N0103: Employability Skills (90 Hours)

SGJ/N4072: Introduction to Solid Waste
Description
This unit tells about Introduction to Solid Waste
Scope
The scope covers the following :
Learn about solid waste Management and understand problems and challenges associated with the
generation of solid waste.
Elements and Performance Criteria
Introduction to Solid Waste
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC1. discuss about understanding of challenges associated with generation of waste.
PC2. explain health and environmental impacts of poor waste management.
PC3. discuss elements of a Solid Waste Management System.
PC4. explain integrated Solid Waste Management.
PC5. show different types of waste collection through pictures or videos.
PC6. showcase challenges associated with generation of waste.
PC7. showcase Health and environmental impacts of poor waste management.
PC8. show how to implement integrated solid waste management at site.
Knowledge and Understanding (KU)
The individual on the job needs to know and understand:
KU1. understanding the composition and types of waste generated (organic, plastic, paper, metal,
glass, etc.) is essential for designing effective management strategies.
KU2. minimizing waste generation at the source is the most sustainable approach. This involves
promoting practices like reusable products, packaging reduction, and composting food scraps.
KU3. efficient collection systems ensure timely waste removal, preventing accumulation and
environmental hazards. Different methods like curbside collection, drop-off centers, and
community composting can be employed.
KU4. different technologies are used to process waste, depending on its type and composition.
These include recycling, composting, incineration, and landfilling.
KU5. landfills, though necessary for non-recyclable waste, pose environmental risks if not managed
properly. Practices like leachate collection, methane capture, and proper closure are crucial.
KU6. improper SWM can lead to air and water pollution, soil contamination, and the spread of
diseases. Understanding these impacts is vital for promoting responsible waste management.
KU7. SWM has social and economic implications, including job creation, resource recovery, and
public health costs. Effective management requires considering these aspects.

SGJ/N4073: Identify characteristic, collection , transfer and transport and
Separation and processing of solid waste
Description
This unit tells about Identify characteristic, collection , transfer and transport and Separation and
processing of solid waste The course will facilitate management of various categories of waste (e.g., solid
waste, plastic, biomedical waste, e-waste, C&D waste etc.) including source segregation, collection,
disposal, processing and recycling.
Scope
The scope covers the following :
understand the sources type and composition of solid waste and classification of solid waste such as
physical, biological, economic and ecological.
Learn about how to design a solid waste collection system including transfer and transport and
Separation and processing of solid waste.
Understand concept of a material recovery facility (MRF) and importance of health and hygiene
Elements and Performance Criteria
Characteristics of Solid Waste
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC1. depict the sources, types and composition of solid waste.
PC2. identify quantities of solid waste.
PC3. demonstrate properties of solid waste.
PC4. perform data collection on waste.
PC5. understand how different materials, product design, and consumer choices contribute to
waste generation.
PC6. identify strategies for source reduction like minimizing packaging, choosing durable
products, and composting food scraps.
Collection, transfer and transport
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC7. show how to develop a solid waste collection system.
PC8. show types of solid waste storage systems and collection vehicles.
PC9. show design of solid waste collection systems.
PC10. illustrate basic concepts, types and design of solid waste transfer stations.
PC11. perform and understand different collection methods (curbside, centralized, etc.) and select
the most effective one for your needs.
PC12. show how to consider waste composition and geography when designing routes and
optimizing resource allocation.
PC13. show how to utilize data analysis to track collection performance and identify areas for
improvement.

Separation and processing
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC14. discuss basic concepts of separation and processing of solid waste.
PC15. discuss basic concepts of operation of a Material Recovery Facility.
PC16. explain design of a Material Recovery Facility.
PC17. perform the different stages of waste processing at an MRF, such as sorting, separation, and
baling.
PC18. demonstrate the various technologies employed in MRFs, such as conveyor belts, trommels,
and optical sorters.
PC19. identify the challenges and opportunities associated with MRF operation, such as
contamination and market fluctuations.
Knowledge and Understanding (KU)
The individual on the job needs to know and understand:
KU1. landfills are used for disposing of non-recyclable and non-treatable waste. Minimizing
reliance on landfills is essential due to environmental concerns.
KU2. technologies like gasification and plasma gasification convert waste into usable energy
sources.
KU3. this approach aims to minimize waste generation by promoting product design for reuse,
repair, and recycling, creating a closed-loop system.
KU4. solid waste comprises diverse materials like organic matter, paper, plastics, glass, metals,
textiles, and electronic waste. Understanding the proportions of these components is crucial
for designing effective management strategies.
KU5. different waste materials possess varying properties like density, moisture content,
flammability, and reactivity. These properties influence storage, transportation, processing,
and disposal methods.
KU6. organic waste degrades naturally, while others like plastics persist for extended periods.
Biodegradability is essential for composting and other biological treatment processes.
KU7. separating waste at the source (homes, offices, etc.) into categories like recyclables,
compostables, and general waste streamlines processing and enhances recycling efficiency.
KU8. depending on waste type, volume, and local infrastructure, various collection methods are
employed, including curbside pickup, drop-off centers, and communal bins.
KU9. these facilities consolidate waste from smaller collection vehicles into larger ones for
efficient long-distance transportation to processing or disposal sites.
KU10. techniques like screening, air separation, and magnetic separation segregate waste
components based on size, density, and material properties.
KU11. composting biodegrades organic waste into nutrient-rich compost for soil amendment.
KU12. processes like anaerobic digestion convert organic waste into biogas, a renewable energy
source.
KU13. incineration burns waste at high temperatures to reduce volume and generate energy.
However, air pollution control is crucial.

SGJ/N4074: Perform Composting and recycling, waste to energy and
sanitary landfilling
Description
This unit tells about Perform Composting and recycling, waste to energy and sanitary landfilling
Scope
The scope covers the following :
Understand the concept of solid waste composting and explain the technologies associated with
solid waste composting.
Learn basic concept and technologies related to energy recovery from solid waste and
understanding refused drive fuel (RDF).
Understand concept of sanitary landfills and learn different type of landfill and landfilling method
and landfill leachate control and treatment.
Elements and Performance Criteria
Composting and recycling
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC1. show types of solid waste recyclable materials.
PC2. prepare design and implementation of a recycling programme.
PC3. demonstrate basic concepts of solid waste composting.
PC4. show composting technologies and approaches.
PC5. show how to develop a solid waste composting programme.
PC6. perform Bio-gasification of solid waste.
PC7. show how to master the different types of recyclable materials like paper, metal, glass,
plastic, and organics.
PC8. show how to learn how to implement an effective recycling program in your home,
community, or organization.
PC9. ensure understanding the basics principles of composting, the breakdown of organic waste
into nutrient-rich soil amendment.
PC10. show how to discover various composting methods like bin composting, vermicomposting,
and windrow composting.
PC11. show how to equip yourself with the knowledge to set up and maintain a successful
composting program.
PC12. show how to learn about bio-gasification, a technology that converts organic waste into
biogas for energy production.
Waste to Energy
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC13. explain basic concepts and types of solid waste incineration.
PC14. discuss recovering energy from solid waste.

Knowledge and Understanding (KU)
The individual on the job needs to know and understand:
KU1. composting involves controlled decomposition of organic waste (food scraps, yard
trimmings) by microorganisms under specific conditions (moisture, oxygen, temperature). It
creates nutrient-rich compost, improving soil health and fertility.
KU2. learn about the browns (carbon-rich) and greens (nitrogen rich) materials needed for
balanced composting.
KU3. understand different composting methods (bin, pile, vermicomposting) and choose the best
for your needs. Monitor moisture, temperature, and aeration for optimal decomposition.
KU4. recycling involves collecting and processing specific materials (paper, plastic, glass, metal)
into new products. It conserves resources, reduces energy use, and minimizes landfill waste.
KU5. learn about local recycling guidelines and what materials are accepted in your area. Rinse
and sort recyclables properly to avoid contamination.
KU6. understand different recycling symbols and their meanings.
KU7. WtE facilities burn non-recyclable waste to generate electricity or heat. It reduces landfill
waste and produces energy, but can have air quality concerns.

KU8. learn about the types of waste accepted at WtE facilities in your area. Be aware of potential

KU10. landfills should be a last resort for waste disposal. Be aware of potential environmental

Generic Skills (GS)
