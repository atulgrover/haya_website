NIE/SSC/Q1701: Scientific Assistant in Data Science for Life Sciences
Brief Job Description
This Short-term Certificate Course is targeted at creating qualified professionals in the field of
Bioinformatics, which will help in the employment or Entrepreneurship development of the qualifier.The
purpose of this qualification is to train the students of Life Sciences with the basics of Bioinformatics to skill
them and increase their employability in the field of Life Sciences and allied disciplines.
Personal Attributes
The required personal attributes span a broad spectrum essential for success in bioinformatics and data
security law. An analytical mindset and adept problem-solving skills are fundamental for understanding
algorithms and utilizing bioinformatics tools effectively. Proficiency in Python programming, coupled with
adaptability, enables individuals to address diverse biological challenges with versatility. Attention to
detail, methodical analysis, and a commitment to compliance standards are vital for comprehending
biomolecular structures and ensuring data security law adherence.
Applicable National Occupational Standards (NOS)
Compulsory NOS:
- 1. NIE/SSC/N1801: Algorithmic perspective of Bioinformatics and introduction to Bioinformatics tools.
- 2. NIE/SSC/N1802: Solving biological problems using Python programming
- 3. NIE/SSC/N1803: Analysis and understanding of biomolecular structures using Bioinformatics tools
- 4. NIE/SSC/N1804: Data security law
- 5. NIE/SSC/N1810: Implementation of project on bioinformatics
- 6. DGT/VSQ/N0102: Employability Skills (60 Hours)

NIE/SSC/N1801: Algorithmic perspective of Bioinformatics and
introduction to Bioinformatics tools.
Description
This NOS delves into bioinformatics from algorithmic and tool-based perspectives. It covers basic biology,
molecular biology concepts, and DNA technology. Students learn about biological data formats, databases,
and sequence alignment techniques. The course also explores gene prediction, molecular phylogeny,
pattern matching, and pathways/systems biology analysis.
Scope
The scope covers the following :
Algorithmic perspectives and introduction to bioinformatics tools.
Basics of molecular biology, recombinant DNA technology, and biological data file formats.
Introduction to biological databases, sequence alignment techniques, gene prediction methods, and
molecular phylogeny.
Exploring pattern matching, pathways/systems biology analysis, and simulation of cellular activities.
Elements and Performance Criteria
Foundational Concepts
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC1. Demonstrate the ability to apply algorithmic thinking in solving bioinformatics problems, such
as sequence alignment or motif discovery.
PC2. Utilize bioinformatics tools effectively to analyze biological data, including sequence data and
structural information.
PC3. Explain the principles of molecular biology and DNA technology and their relevance to
bioinformatics applications, such as gene prediction or protein structure analysis.
Data Handling and Analysis
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC4. Successfully navigate and work with different biological data file formats (GenBank, FASTA,
etc.) using appropriate conversion tools.
PC5. Effectively use databases like NCBI or EMBL to retrieve and analyze biological data for specific
research questions or projects.
Advanced Applications
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC6. Apply pattern matching techniques to identify regulatory sequences or motifs within DNA
sequences, demonstrating an understanding of motif discovery algorithms.
PC7. Analyze biological pathways and systems, including metabolic networks, and conduct control
analysis or simulation studies to understand cellular activities.
PC8. Use simulation methods to model and analyze biological processes, such as protein-protein
interactions or molecular dynamics, and interpret the results in the context of biological
function.

Knowledge and Understanding (KU)
The individual on the job needs to know and understand:
KU1. • Knowledge of Bioinformatics Tools and Algorithms:
• Understanding of bioinformatics tools for sequence analysis, motif discovery, and phylogenetic
analysis.
• Familiarity with algorithms such as sequence alignment (Needleman-Wunsch, Smith Waterman),
gene prediction methods, and phylogenetic tree construction.
• Ability to apply heuristic methods like FASTA and BLAST for sequence similarity searches and
motif identification.
KU2. • Knowledge of Biological Data Handling:
• Understanding of different file formats used in bioinformatics (GenBank, FASTA, etc.) and their
conversion tools.
• Knowledge of biological databases like NCBI, EMBL, and their classification systems.
• Ability to handle and interpret biological data from databases and files for analysis and research
purposes.
KU3. • Understanding of Molecular Biology Concepts:
• Knowledge of basic molecular biology principles, including DNA technology, gene prediction, and
protein structure.
• Understanding of molecular phylogeny, evolutionary models, and methods for constructing
phylogenetic trees.
• Familiarity with pathways and systems biology, including metabolic network analysis and
simulation techniques.
Generic Skills (GS)
User/individual on the job needs to know how to:
GS1. • Analytical Thinking:
• Ability to analyze biological data using bioinformatics tools and algorithms.
• Critical thinking skills in interpreting sequence alignments, gene predictions, and phylogenetic
trees.
• Capability to identify patterns, motifs, and regulatory sequences in DNA sequences.
GS2. • Research Skills:
• Proficiency in navigating and utilizing biological databases for research purposes.
• Skill in conducting literature reviews and staying updated with advancements in bioinformatics.
• Ability to apply computational methods to solve biological problems and contribute to scientific
research.
GS3. • Communication and Presentation:
• Effective communication of bioinformatics analyses and findings to peers and stakeholders.
• Skill in presenting complex biological data, algorithms, and models in a clear and concise
manner.
• Capability to collaborate with interdisciplinary teams and convey bioinformatics concepts to
diverse audiences.

NIE/SSC/N1802: Solving biological problems using Python programming
Description
The NOS delves into essential programming and computer fundamentals crucial for tackling biological
challenges using Python. Students learn about computer systems, data representations, and IEEE
standards, alongside scripting in Linux/Unix for bioinformatics tasks. Python programming basics cover
input/output, data types, operators, and control structures, while emphasizing modular programming and
functional decomposition. The course also introduces object-oriented programming concepts, aggregate
data types, and fundamental data structures in Python, vital for efficient data handling and algorithmic
design in bioinformatics.
Scope
The scope covers the following :
Basic Computer Fundamentals: Understanding computer systems, data representation, and CPU
organization.
Scripting and Linux Commands: Learning basic scripting in Linux/Unix for bioinformatics tasks.
Programming in Python: Covering Python basics, file handling, and modular programming concepts.
Object-Oriented Programming (OOP) and Data Structures: Exploring OOP principles and essential
data structures in Python for bioinformatics applications.
Elements and Performance Criteria
Computer Fundamentals
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC1. Demonstrate understanding of number systems, including binary, decimal, and hexadecimal
conversions.
PC2. Explain the functionality of CPU components such as ALU, registers, and memory in program
execution.
PC3. Analyze and compare different data representations like integers, floating-point numbers, and
character encoding.
Scripting and Linux Commands
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC4. Create and execute basic scripts in Linux/Unix for file management and automation tasks.
PC5. Apply Linux commands effectively for navigating the file system, managing processes, and
configuring system settings.
PC6. Troubleshoot scripting errors and utilize command-line utilities for system administration
tasks.
Python Programming
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC7. Develop Python programs that handle input/output operations, including reading from and
writing to files.
PC8. Implement control structures like loops, conditions, and functions to solve programming
challenges.

NIE/SSC/N1803: Analysis and understanding of biomolecular structures
using Bioinformatics tools
Description
This NOS delves into the intricacies of biomolecular structures through Bioinformatics tools, covering
fundamental concepts such as the physical and chemical properties of amino acids and polypeptides.
Students will explore the complexities of protein structures, including non-covalent and covalent
interactions, primary and secondary structures, and various structural motifs. The curriculum includes in-
depth discussions on protein folding, post-translation modifications, and the relationship between protein
sequence, structure, and function.
Scope
The scope covers the following :
Fundamentals of Biomolecular Structures: Covers amino acid properties, structural motifs, and
protein folding principles.
Protein Structure Determination Techniques: Includes X-ray diffraction, NMR, and electron
microscopy methods.
Exploration of Structural Databases: Introduces databases like PDB, NDB, and tools for structural
analysis.
Prediction, Modeling, and Simulation: Focuses on predicting protein structures, molecular dynamics
simulations, and practical applications.
Elements and Performance Criteria
Theoretical Foundations
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC1. Demonstrate knowledge of amino acid properties and their role in protein structure.
PC2. Explain the principles of secondary structures like alpha helices and beta sheets
PC3. Analyze the factors influencing protein folding and the implications of the Levinthal Paradox
Experimental Techniques
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC4. Describe the steps involved in protein isolation and purification techniques
PC5. Evaluate different methods of protein structure determination such as X-ray diffraction and
NMR spectroscopy.
PC6. Interpret electron density maps and understand the process of structure refinement.
Computational Tools and Databases
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC7. Use software tools to predict protein secondary structures and analyze structural motifs.
PC8. Demonstrate proficiency in accessing and querying protein databases like PDB and SCOP.
PC9. Apply molecular modeling techniques to simulate protein interactions and dynamics.
Knowledge and Understanding (KU)

Description

• practical demonstration of skills & knowledge gained by the candidate through the training. Each
