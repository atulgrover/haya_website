CUTM/AGR/Q0701: Aquaponics Cultivator
Brief Job Description
The Individual is responsible to understand that Aquaponics is a productive, innovative and sustainable
approach to food production that combines hydroponics the practice of growing plants in water with
aquaculture, the rearing of aquatic animals. Fish faces serve as a natural fertilizer for the plants in this
recirculating system.
Personal Attributes
An Aquaponics Cultivator should be observant, environmentally conscious, and technically skilled. They
must possess patience, problem-solving abilities, and a hands-on approach to managing integrated
systems. A willingness to learn, adaptability to changing conditions, and strong attention to hygiene, water
quality, and plant-fish health are essential for sustainable operations.

CUTM/AGR/N0701: Introduction to Aquaponics
Description
This NOS covers the fundamental understanding of aquaponics, an integrated system combining
aquaculture and hydroponics. It involves knowledge of biological processes, system setup, water quality,
and environmental management. Learners gain insights into sustainable food production using closed-loop
systems that are efficient, eco-friendly, and suitable for small to medium-scale cultivation.
Scope
The scope covers the following :
The scope includes the identification and setup of basic aquaponics systems, understanding the
interaction between fish, plants, and microbes, and monitoring environmental conditions. It also
covers daily system maintenance, recognizing and resolving basic issues, and ensuring proper care
of aquatic and plant life in an integrated and sustainable farming system.
Elements and Performance Criteria
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC1. History, evolution and principles of Aquaponics.
PC2. Components of an Aquaponics system.
PC3. Types of an Aquaponics system.
PC4. Water quality monitoring.
PC5. Aquaponics vs traditional agriculture.
PC6. Future trends and Innovations of Aquaponics.
Knowledge and Understanding (KU)
The individual on the job needs to know and understand:
KU1. the basic principles and concepts of aquaponics and its sustainable advantages.
KU2. the symbiotic relationship between fish, plants, and bacteria in a closed-loop system.
KU3. the different types of aquaponics systems (media bed, NFT, deep water culture).
KU4. the water chemistry parameters (pH, ammonia, nitrites, nitrates) critical for system health.
KU5. suitable fish species and plant varieties for aquaponics.
KU6. basic biological processes such as the nitrogen cycle.
KU7. the components of an aquaponics setup: fish tank, grow beds, biofilters, and aeration
systems.
KU8. environmental requirements: light, temperature, and humidity for optimal plant and fish
growth.
KU9. common challenges in aquaponics systems and basic troubleshooting techniques.
KU10. sustainable and organic practices in aquaponics cultivation.

CUTM/AGR/N0702: Aquaponics System Layout and its Components.
Description
This NOS outlines the knowledge and skills required to design and install the layout of an aquaponics
system. It includes understanding system types, identifying components, planning spatial arrangements,
and assembling all parts to ensure smooth circulation and effective functioning. It prepares learners to set
up functional and scalable aquaponics units.
Scope
The scope covers the following :
The scope of this NOS includes identifying and arranging essential components of an aquaponics
system, interpreting layout plans, and ensuring proper connectivity. It focuses on structural setup,
water flow design, and component placement to support healthy fish and plant growth. It also
addresses safety, troubleshooting, and basic system testing.
Elements and Performance Criteria
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC1. Aquaponic System Components.
PC2. Circulation and Water Flow
PC3. Media-Based Systems
PC4. Nutrient Film Technique (NFT) Systems
PC5. Deep Water Culture (DWC) Systems
PC6. Hybrid Systems
PC7. Energy Efficiency in System Design.
PC8. Cost and Budgeting
Knowledge and Understanding (KU)
The individual on the job needs to know and understand:
KU1. the fundamental design principles of various aquaponics systems.
KU2. key components of the system: fish tank, grow bed, sump tank, water pump, biofilter, and
aerators.
KU3. the function and interdependence of each component in maintaining a healthy system.
KU4. plumbing layouts for efficient water circulation and nutrient flow.
KU5. system sizing based on space, fish stock, and plant load.
KU6. materials and tools required for system construction and assembly.
KU7. the role of mechanical and biological filtration in system health.
KU8. how to position components for easy access and optimal sunlight exposure for plants.
KU9. safety and ergonomic considerations in system layout.

CUTM/AGR/N0703: The Nitrogen cycle/Water testing and Quality control
Description
This NOS focuses on understanding the nitrogen cycle and conducting water quality testing in an
aquaponics system. It covers the importance of maintaining optimal water parameters for fish and plant
health, the function of beneficial bacteria, and the use of test kits to ensure a safe and productive
environment.
Scope
The scope covers the following :
The scope of this NOS includes monitoring and managing the nitrogen cycle through regular water
testing. It involves understanding biological filtration, interpreting test results, and ensuring safe
nitrogen levels. It also addresses preventive and corrective actions to maintain high-quality water,
ensuring healthy aquaponics system performance.
Elements and Performance Criteria
Understanding the different quality parameters associated with aquaponics
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC1. Important water quality parameters, ensuring optimal conditions for both fish and plant
health.
PC2. Factors influencing bacterial populations.
Knowledge on various methods of assessment and maintenance of quality
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC3. Nutrient balance to maintain the quality of water.
PC4. Fish and Plant health Assessment.
PC5. Management of water quality parameters within prescribed limits.
PC6. Assessment of Fish and plant health.
PC7. Quality certification standards.
Knowledge and Understanding (KU)
The individual on the job needs to know and understand:
KU1. the nitrogen cycle and its relevance in aquaponics (ammonia, nitrites, nitrates).
KU2. the role of beneficial bacteria in converting fish waste into plant nutrients.
KU3. the signs and consequences of ammonia or nitrite toxicity in fish.
KU4. optimal water parameters: pH, temperature, DO (Dissolved Oxygen), ammonia, nitrite,
nitrate.
KU5. how imbalances in the nitrogen cycle affect plant and fish health.
KU6. with biological filtration and its importance in nitrogen processing.

CUTM/AGR/N0704: Ornamental fish in Aquaponics system
Description
This NOS covers the knowledge and practices required to rear and maintain ornamental fish in an
aquaponics system. It includes fish selection, care, feeding, health management, and understanding their
role in nutrient cycling and system aesthetics, thereby contributing to the sustainability and market appeal
of aquaponics setups.
Scope
The scope covers the following :
The scope involves selecting suitable ornamental fish, ensuring their optimal health and water
quality, monitoring for disease, and implementing proper feeding and handling practices. It also
includes understanding their role in ecosystem balance and leveraging ornamental fish to enhance
the commercial and aesthetic value of aquaponics systems.
Elements and Performance Criteria
Knowledge on taxonomy of ornamental fish
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC1. Selection of suitable ornamental fish species for aquaponics.
PC2. Assessing the compatibility of Ornamental fish with that of the plants.
PC3. Enhancing the well-being of fish in aquaponics environments.
Information about handling of ornamental fish waste
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC4. Bio filtration and Nitrification.
PC5. Integrating grow beds as biological filters.
PC6. Designing efficient siphon or drain systems to remove excess waste.
PC7. Monitoring water quality parameters.
Knowledge on Waste-to-Energy Concepts:
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC8. Exploring the potential for converting fish waste into energy.
PC9. Adjusting waste management strategies based on water quality results.
PC10. Continuous improvement strategies for minimizing waste.
PC11. Continuous improvement strategies for minimizing waste production.
Knowledge and Understanding (KU)
The individual on the job needs to know and understand:
KU1. the role of ornamental fish in aquaponics systems for aesthetic and commercial purposes.
KU2. commonly used ornamental fish species suitable for aquaponics (e.g., goldfish, koi, guppies).
