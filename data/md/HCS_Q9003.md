HCS/Q9003: Upcycling Scraps and E-waste Artisan
Brief Job Description
An upcycling scraps and e-waste artisan transforms discarded materials and electronic waste into unique,
functional art pieces or products. By creatively repurposing these items, they promote sustainability while
showcasing their artistic vision and craftsmanship.
Personal Attributes
An upcycling artisan is resourceful and innovative, with a strong commitment to sustainability and
environmental awareness. They possess a keen eye for design and detail, allowing them to creatively
transform waste materials into beautiful and functional items.

HCS/N9015: Carry out the process of upcycling scraps and e-waste
Description
This OS unit is about preparing raw materials, and upcycling scraps and e-waste to an end product.
Scope
The scope covers the following :
collect required raw materials and tools for making final product
follow safety measures while handling waste
perform the process of upcycling scraps and e-waste
check quality and productivity standards of upcycled scraps and e-waste
Elements and Performance Criteria
Collect required raw materials and tools for making final product
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC1. Identify and plan the structure, artifacts, and decorative to be made.
PC2. Collect raw materials like wood, metal, glass, rubber, e-waste, plastic, and cloth waste.
PC3. Check and sort scraps and e-waste as per requirements.
PC4. Place raw materials at designated locations safely.
PC5. Collect tools (marker, ruler, cutters, welding machine, etc.) required for upcycling.
PC6. Place tools at designated locations safely.
PC7. Deliver remaining raw materials to storage locations.
Follow safety measures while handling waste
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC8. Handle all waste materials safely and securely.
PC9. Use PPE for tasks (e.g., goggles, gloves, face mask).
PC10. Maintain proper posture while lifting or working.
Perform the process of upcycling scraps and e-waste
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC11. Prepare and upcycle wood scraps by marking, cutting, sanding, and engraving the pieces,
then assembling them into the required structure or decorative item, while checking the
quality for modifications if necessary.
PC12. Prepare and upcycle metal scraps by marking, cutting, assembling, and welding the pieces
into the required structure or decorative item, then checking the quality for modifications if
necessary.
PC13. Prepare and upcycle glass scraps by marking, cutting, assembling pieces into the required
structure or decorative item, and checking the quality for modifications if necessary.
PC14. Prepare and upcycle rubber scraps by marking, cutting, assembling, and joining pieces with
industrial glue, and checking the quality of the final product.

HCS/N8506: Entrepreneurship Skills in Upcycling Scraps and E-waste
Sector
Description
This unit focuses on entrepreneurship in the upcycling scraps and e-waste sector involves transforming
discarded materials into valuable products, driving sustainability while addressing waste management
challenges. This requires skills in innovation, product design, and resourcefulness to create marketable
goods from waste.
Scope
The scope covers the following :
understanding waste materials
creative thinking
sustainability awareness
understanding waste processing technology
awareness of regulations
Elements and Performance Criteria
Understanding Waste Materials
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC1. identify the different types of waste materials, such as metals, plastics, and electronic
components, and know how they can be safely and effectively reused
PC2. identify which parts of the waste have value and how to turn them into usable products
Creative Thinking
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC3. need to think creatively about how to transform old, discarded items into something new and
useful
PC4. use innovative design helps in creating appealing products from waste, which can attract
more customers
Sustainability Awareness
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC5. knowing the positive impact of upcycling on reducing pollution, conserving resources, and
promoting eco-friendly living
PC6. use processes that are environmentally safe and energy-efficient, which is key to appealing
to eco-conscious consumers
Understanding Waste Processing Technology
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC7. learn how to operate machines used in processing scrap, like shredders, compactors, or
smelters
PC8. know about safe methods to handle chemicals used in extracting metals from e-waste (like
gold from circuit boards)

Awareness of Regulations
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC9. know the regulations around e-waste handling, disposal, and upcycling, including any
certifications or permits they need
PC10. aware of the recycling and waste management standards that apply to their business
PC11. know local laws and regulations regarding the disposal and recycling of e-waste to stay
compliant
Knowledge and Understanding (KU)
The individual on the job needs to know and understand:
KU1. need a deep understanding of circular economy principles, which focus on reducing waste,
reusing resources, and minimizing environmental harm
KU2. techniques that minimize waste and promote recycling and upcycling, such as resource
optimization and material innovation
KU3. knowledge of local and international environmental laws and regulations governing the
handling, disposal, and recycling of e-waste and scrap materials
KU4. different categories of scrap (metal, plastic, fabric, etc.) and e-waste (computers, phones,
electronics) is essential for identifying valuable materials for upcycling
KU5. organizing and optimizing the collection, transportation, and storage of waste materials,
ensuring cost-efficiency and sustainability
KU6. know where and how to source scrap and e-waste materials reliably
KU7. know how to properly sort and categorize waste materials to maximize their potential for
upcycling
KU8. recognize market trends to identify which upcycled products are in demand, such as fashion
accessories, home decor, or tech gadgets made from e-waste
KU9. story behind upcycled products often adds value, highlighting their sustainable impact
KU10. legal framework surrounding e-waste management, including health and safety standards
for handling toxic substances and ensuring proper disposal of non-recyclable waste
Generic Skills (GS)
User/individual on the job needs to know how to:
GS1. thinking of new ways to turn waste and scraps into useful or artistic products
GS2. creating designs that are attractive, functional, and made from recycled materials
GS3. knowing the types of scrap and e-waste materials, like metals, plastics, and electronics, and
how to work with them
GS4. environmental benefits of upcycling and scraps
GS5. methods that reduce waste and promote recycling
GS6. being flexible and able to adjust to new trends, materials, or market demands
GS7. listening to what customers want and creating products that meet their desires

HCS/N9901: Coordinate with colleagues and work as a team
Description
This NOS describes the communication and coordination skills and knowledge to work with colleagues and
supervisor to achieve a smooth and hazard-free workflow.
Scope
The scope covers the following :
interact with supervisor or superior
work as a team by coordinating with colleagues within and outside the department and include
inputs on PwD & Gender Sensitisation
report and Document
Elements and Performance Criteria
Interact with supervisor or superior
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC1. comply with health, safety gender, and PwD (People with disability) related instructions
applicable to the workplace
PC2. actively participate in mock drills/ evacuation procedures; group discussions, training
sensitization programs for gender, and PwD awareness organized at the workplace.
PC3. receive job orders and instructions from reporting supervisor and receive feedback on work
standards.
PC4. understand the work output requirements, targets, performance indicators and incentives.
PC5. deliver quality work on time and report any anticipated reasons for delays and handover
completed work to supervisor
PC6. report on any grievances, production defects and any potential hazards.
Work as a team by coordinating with colleagues within and outside the department and include inputs on
PwD & Gender Sensitisation
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC7. communicate maintenance and repair schedule proactively to the supervisor
PC8. interact and clarify doubts on design, usage of materials & tools, quality & standards
compliance, etc.
Report and Document
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC9. report in time for shortage or need of raw materials
PC10. communicate with colleagues from within and other departments, clearly and effectively on
all aspects to carry out the work among the team
PC11. maintain the etiquette, use polite language, demonstrate responsible and disciplined
behavior towards colleagues.
PC12. put team over individual goals and multi-task or share work where necessary supporting the
colleagues.

| Sector | Sector is a conglomeration of different business operations having similar business and interests. It may also be defined as a distinct subset of the economy whose components share similar characteristics and interests. |
| :--- | :---: |
| Sub-sector | Sub-sector is derived from a further breakdown based on the characteristics and interests of its components. |
| Occupation | Occupation is a set of job roles, which perform similar/ related set of functions in an industry. |
| Job role | Job role defines a unique set of functions that together form a unique employment opportunity in an organisation. |
| Occupational Standards (OS) | OS specify the standards of performance an individual must achieve when carrying out a function in the workplace, together with the Knowledge and Understanding (KU) they need to meet that standard consistently. Occupational Standards are applicable both in the Indian and global contexts. |
| Performance Criteria (PC) | Performance Criteria (PC) are statements that together specify the standard of performance required when carrying out a task. |
| National Occupational Standards (NOS) | NOS are occupational standards which apply uniquely in the Indian context. |
| Qualifications Pack (QP) | QP comprises the set of OS, together with the educational, training and other criteria required to perform a job role. A QP is assigned a unique qualifications pack code. |
| Unit Code | Unit code is a unique identifier for an Occupational Standard, which is denoted by an ‘N’ |
| Unit Title | Unit title gives a clear overall statement about what the incumbent should be able to do. |
| Description | Description gives a short summary of the unit content. This would be helpful to anyone searching on a database to verify that this is the appropriate OS they are looking for. |
| Scope | Scope is a set of statements specifying the range of variables that an individual may have to deal with in carrying out the function which have a critical impact on quality of performance required. |

| Electives | Electives are NOS/set of NOS that are identified by the sector as contributive to specialization in a job role. There may be multiple electives within a QP for each specialized job role. Trainees must select at least one elective for the successful completion of a QP with Electives. |
| Options | Options are NOS/set of NOS that are identified by the sector as additional skills. There may be multiple options within a QP. It is not mandatory to select any of the options to complete a QP with Options. |
