ELE/Q1502: Electronics
Brief Job Description
This qualification is designed for Electronics Technologists working in Manufacturing and Engineering
Technology. It prepares professionals to specialize in areas such as product assembly, circuit design,
installation, maintenance, and testing. Key responsibilities include utilizing specialized software for
schematic capture, handling a range of tools and equipment, and maintaining professional client
interactions with a strong emphasis on confidentiality and ethical practice. The curriculum encompasses
essential topics like work organization, electronics applications, prototype hardware design, embedded
systems programming, fault diagnosis and repair, as well as assembly and measurement techniques. With
a focus on practical skills and lifelong learning, this qualification aligns with international standards and
best practices in the rapidly evolving field of Electronics Engineering.
Personal Attributes
The person should be result oriented with good technical and analytical skills, should have Excellent
Interpersonal Skills, communication and presentation skills and a good team player. They should have
ability to manage projects, prioritizing of work and mentoring the budding engineers.
Applicable National Occupational Standards (NOS)
Compulsory NOS:
- 1. ELE/N1502: Work organization and management (Electronics)
- 2. ELE/N1511: Communication and interpersonal skills (Electronics)
- 3. ELE/N1513: Hardware design and development (Electronics)
- 4. ELE/N1512: Assembly, modification, and repair (Electronics)
- 5. ELE/N1506: Fault finding (Electronics)
- 6. ELE/N1505: Embedded systems programming (Electronics)
- 7. ELE/N1507: Measurement, documentation, and reporting (Electronics)

ELE/N1502: Work organization and management (Electronics)
Description
This NOS unit is about proper training, the correct use of equipment, and adherence to safety protocols in
manufacturing settings.
Scope
The scope covers the following :
Principles and Applications of Safe Working
Purposes, Uses, Care, and Maintenance of Equipment and Materials
Environmental and Safety Principles in Good Housekeeping
Principles of Team Working and Their Applications
Elements and Performance Criteria
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC1. Plan and organize work tasks based on available time, workflow, and required resources.
PC2. Prioritize assignments and resolve challenges using appropriate organizational methods.
PC3. Maintain a professional and ethical demeanour in all work-related interactions.
PC4. Apply health, safety, and environmental regulations in all workplace activities.
PC5. Use tools and equipment correctly while ensuring proper care, storage, and maintenance.
PC6. Follow sustainable practices and minimize waste in daily activities.
PC7. Reflect on one’s work output and identify areas for personal and process improvement.
PC8. Engage proactively in professional development to enhance knowledge and skills.
PC9. Work responsibly with autonomy while ensuring the wellbeing of oneself and others.
PC10. Contribute ideas and actions that support continuous improvement in the organization or
project.
Knowledge and Understanding (KU)
The individual on the job needs to know and understand:
KU1. Knowledge of task planning, workflow management, and resource allocation principles.
KU2. Understanding of prioritization techniques and problem-solving approaches.
KU3. Knowledge of professional ethics and workplace conduct standards.
KU4. Understanding of health, safety, and environmental regulations.
KU5. Knowledge of proper usage, care, and maintenance of tools and equipment.

ELE/N1511: Communication and interpersonal skills (Electronics)
Description
This NOS is about to covers the ability to communicate effectively and maintain professional interpersonal
relationships to support coordination and teamwork in electronics-related tasks.
Scope
The scope covers the following :
Covers effective verbal and non-verbal communication, active listening, teamwork, coordination,
and maintaining professional relationships in electronics-related work environments.
Elements and Performance Criteria
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC1. Maintain professional relationships and communicate effectively with colleagues,
supervisors, and external stakeholders.
PC2. Use ICT tools for workplace communication, documentation, and external coordination.
PC3. Present ideas, proposals, and technical information clearly to internal teams and external
groups.
PC4. Explain technical concepts in simple terms to clients and non-specialists.
PC5. Share knowledge, collaborate in teams, and support collective problem-solving.
PC6. Adapt communication style, language, and behaviour based on situational needs while
maintaining professionalism.
PC7. Challenge inappropriate language or behaviour respectfully, ensuring adherence to
workplace ethics.
PC8. Maintain accurate records, logs, and documents to ensure traceability and compliance.
PC9. Use international symbols, diagrams, and technical languages correctly in communication
and documentation.
PC10. Comply with ISO and other standardized formats for reports, documentation, and
communication.
Knowledge and Understanding (KU)
The individual on the job needs to know and understand:
KU1. Knowledge of professional communication and relationship management in the workplace.
KU2. Understanding of ICT tools for communication, documentation, and coordination.
KU3. Knowledge of effective presentation techniques for technical and non-technical audiences.
KU4. Understanding of methods to simplify technical concepts for non-specialists.
KU5. Knowledge of teamwork, collaboration, and knowledge-sharing practices.
Generic Skills (GS)

ELE/N1513: Hardware design and development (Electronics)
Description
This NOS unit is about selecting and calculating appropriate component values, designing and modifying
circuits to meet specifications, and using simulation software for testing.
Scope
The scope covers the following :
Calculate and Select Component Values That Are Fit-for-Purpose
Implement Heatsinking Principles
Design Modifications to Given Basic Electronics Blocks
Design Circuits that Meet Specifications and Are Fit for Purpose
Elements and Performance Criteria
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC1. Read and interpret wiring diagrams, schematics, engineering drawings, and technical
manuals accurately.
PC2. Assist in designing and developing electronic circuits and systems across analog, digital,
communication, embedded, and power domains.
PC3. Calculate and select appropriate electronic component values based on design
specifications.
PC4. Use PCB design and schematic capture software to develop circuit schematics and PCB
layouts.
PC5. Apply 3D PCB visualization tools to verify component placement, mechanical fit, and
manufacturability.
PC6. Use circuit simulation tools to validate circuit behaviour and ensure designs are fit for
purpose.
PC7. Conduct research and analysis proportional to the design requirement, applying innovation
and creative problem-solving.
PC8. Prepare project schedules, cost estimates, bills of materials, and resource requirements
based on specifications.
PC9. Generate PCB manufacturing data and documentation as per industry standards such as
ANSI and IEC.
PC10. Evaluate design options considering constraints like time, cost, resources, and functionality,
and recommend optimal solutions.
Knowledge and Understanding (KU)
The individual on the job needs to know and understand:
KU1. Knowledge of interpreting wiring diagrams, schematics, and engineering drawings.

ELE/N1512: Assembly, modification, and repair (Electronics)
Description
This NOS is about to covers the ability to assemble, modify, troubleshoot, and repair electronic systems
and components in accordance with specifications and standards.
Scope
The scope covers the following :
Covers assembling, modifying, troubleshooting, and repairing electronic systems and components,
including component handling, soldering, testing, fault diagnosis, and ensuring functionality as per
specifications.
Elements and Performance Criteria
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC1. Identify and select appropriate tools required for electronic assembly and repair.
PC2. Follow IPC 610 and other relevant industry standards during electronic assembly.
PC3. Apply IPC 7711/7721 or equivalent standards for rework and repair activities.
PC4. Maintain safe working practices in handling tools, components, and equipment.
PC5. Implement ESD-safe procedures throughout assembly and repair operations.
PC6. Assemble electronic components onto PCBs to create functional circuits.
PC7. Identify, assemble, and use electromechanical components and parts.
PC8. Form, crimp, route, and assemble cable harnesses as per specifications.
PC9. Rework design mistakes to bring assemblies in line with industry standards.
PC10. Replace damaged or incorrect components using through-hole and SMD soldering
techniques.
PC11. Diagnose faulty PCBs to identify defective components or soldering issues.
PC12. Repair or replace defective components according to IPC-approved repair techniques.
PC13. Conduct soldering, desoldering, cleaning, and inspection as per quality requirements.
PC14. Fit or adapt non-original or substitute components/modules to achieve temporary or
prototype functionality.
PC15. Inspect completed assemblies and verify that workmanship meets industry quality and
safety standards.
Knowledge and Understanding (KU)
The individual on the job needs to know and understand:
KU1. Knowledge of tools and equipment used in electronic assembly and repair.
KU2. Understanding of IPC 610 standards and industry requirements for assembly quality.
KU3. Knowledge of IPC 7711/7721 standards for rework and repair processes.
