TEL/Q2400: Telecom E-Waste Handler
Brief Job Description
A Telecom E-Waste Handler operates as a micro-entrepreneur, establishing and managing telecom e-waste
collection and aggregation centres. The individual ensures safe handling, segregation, and secure
transportation of waste for further processing while promoting sustainable disposal practices. The person
collaborates with stakeholders and utilizes advanced technologies to enhance operational efficiency and
regulatory compliance.
Personal Attributes
The individual should possess excellent communication, organizational, and problem-solving skills, along
with attention to detail and a commitment to safety, sustainability, and stakeholder collaboration.
Applicable National Occupational Standards (NOS)
Compulsory NOS:
- 1. TEL/N2401: Undertake telecom e-waste collection, handling, and disposal operations
- 2. TEL/N9108: Follow sustainability practices in telecom operations
- 3. DGT/VSQ/N0101: Employability Skills (30 Hours)

TEL/N2401: Undertake telecom e-waste collection, handling, and disposal
operations
Description
This unit covers the coordination and execution of telecom e-waste collection, safe handling, segregation,
and disposal while ensuring compliance with safety and environmental regulations.
Scope
The scope covers the following :
Assess the value and hazards of telecom e-waste streams
Identify, collect, and transport telecom e-waste
Segregate and handle telecom e-waste safely
Establish and manage a telecom e-waste collection and handling centre
Elements and Performance Criteria
Assess the value and hazards of telecom e-waste streams
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC1. classify telecom and non-telecom e-waste based on material composition and potential for
reuse or recycling
PC2. identify valuable and hazardous components in different types of telecom e-waste
PC3. apply circular economy practices, including segregation for reuse, refurbishment, and
recycling
PC4. communicate the benefits of responsible e-waste management to stakeholders using
posters, digital platforms, and community outreach
PC5. follow legal requirements and safety guidelines for handling and storing telecom e-waste
Identify, collect, and transport telecom e-waste
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC6. identify key sources and generators of telecom e-waste, such as service providers, retailers,
and consumers
PC7. select and use appropriate transport options for collecting and transporting telecom e-waste,
ensuring compliance with safety regulations
PC8. use the necessary Personal Protective Equipment (PPE) while handling and transporting
telecom e-waste to prevent exposure to hazardous materials
PC9. maintain documentation and records of collected e-waste, including source details, weight,
and classification
Segregate and handle telecom e-waste safely
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC10. use appropriate tools and equipment to dismantle and segregate telecom e-waste
PC11. categorize the collected telecom e-waste based on type, condition, and value for reuse or
recycling

The individual on the job needs to know and understand:

KU2. the principles of circular economy and 3Rs (Reduce, Reuse, Recycle) in telecom e-waste
management
KU3. the typical material composition of telecom e-waste, including valuable and hazardous
components
KU4. the environmental and health hazards of improper disposal and handling of telecom e-waste
KU5. pricing and economic aspects of e-waste management, including negotiations with upstream
and downstream partners
KU6. emergency response protocols, including firefighting and chemical spill management
KU7. the importance of PPE, hazard communication, and workplace safety measures
KU8. proper packaging, labeling, and transport protocols for safe e-waste movement
KU9. transport and logistics options for efficient and compliant e-waste collection
KU10. basic entrepreneurship and accounting principles, including financial tracking, profit
margins, and operational costs
KU11. marketing and outreach strategies to build a network of regular e-waste generators
KU12. methods to identify and expand collection networks in under-served areas
KU13. basics of national and international regulations related to telecom e-waste management and
disposal

TEL/N9108: Follow sustainability practices in telecom operations
Description
This unit focuses on sustainability practices in the telecom sector, detailing processes to identify
components for recycling, refurbishment, or responsible disposal while ensuring compliance with
environmental standards and promoting sustainable repair and maintenance practices.
Scope
The scope covers the following :
Identify recyclable, reusable, and disposable components
Adhere to environmental standards
Follow sustainable repair practices
Comply with sustainability protocols
Elements and Performance Criteria
Identify recyclable, reusable, and disposable components
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC1. identify telecom components that can be recycled, refurbished, or reused during repair and
maintenance
PC2. sort materials into recyclable, reusable, and hazardous waste categories
PC3. examine faulty components for potential refurbishment and document findings
PC4. label and segregate hazardous materials for specialized disposal
PC5. evaluate dismantled parts to determine their suitability for recycling or safe disposal
Adhere to environmental standards
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC6. follow guidelines for safe handling, storage, and disposal of hazardous and non-hazardous
materials
PC7. maintain compliance with environmental regulations relevant to e-waste management
PC8. record all e-waste disposal and recycling activities as per company policies
PC9. use protective equipment when handling hazardous waste to prevent environmental
contamination
PC10. conduct regular audits of waste management processes to ensure alignment with
sustainability standards
Follow sustainable repair practices
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC11. use repair techniques that minimize waste generation and energy consumption
PC12. select energy-efficient and eco-friendly spare parts for repairs
PC13. optimize material usage by reusing functional components from discarded devices
PC14. minimize the use of single-use materials during maintenance and repair tasks
PC15. diagnose faults accurately to avoid unnecessary replacements
