#### HCS/Q0801: Ceramics Preparation Artisan
Brief Job Description
Ceramics Preparation Artisan prepares ceramics in various steps and various methods to make final
prepared ceramic for further processes.
Personal Attributes
A good Ceramics Preparation Artisan is detail-oriented and has a strong sense of creativity, along with
good hands-on skills for working with clay. They are patient and organised, ensuring that materials are
prepared correctly for the best results.
Applicable National Occupational Standards (NOS)
Compulsory NOS:
1. HCS/N0110: Packaging and Presentation of Ceramic Products
2. HCS/N8509: Entrepreneurship Skill for Ceramics Preparation Artisan
3. HCS/N9952: Working together as a team
4. HCS/N9946: Managing workplace safety
5. HCS/N9937: Maintain Cleanliness
6. HCS/N9941: Understanding Business Operations
7. DGT/VSQ/N0101: Employability Skills (30 Hours)
Electives(mandatory to select at least one):
Elective 1: Casting Operator- Ceramics
The Casting Operator in ceramics is responsible for pouring liquid clay into moulds to create various
ceramic shapes and ensuring that the casting process runs smoothly. This role requires attention to detail
and precision to achieve high-quality finished products.
1. HCS/N0602: Carry out the processes required to prepare the cast
Elective 2: Colour Mixing
Colour mixing in ceramics is about blending different colours and glazes to make new shades. It requires
knowing how colours work together to get the right look, so the finished pieces are bright and even after
they are fired in the kiln.

Elective 3: Engobing Operator (Ceramics)
An Engobing Operator in ceramics applies a layer of engobe, which is a liquid clay mixture, to the surface
of ceramic pieces. This process helps to add colour, texture, and a smooth finish to the product before it is
fired.
1. HCS/N0703: Engobing of ceramics products
Elective 4: Furnace Operator
A Furnace Operator in ceramics is responsible for managing and operating kilns used to fire ceramic
pieces.
1. HCS/N2104: Carry out furnace operation
Elective 5: Glazing Operator
A Glazing Operator in ceramics applies glaze to finished pieces to enhance their appearance and durability.
This role involves preparing the glaze, ensuring even application, and sometimes adjusting the glaze
mixture for desired effects.
1. HCS/N0817: Perform Glazing of Ceramics Products
Elective 6: Jigger Operator
A Jigger Operator in ceramics is responsible for using a jigger machine to shape and form clay into specific
designs.
1. HCS/N0502: Perform jiggering of Dough
Elective 7: Moulder
A Moulder in ceramics is responsible for shaping and forming clay into specific designs, often using hand
techniques or molds.
1. HCS/N0408: Prepare the Mould for production
Elective 8: Mixing Operator
A Mixing Operator in ceramics is responsible for preparing and mixing raw materials, such as clay, glazes,
and other additives, to create the desired consistency and properties for ceramic production.
1. HCS/N0303: Perform mixing of raw materials using equipment

#### HCS/N0110: Packaging and Presentation of Ceramic Products
Description
This unit focuses on the packaging and presentation of ceramic products, emphasizing the importance of
protective measures, branding, and aesthetic appeal. It covers the selection of materials, packing
techniques, and the use of eco-friendly options.
Scope
The scope covers the following :
packaging for protection presentation for aesthetic appeal storytelling and experience sustainability considerations
point of sale presentation
#### Elements and Performance Criteria
Packaging for Protection
#### To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
- PC1. Use the bubble wrap and foam materials to cushion the ceramic items and protect them from
shocks and impacts during transportation
- PC2. use corrugated cardboard boxes provide structural integrity
- PC3. fill any empty spaces within the box to prevent movement during transit
- PC4. use cardboard dividers for fragile items (like plates or bowls) to prevent them from touching
each other
- PC5. tape the boxes and consider using fragile stickers or labels to indicate care needed when
handling
Presentation for Aesthetic Appeal
#### To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
- PC6. invest in branded boxes or wrapping paper featuring the logo, which can enhance brand
recognition and create a professional image
- PC7. include labels with product information, care instructions, and brand storytelling
- PC8. use colors that complement the ceramics, enhancing the overall aesthetic
- PC9. a minimalist white box might highlight a colorful ceramic piece
- PC10. use boxes with transparent windows to showcase the product without opening the packaging
Storytelling and Experience
#### To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
- PC11. share the story behind the ceramics products
- PC12. add a small surprise element, like a thank-you note or a sample of another product
Sustainability Considerations
#### To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:

#### HCS/N8509: Entrepreneurship Skill for Ceramics Preparation Artisan
Description
This unit is designed to equip ceramics preparation artisans with the essential entrepreneurship skills
needed to build, manage, and grow a successful business in the ceramics industry. It focuses on providing
the practical knowledge and abilities required to operate effectively as a creative entrepreneur, from
product development to financial management and marketing.
Scope
The scope covers the following :
Introduction to Entrepreneurship
Entrepreneurial mindset
Business planning for ceramics artisans
Marketing and branding
Financial management
Sales and customer relations
Networking and collaboration
Problem-solving and adaptability
#### Elements and Performance Criteria
Introduction to Entrepreneurship
#### To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
- PC1. Overview of entrepreneurship and its relevance to ceramics artisans.
- PC2. Characteristics of successful entrepreneur artisans.
Entrepreneurial Mindset
#### To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
- PC3. Learn creativity and innovation in the context of ceramics.
- PC4. Embracing new ideas and being willing to experiment with different approaches to problem-
solving.
Business Planning for Ceramics Artisans
#### To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
- PC5. Craft a comprehensive business plan.
- PC6. Use market research method and identifying the niche (luxury ceramics, functional pottery,
decorative pieces).
- PC7. Define a clear business goals and timelines for growth.
Marketing and Branding
#### To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
- PC8. Develop a unique brand identity for ceramics products.
- PC9. Use storytelling and behind-the-scenes content to engage customers.
- PC10. Identify the social media and online platforms to showcase ceramics work.

#### To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
- PC11. Manage production costs (materials, tools, kiln usage).
- PC12. Use simple financial management software or even spreadsheets to track cash flow, profits,

Sales and Customer Relations
#### To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
- PC13. Use effective sales techniques for artisans.
- PC14. Build a lasting customer relationships through custom orders and feedback.

- PC16. Opportunities for collaborations with other artists or designers.

Problem-Solving and Adaptability
#### To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
- PC18. Handling production challenges like kiln issues or material shortages.
- PC19. Adapting designs and production methods based on market trends or customer preferences.
- PC20. Manage time effectively to balance production, marketing, and business management.
#### Knowledge and Understanding (KU)
The individual on the job needs to know and understand:
- KU1. know the craft deeply, including material properties (clay types, glaze chemistry, firing
techniques), and the needs of the target customers
- KU2. different types of ceramics (e.g., earthenware, stoneware, porcelain) and their uses
- KU3. preferences and trends in the ceramics market, such as minimalistic styles, functional home
decor, or artistic sculptures
- KU4. pricing strategies based on product quality, uniqueness, and customer expectations
- KU5. understand how to create unique selling propositions (USP) by focusing on design innovations, sustainable practices, or craftsmanship techniques
- KU6. study successful artisans and identify gaps or opportunities in the market to position the
brand uniquely
- KU7. understand batch production versus custom-made products and when to apply each based on customer demand and pricing
- KU8. understand the cost structure, including fixed costs (e.g., equipment, rent) and variable
costs (e.g., materials, labor)
- KU9. know how to interact with customers professionally, both online and offline, and respond to
feedback or complaints constructively
- KU10. develop loyalty programs or after-sales services to encourage repeat purchases

#### HCS/N0109: Perform Slip Trailing
Description
This unit focuses on decorating technique where liquid clay (slip) is applied to the surface of a leather-hard
piece using a fine-tipped squeeze bottle. The process allows for intricate designs, raised patterns, and
textures. Artists can create dots, lines, and shapes, controlling the flow of slip to achieve various effects.
Scope
The scope covers the following :
Prepare the slip
Fill the slip trailer
Test the flow
Apply the slip
Touch up and drying
#### Elements and Performance Criteria
Prepare the Slip
#### To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
- PC1. use the same type of clay that the pottery piece is made from
- PC2. break the clay into small pieces for easier mixing
- PC3. Use a hand blender, stick, or even a whisk to blend the clay and water mixture thoroughly
- PC4. use a fine mesh or sieve to remove any remaining lumps or impurities
Fill the Slip Trailer
#### To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
- PC5. select a slip trailer or squeeze bottle with a nozzle
- PC6. use a small funnel to carefully pour the slip into the bottle
- PC7. leave some space at the top (about 1/3 of the bottle empty) to prevent the slip from spilling
out when squeezed, and to allow air for smooth flow
- PC8. lightly squeeze the bottle to ensure the slip flows smoothly through the nozzle
Test the Flow
#### To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
- PC9. Use a scrap piece of clay, a tile, or even a piece of paper to test the flow of the slip before
applying it to the final piece
- PC10. start by applying gentle pressure to the slip trailer or squeeze bottle to see how easily the
slip flows
- PC11. practice making lines, dots, or patterns on the test surface
Apply the Slip
#### To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
- PC12. position the slip trailer close to the surface of your leather-hard clay piece

| Sector | Sector is a conglomeration of different business operations having similar business and interests. It may also be defined as a distinct subset of the economy whose components share similar characteristics and interests. |

| Sub-sector | Sub-sector is derived from a further breakdown based on the characteristics and interests of its components. |
| Occupation | Occupation is a set of job roles, which perform similar/ related set of functions in an industry. |
| Job role | Job role defines a unique set of functions that together form a unique employment opportunity in an organisation. |
| Occupational Standards (OS) | OS specify the standards of performance an individual must achieve when carrying out a function in the workplace, together with the Knowledge and Understanding (KU) they need to meet that standard consistently. Occupational Standards are applicable both in the Indian and global contexts. |
| Performance Criteria (PC) | Performance Criteria (PC) are statements that together specify the standard of performance required when carrying out a task. |
| National Occupational Standards (NOS) | NOS are occupational standards which apply uniquely in the Indian context. |
| Qualifications Pack (QP) | QP comprises the set of OS, together with the educational, training and other criteria required to perform a job role. A QP is assigned a unique qualifications pack code. |
| Unit Code | Unit code is a unique identifier for an Occupational Standard, which is denoted by an ‘N’ |
| Unit Title | Unit title gives a clear overall statement about what the incumbent should be able to do. |
| Description | Description gives a short summary of the unit content. This would be helpful to anyone searching on a database to verify that this is the appropriate OS they are looking for. |
| Scope | Scope is a set of statements specifying the range of variables that an individual may have to deal with in carrying out the function which have a critical impact on quality of performance required. |
