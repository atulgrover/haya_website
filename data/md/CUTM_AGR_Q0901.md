CUTM/AGR/Q0901: Vermimanuring Technician
Brief Job Description
The Individual is responsible to understand the vermimanuring and production of vermimanuring, selection
of composting material (organic and inorganic) and worm species, vermibed preparation and value
addition of vermicomposting and by-products for the sustainable agriculture production.
Personal Attributes
A Vermimanuring Technician should be environmentally conscious, physically fit, and attentive to detail.
Patience, cleanliness, and responsibility are essential for managing earthworms and organic waste. The
individual should possess basic technical and observational skills, a willingness to work outdoors, and a
positive attitude toward sustainable farming and soil health improvement.

CUTM/AGR/N0901: Handling of Vermimanuring
Description
This NOS focuses on the foundational steps of handling vermimanuring, including compost material
selection, vermibed preparation, inoculation of worms, and maintenance of composting environments to
produce high-quality organic manure.
Scope
The scope covers the following :
Involves hands-on activities for setting up vermibeds, monitoring physico-chemical conditions, and
post-processing vermicompost. Learners gain skills in initiating and managing vermimanuring
operations effectively for sustainable agriculture.
Elements and Performance Criteria
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC1. Identify the best method of preparing vermibed and selection of suitable species.
PC2. Selection of composting materials.
PC3. Pre-processing techniques of vermimanuring and inoculation of earthworms.
PC4. Analysis of chemical and physical parameters during vermimanuring.
PC5. Harvest and post harvesting process of vermimanure and it’s by- Product.
PC6. Identify different equipment for microbial and chemical analysis.
Knowledge and Understanding (KU)
The individual on the job needs to know and understand:
KU1. Concepts and methods of preparing vermibeds.
KU2. Types of compostable organic materials.
KU3. Pre-processing techniques for compost.
KU4. Physico-chemical parameters in composting (moisture, pH, aeration).
KU5. Earthworm inoculation practices.Post-harvest handling of vermicompost and by-products.
KU6. Safety and sanitation protocols during handling.
KU7. Tools and equipment for vermibed preparation.
KU8. Importance of temperature, humidity, and water activity.
KU9. Monitoring vermibed health and quality parameters.

CUTM/AGR/N0902: The Role of Earthworms in Vermimanuring
Description
This NOS explains the ecological, biological, and economic roles of earthworms in vermimanuring. Learners
understand how worms convert organic waste into rich manure and improve soil fertility and sustainability.
Scope
The scope covers the following :
Focuses on recognizing worm types, their contributions to soil health, and their effective role in the
vermicomposting ecosystem. It prepares learners to manage and optimize earthworm-based organic
recycling.
Elements and Performance Criteria
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC1. Types of earthworms used in Vermimanuring.
PC2. Anatomy and life cycle of earthworms.
PC3. Earthworm ecology and their role in soil health.
PC4. Differences and similarities of earthworms.
PC5. Setting Up a Vermimanuring System.
Knowledge and Understanding (KU)
The individual on the job needs to know and understand:
KU1. Earthworm anatomy and life cycle.
KU2. Ecology and behavioral traits of composting worms.
KU3. Role in soil structure, aeration, and microbial enrichment.
KU4. Differences among composting and burrowing worms.
KU5. Earthworm activity in nutrient cycling.
KU6. Contribution to sustainable agriculture.
KU7. Waste decomposition process via earthworms.
KU8. Organic matter transformation to vermicompost.
KU9. Environmental and economic impact.
Generic Skills (GS)
User/individual on the job needs to know how to:
GS1. Identify key composting earthworm species.
GS2. Set up and monitor worm activity in compost.

CUTM/AGR/N0903: Choosing the Right Earthworm Species
Description
This NOS guides the learner on selecting earthworm species best suited for vermicomposting. It enables
efficient organic waste decomposition and production of nutrient-rich compost by aligning environmental
and species-specific factors.
Scope
The scope covers the following :
Focuses on species identification, suitability to climatic and feed conditions, and management
practices. Learners are trained to choose and manage worms to optimize vermicomposting results.
Elements and Performance Criteria
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC1. Eisenia fetida (Red Wiggler)
PC2. Eudrilus eugeniae (African Nightcrawler)
PC3. Perionyx excavatus (Indian Blue)
PC4. Building Types of bins (plastic, wooden, stacked, continuous flow)
PC5. Types of feedstock (fruit and vegetable scraps, coffee grounds, eggshells)
PC6. Feeding schedules and quantities
PC7. Identifying and solving problems (odor, pests, slow composting rates)
PC8. Health indicators of a thriving Vermimanuring system
Knowledge and Understanding (KU)
The individual on the job needs to know and understand:
KU1. Species identification (e.g., Eisenia fetida, Lumbricus rubellus).
KU2. Environmental suitability of different species.
KU3. Decomposition capacity of earthworm types.
KU4. Resilience to environmental stressors.
KU5. Earthworm adaptability to feedstocks.
KU6. Disease resistance among species.
KU7. Role in enhancing soil nutrients.
KU8. Impact on composting efficiency.
KU9. Species-specific maintenance needs.
KU10. Benefits for large-scale composting.

CUTM/AGR/N0905: Value added to Vermimanuring and Preservation
Description
This NOS involves enhancing the value of vermicompost through improved quality, preservation, and
packaging practices. It covers post-production handling to prepare the product for market or long-term
use.
Scope
The scope covers the following :
Focuses on sieving, enrichment, vermiwash collection, packaging, storage, and product grading.
Learners gain essential skills to prepare compost for commercial sale and ensure customer
satisfaction.
Elements and Performance Criteria
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC1. Knowledge about various products produced from vermimanuring for agricultural aspects.
PC2. Vermimanure is a quality source for improve soil nutrient and fertility.
PC3. Vermiwash/Vermifilters as a byproduct aid to improve soil fertility as well as function as a
pesticides.
PC4. Vermiculture is a preserving technique for mass production of various beneficiary earthworm
species for recycling of vermibed formation.
PC5. Understand the end product quality for preservation.
PC6. Knowledge on natural preservative applicable of .vermimanure on green revolution.
PC7. Understanding on packaging and storage methods of value-added vermimanuring products.
PC8. Understand different parameters involved in the preservation (time, temperature, humidity,
water activity)
Knowledge and Understanding (KU)
The individual on the job needs to know and understand:
KU1. Techniques to improve nutrient density in vermicompost.
KU2. Sieving, drying, and granulation processes.
KU3. Vermiwash collection and usage.
KU4. Packaging materials and labeling standards.
KU5. Storage conditions to maintain quality.
KU6. Shelf-life and contamination risks.
KU7. Market grading and pricing standards.
KU8. Branding and local value addition practices.
KU9. Regulatory norms for compost marketing.
