HYC/Q6106: Junior Plumber (City Gas Distribution)
Brief Job Description
Plumbing activities in the oil and gas sector involve specialized tasks that differ significantly from
residential or commercial plumbing due to the industry's unique requirements and challenges. These
activities focus on ensuring the safe and efficient transport and handling of various fluids, including
hydrocarbons, water, and chemical solutions, under high pressure and often in hazardous environments.
The Helper Plumber assists the head plumber in the installation, maintenance, and repair of plumbing
systems. This role involves helping with the assembly, installation, and repair of pipes, fittings, and fixtures
of heating, water, and drainage systems, according to specifications and plumbing codes. The helper also
organises and hands over tools, clears work area, cuts and bends pipes as per the specified dimensions.
The individual also supports the plumber with other tasks as per instructions received.
Personal Attributes
The individual must have a basic knowledge of plumbing systems and materials with an ability to use
plumbing tools and equipment. The helper must be able to perform laborious work, be good at receiving
and following instructions, have physical stamina and ability to work in various environments (e.g.,
confined spaces, outdoor settings).

Compulsory NOS:

HYC/N6114: Carry out helping Activities for Plumbing Work
Description
This unit describes how a helper plumber provides support to the main plumber in carrying out installation,
repair and maintenance work in an oil and gas sector.
Scope
The scope covers the following :
Prepare for plumbing work
Providing help in plumbing work
Elements and Performance Criteria
Prepare for plumbing work
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC1. Ensure that all necessary tools and materials are on-site and organized
PC2. Set up ladders, scaffolding, and workstations as required.
PC3. Transport pipes, fittings, and tools to the work area.
PC4. Unload and organize materials from delivery trucks.
PC5. Understand and follow local building codes and regulations.
PC6. Clear and clean the work area.
PC7. Drill holes in walls, floors, and ceilings to accommodate pipes.
PC8. Use hangers and brackets to secure pipes in place
PC9. Store tools and unused materials neatly.
PC10. Read blueprints and drawings to determine exact specifications and where plumbing system
will be installed.
PC11. Determine water supply networks and waste drainage systems.
PC12. Coordinate with project manager on installation.
PC13. Install systems used to transport water, waste, gases, or liquids.
PC14. Select all materials needed for installation, including type and size of pipe.
PC15. unpack pipes, fittings and fixtures as per manufacturer’s guidelines and safety measures
PC16. check quantity and quality of various plumbing materials and equipment present at the work
site
PC17. Follow safety protocols and wear appropriate personal protective equipment (PPE) such as
gloves, goggles, and helmets.
PC18. Clean the work area after installations or repairs are complete.
PC19. report any shortcomings of supplies against specifications or requirements
Providing help in plumbing work
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC20. follow the instructions received from head plumber or supervisor

HYC/N6115: Maintaining Plumbing Tools and Equipment
Description
This unit deals in detail with the upkeep and maintenance of various plumbing tools and equipment.
Regular maintenance of plumbing tools and equipment not only ensures their longevity and reliability but
also enhances safety and efficiency on the job.
Scope
The scope covers the following :
Perform maintenance work
Elements and Performance Criteria
Perform maintenance work
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC1. Clean and maintain plumbing tools and equipment.
PC2. Report any damaged or malfunctioning tools to the supervising plumber.
PC3. apply suitable treatment method to remove rust and corrosion from damaged tools
PC4. Wipe down tools like wrenches, pliers, and pipe cutters with a clean, dry cloth to remove dirt,
grime, and moisture after each use.
PC5. Clean the exterior of an electrical tools with a damp cloth, avoiding electrical components.
PC6. Use compressed air to blow out dust and debris from vents and moving parts.
PC7. Inspect tools for any visible damage, such as cracks, chips, or worn-out parts
PC8. Perform functionality Test to ensure tools are operating correctly
PC9. Check for smooth operation, proper alignment, and effectiveness.
PC10. Apply appropriate lubricants to moving parts, such as joints and hinges, to ensure smooth
operation and prevent rust.
PC11. Use manufacturer-recommended lubricants for power tools, particularly on parts that
experience friction.
PC12. Perform deep Cleaning for tools that can be taken apart, disassemble them to clean hard-to-
reach areas. Use brushes, cloths, and appropriate cleaning solutions.
PC13. Apply a rust-preventive solution to metal tools to protect against corrosion.
PC14. Sharpen blades, cutters, and other cutting tools using a file, sharpening stone, or an electric
sharpener to maintain their effectiveness
PC15. Check for signs of wear and tear that might not be immediately visible. Pay attention to
handles, grips, and power cords.
PC16. Ensure measuring tools and gauges are accurately calibrated.
PC17. Replace any missing or damaged tools.
PC18. Inspect and clean the motors of power tools, ensuring they are free from dust and debris.
PC19. Replace worn-out brushes and other components.
