SSC/Q0909: Security Operations Centre Analyst
Brief Job Description
The Analyst in the Security Operations Centre is responsible for detecting threats and monitoring events
using Sysinternals tools. They analyze security events, respond to detected threats, and identify and
mitigate cyber-attacks, ensuring robust protection of information systems while collaborating effectively
within the security team.
Personal Attributes
This job may require the individual to work in a team/shifts. The individual should be result oriented and
have a high attention for detail. The individual should also be able to demonstrate good communication
skills and logical thinking with willingness to work in shifts.
Applicable National Occupational Standards (NOS)
Compulsory NOS:
- 1. SSC/N0961: Detect threats and monitor events using Sysinternal Tools
- 2. SSC/N0962: Analize security events and response to detected threats
- 3. SSC/N0963: Identify and response to cyber attack
- 4. DGT/VSQ/N0102: Employability Skills (60 Hours)

SSC/N0961: Detect threats and monitor events using Sysinternal Tools
Description
The OS unit is about using Sysinternal Tools to detect security threats and monitor system events,
ensuring timely identification and response to potential risks.
Scope
The scope covers the following :
Monitor and Analyze Security Threats in ICT Systems
Threat Monitoring and Incident Analysis
Threat Detection and Log Analysis for Cybersecurity Operations
Security Operations Monitoring and Incident Management
Elements and Performance Criteria
Monitor and Analyze Security Threats in ICT Systems
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC1. confirm the range of information assets and system components to be monitored in
collaboration with authorized personnel
PC2. employs designated monitoring and data collection techniques and tools in accordance with
organizational procedures and policies
PC3. oversee the organization’s traffic and logs generated by ICT systems using various security
technologies to identify security threats and assess the health of the ICT systems
PC4. oversee external data sources such as Computer Network Defense (CND) vendor websites,
Computer Emergency Response Teams, SANS, and Security Focus
PC5. identify security issues that could affect the enterprise
PC6. perform detailed email analysis to identify phishing attempts by reviewing suspicious
elements such as email addresses, links, or domain names, ensuring alignment with
legitimate sources
PC7. analyze unexpected attachments or links in emails to detect and prevent potential malware
downloads, utilizing phishing detection techniques to safeguard the organization's network
PC8. verify sender authenticity by cross-checking the sender's email address against the
organization it claims to represent, applying phishing analysis methods to prevent fraudulent
emails from compromising security
Threat Monitoring and Incident Analysis
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC9. use Sysinternals tools (such as Process Explorer, Autoruns, and TCPView) to identify and
examine potential malware or suspicious processes on the network
PC10. utilize Sysinternal tools (such as Process Explorer, Autoruns, and TCPView) for thorough
system investigations to detect unauthorized changes or applications that may impact
security.
PC11. gather logs from all types of ICT systems, devices, and applications as mandated by the
organization

PC12. maintain continuous monitoring of all critical infrastructure, including servers, cloud
workloads, applications, and networks, to identify signs of intrusion or suspicious activity
around the clock
PC13. employ SIEM (Security Information and Event Management) or XDR (Extended Detection and
Response) tools to consolidate alerts, examine telemetry data, and identify threats in real-
time
PC14. analyze trends and patterns utilizing the SIEM tool
PC15. collaborate with enterprise-wide Computer Network Defense (CND) teams to verify network
alerts
PC16. conduct event correlation using collected information to enhance situational awareness and
assess the potential threat level
Threat Detection and Log Analysis for Cybersecurity Operations
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC17. conduct a thorough analysis of log data gathered from different network events to identify
anomalies
PC18. recognize standard activities and detect anomalies that suggest suspicious behavior
PC19. support the identification of real cyber threats by filtering out false positives and classifying
threats based on their severity to ensure prompt action
PC20. utilize AI-powered SIEM tools to enhance the precision of threat detection progressively.
PC21. prioritize the service request according to organizational procedures and policies
PC22. obtain help or advice from specialist if the problem is outside his/her area of competence or
experience
Security Operations Monitoring and Incident Management
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC23. report the results of the monitoring, ticket raising and ticket closure activities using standard
documentation following organisational procedures
PC24. participate in 24/7 security operations centre shift schedule
PC25. receive shift handover along with relevant information, authorities and instructions
PC26. comply with relevant legislation, standards, policies and procedures
PC27. maintain a knowledge base of the known problems
PC28. use escalation matrix for unresolved tickets within agreed turnaround times
Knowledge and Understanding (KU)
The individual on the job needs to know and understand:
KU1. The principles of Security Information and Event Management (SIEM) and Extended Detection
and Response (XDR) tools, including their role in consolidating alerts and identifying threats in
real-time.
KU2. Various cyber threats, including phishing, malware, ransomware, and insider threats, and the
methodologies used to detect, analyze, and mitigate them.
KU3. Log analysis techniques and how to gather, interpret, and correlate logs from different ICT
systems, applications, and network events to detect anomalies.

KU4. The fundamentals of Computer Network Defense (CND), including the importance of external
threat intelligence sources such as CERT, SANS, and Security Focus.
KU5. The importance of AI-powered security tools in enhancing the accuracy of threat detection
and how they improve over time through continuous learning.
KU6. The organizational policies, regulations, and compliance frameworks (such as GDPR, NIST, and
ISO 27001) that govern cybersecurity monitoring and incident response.
KU7. The processes and best practices for conducting email security analysis, including identifying
phishing attempts, verifying sender authenticity, and analyzing malicious attachments.
KU8. The procedures for security operations center (SOC) shift management, including shift
handover protocols, documentation standards, and escalation matrix guidelines.
Generic Skills (GS)
User/individual on the job needs to know how to:
GS1. Utilize SIEM, XDR, and threat intelligence platforms to conduct real-time monitoring and threat
detection, filtering out false positives while identifying real security threats.
GS2. Analyze logs and telemetry data to detect abnormal patterns, correlate security events, and
assess the level of potential cyber threats to the organization.
GS3. Investigate phishing emails by analyzing links, domain names, and attachments, verifying
sender authenticity, and implementing measures to prevent fraudulent email attacks.
GS4. Use Sysinternals tools (such as Process Explorer, Autoruns, and TCPView) to identify
suspicious processes, unauthorized applications, and security vulnerabilities in ICT systems.
GS5. Prioritize and classify cybersecurity incidents based on severity, urgency, and business
impact, ensuring timely response and mitigation actions.
GS6. Follow established escalation protocols to report security incidents, raise service requests, and
allocate tickets to the appropriate teams for resolution.
GS7. Maintain detailed documentation of security monitoring activities, including incident reports,
ticket closure records, and known threat intelligence updates.
GS8. Collaborate effectively with enterprise-wide CND teams, IT personnel, and security specialists
to ensure a coordinated response to detected threats and cyber incidents.

SSC/N0962: Analize security events and response to detected threats
Description
The OS unit is about analyzing security events and responding to detected threats promptly to mitigate
risks and ensure the protection of digital assets.
Scope
The scope covers the following :
Security Monitoring and Vulnerability Management
Cyber Threat Detection and Incident Response
Incident Response and Security Monitoring
Elements and Performance Criteria
Security Monitoring and Vulnerability Management
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC1. receive and evaluate alarms and alerts from multiple sources across the enterprise,
identifying potential causes and determining the underlying issues behind the alerts
PC2. maintain up-to-date inventories of all IT assets, including applications, databases, servers,
cloud services, and endpoints, to ensure comprehensive protection and security
PC3. utilize asset discovery tools (Cacti, IP Scanning, etc.) to monitor and track changes, identify
vulnerabilities, and assess potential security risks within the IT infrastructure
PC4. regularly implement patches, upgrades, and updates for security tools, including firewalls,
antivirus, and monitoring software, to ensure continuous protection and optimal functioning
of the Security Operations Centre (SOC)
PC5. contribute to the development of backup policies and procedures, ensuring data availability
and integrity to support business continuity and disaster recovery efforts
PC6. conduct vulnerability assessments to identify potential weaknesses across IT resources,
ensuring comprehensive analysis and reporting to enhance the organization's security
posture
PC7. determine which information assets and system components could be affected by identified
security incidents
PC8. analyze detected malicious activities to assess exploited vulnerabilities, methods of
exploitation, and their impact on systems and data
Cyber Threat Detection and Incident Response
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC9. analyze log files from multiple sources to detect potential threats to network security
PC10. conduct triage for computer network defense (CND) incidents, which involves evaluating the
scope, urgency, and potential impact of each incident
PC11. conduct and assist in penetration testing activities to simulate cyberattacks, identifying
vulnerabilities and strengthening the organization's security posture

vulnerabilities and monitoring changes in IT infrastructure.

to simulate cyberattacks to identify system weaknesses.
KU4. The methodologies for analyzing security alerts, logs, and malicious activities to determine
the root cause, methods of exploitation, and the potential impact on information assets.
KU5. Organizational incident management protocols, including classification, prioritization,
escalation, and closure of security incidents in compliance with standard operating procedures
(SOPs).
KU6. Techniques for collecting, preserving, and securing digital evidence related to security
incidents, ensuring compliance with organizational and legal requirements.

SSC/N0963: Identify and response to cyber attack
Description
The OS unit is about detecting potential cyber threats and responding promptly to mitigate risks and
ensure system security.
Scope
The scope covers the following :
Threat Detection, Incident Analysis, and Response
Incident Containment, Mitigation, and Recovery
Elements and Performance Criteria
Threat Detection, Incident Analysis, and Response
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC1. utilize advanced security tools (Wireshark, KisMAC, NetStumbler, etc.) to monitor network
traffic, system logs, and user behavior continuously for signs of suspicious activity
PC2. leverage threat intelligence feeds to remain informed about emerging threats and attack
patterns
PC3. employ Security Information and Event Management (SIEM) solutions to aggregate and
correlate security events from multiple sources to identify potential incidents
PC4. apply algorithms for detecting deviations from normal behavior, including unusual network
traffic or unauthorized access attempts
PC5. correlate alerts from various sources when suspicious activities are detected to determine
potential incidents
PC6. categorize incidents based on severity, impact, and type (e.g., data breach, malware
infection, denial-of-service attack)
PC7. take immediate containment measures, such as isolating affected systems or disabling
compromised accounts
PC8. perform thorough investigations to understand the root cause of attacks, identify attacker
methods, and assess damage extent
Incident Containment, Mitigation, and Recovery
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC9. develop a detailed timeline of events to reconstruct the attack sequence
PC10. isolate affected systems or networks to prevent further spread of the attack
PC11. identify and remove malicious software from infected systems effectively
PC12. patch known vulnerabilities to prevent future attacks
PC13. use backup procedures to restore lost or corrupted data
PC14. document the incident response process for future reference
Knowledge and Understanding (KU)

Assessment Guidelines and Assessment Weightage
Assessment Guidelines
- 1. Criteria for assessment for each Qualification Pack will be created by the Sector Skill Council. Each
Performance Criteria (PC) will be assigned marks proportional to its importance in NOS. SSC will also lay
down proportion of marks for Theory and Skills Practical for each PC.
- 2. The assessment for the theory part will be based on knowledge bank of questions created by the SSC.
- 3. Individual assessment agencies will create unique question papers for theory part for each candidate at
each examination/training center (as per assessment criteria below).
- 4. Individual assessment agencies will create unique evaluations for skill practical for every student at
each examination/ training center based on these criteria.
- 5. In case of successfully passing only certain number of NOSs, the trainee is eligible to take subsequent
assessment on the balance NOS's to pass the Qualification Pack.
- 6. In case of unsuccessful completion, the trainee may seek reassessment on the Qualification Pack.
- 7. Candidate needs to achieve 70% or more for successfully passing the QP.
