IAC/ASC/Q2501: Technician - Registered Vehicle Scrapping Facility (RVSF)
Brief Job Description
This course provides participants with the necessary knowledge and skills for safe and responsible end-of-
life vehicle (ELV) de-pollution and dismantling. It covers topics such as safety, hazards and risks associated
with vehicle dismantling, ELV guidance for waste handling, personal protective equipment training,
personnel safety, Tools, and equipment. The course aims to ensure compliance with regulations and
promote environmental responsibility in the automotive industry.
Personal Attributes
Basic report generation skills and ability to follow guidelines for scrappage processes. Effective
communication skills to interact with team members.
Applicable National Occupational Standards (NOS)
Compulsory NOS:
- 1. IAC/ASC/N2501: An introduction to End-of-Life Vehicles (ELVs)
- 2. IAC/ASC/N2502: Safe Working Practices

IAC/ASC/N2501: An introduction to End-of-Life Vehicles (ELVs)
Description
An introduction to End-of-Life Vehicles (ELVs) and their effects on the environment
Scope
The scope covers the following :
Learners will be aware about End-of-Life Vehicles (ELVs) and their significance in terms of legal,
sustainability, recycling, and responsible disposal.
Elements and Performance Criteria
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC1. Acquire a holistic understanding of the automotive industry, including its historical evolution,
market segmentation, and technological advancements.
PC2. Awareness about End-of-Life Vehicles (ELVs) and their significance in terms of legal,
sustainability, recycling, and responsible disposal.
PC3. Recognize and navigate the complex stakeholder ecosystem in the ELV industry, fostering
effective relationships with customers, suppliers, and competitors to drive sustainable
practices and compliance.
Knowledge and Understanding (KU)
The individual on the job needs to know and understand:
KU1. Understanding the automobile industry.
KU2. Understanding End-of-Life Vehicles (ELVs) in the Automotive Industry.
KU3. Legislation, regulations, and policies governing ELVs.
KU4. Roles and Responsibilities within Recycling and Vehicle Salvage Facilities (RVSF).
KU5. Global scope of ELV management and its implications on different regions.
KU6. Understanding the various Stakeholder Ecosystem in ELV including customers, suppliers, and
competitors.

Recognize and comply with safe working practices, environment regulations, and housekeeping in the
Registered Vehicle Scrapping Facility

Recognize & comply with safe working practices, environment regulation and housekeeping in

prevention.

IAC/ASC/N2503: Vehicle Scrapping Process
Description
General Vehicle Scrapping Process
Scope
The scope covers the following :
General Vehicle Scrapping Process
Elements and Performance Criteria
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC1. Identify the comprehensive range of tools in an essential ELV toolkit, ensuring efficient
operations.
PC2. Apply critical factors to select suitable tools, adhere to safety protocols, follow guidelines, and
manage tools effectively for safe and responsible ELV operations.
PC3. Understanding of vehicle components, assemblies, and subassemblies, focusing on practical
skills for efficient vehicle dismantling. This includes hands-on training in the proper usage of
tools and equipment, safe handling of hazardous materials during dismantling, and an
awareness of associated risks.
PC4. Essentials of record-keeping, distinguishing between acceptable and unacceptable materials,
and mastering proper material handling techniques.
Knowledge and Understanding (KU)
The individual on the job needs to know and understand:
KU1. Adhere to standard operating procedures, particularly those outlined in ELV management
guidelines and manuals, when utilizing workshop tools and equipment for ELV-related tasks.
KU2. Identifying end-of-life vehicles, understanding vehicle documentation & technical data.
KU3. Recognizing the various components and subassemblies present in ELVs.
KU4. Ability to transfer information to connected authorities.
KU5. Awareness of safety protocols when handling hazardous components and fluids.
KU6. Adherence to government norms for the proper drainage and collection of fluids, including
oil, coolant, brake fluid, refrigerants, and fuel.
KU7. Handling unforeseen incidents such as battery damage, fluid leakage, insufficient storage
capacity, equipment malfunction, and contact with hazardous substances.
KU8. Practicing safety protocols during the dismantling process.
KU9. Implementing the concept of labeling & tagging.
KU10. Applying the concept of re-use, re-manufacture, or recycling.
KU11. Contributing to optimum resource utilization and reduction of wastage through process
improvements, Kaizens, Poka Yoke, etc
