SGJ/Q6901: Soil Pollution Monitoring Specialist
Brief Job Description
The Soil Pollution Monitoring Specialist monitors and manages soil health by analyzing composition,
quality, and pollution levels. The role includes soil classification, sampling, and data analysis using
statistical and GIS tools. Specialists also develop pollution mitigation strategies and promote advanced
technologies for sustainable soil and environmental management.
Personal Attributes
Analytical, detail-oriented, adaptable, environmentally conscious, collaborative, and tech-savvy.
Applicable National Occupational Standards (NOS)
Compulsory NOS:
- 1. SGJ/N6901: Introduce Soil Composition, Quality, and Health Implications
- 2. SGJ/N6902: Assess and Classify Soil Properties

SGJ/N6901: Introduce Soil Composition, Quality, and Health Implications
Description
It provides essential knowledge and tools to evaluate soil health, identify contaminants, assess ecological
and human health risks, and implement effective soil conservation strategies.
Scope
The scope covers the following :
This focuses on the Earth lithosphere, soil formation, classification, pollution, risk assessment, and
sustainable soil management.
Elements and Performance Criteria
Introduction to Soil, Pollution, Quality and relation to human.
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC1. Describe the structure and composition of the Earth’s lithosphere.
PC2. Explain the processes involved in forming and transforming rocks and minerals
PC3. Identify different types of rocks and their properties.
PC4. Describe the factors influencing soil formation (parent material, climate, organisms,
topography, time).
PC5. Explain the processes of soil development (weathering, leaching, accumulation, etc.).
PC6. Identify various sources of soil pollution (industrial, agricultural, urban) and recognize
different types of pollutants (heavy metals, pesticides, organic contaminants).
PC7. Understand the adverse effects of soil pollution on soil health, ecosystem function, food
safety, and human health.
PC8. Describe the processes governing the movement and transformation of pollutants in soil,
including adsorption, leaching, and biodegradation
PC9. Identify different soil types and their characteristics.
PC10. Identify different types of soil pollutants (heavy metals, organic contaminants, pesticides,
etc.) and their sources.
PC11. Describe the key components of an ecological risk assessment (problem formulation,
exposure assessment, effects assessment, risk characterization).
PC12. Evaluate the potential adverse effects of soil pollution on living organisms.
PC13. Characterize the ecological risks and develop risk management strategies.
PC14. Define soil quality and identify key physical, chemical, and biological indicators used to
assess soil health and function.
PC15. Understand the causes and consequences of soil degradation, including erosion, compaction,
nutrient depletion, and salinization.
PC16. Learn methods for monitoring soil quality, interpreting data, and making informed
management decisions.

SGJ/N6902: Assess and Classify Soil Properties
Description
This integrates soil texture, structure, nutrients, organic matter, microbial activity, and advanced
analytical techniques to evaluate soil quality, support crop productivity, and guide sustainable land
management.
Scope
The scope covers the following :
To comprehensively study soil's physical, chemical, and biological properties, processes, and
analysis methods to assess soil health, fertility, and its role in sustainable plant growth and
environmental management.
Elements and Performance Criteria
Soil Properties
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC1. Understand the concept of soil texture, its classification based on particle size distribution
(sand, silt, clay), and its influence on other soil properties and functions.
PC2. Learn about soil structure, the formation and types of soil aggregates, their role in soil
porosity, water infiltration, and root growth.
PC3. Grasp the concept of soil water holding capacity, factors affecting it (texture, structure,
organic matter), and its importance for plant growth and water management.
PC4. Explore additional physical properties such as soil bulk density, particle density, porosity, soil
temperature, and colour, and their implications for soil management and plant growth.
PC5. Understand the role of macro (N, P, K, Ca, Mg, S) & micronutrients (Fe, Mn, Zn, Cu, B, Mo, Cl)
in plant growth and soil health.
PC6. Learn about the composition, decomposition, and functions of soil organic matter and its
significance as a carbon sink.
PC7. Grasp the concepts of soil pH, salinity, and electrical conductivity and their influence on
nutrient availability, plant growth, and soil health.
PC8. Identify common heavy metals and toxic ions in soil, their sources, and their potential
adverse effects on ecosystems and human health.
PC9. Understand the diversity of soil organisms (bacteria, fungi, etc.) and their crucial roles in
nutrient cycling, decomposition, and soil structure formation.
PC10. Learn about the types and functions of soil enzymes (oxidoreductases, hydrolases,
transferases, etc.) and their significance as indicators of soil biological activity and health.
PC11. Metagenomics and Meta transcriptomics: Understand the principles and applications of
metagenomics and meta transcriptomics data in studying soil microbial communities and
their functional potential
PC12. Conduct laboratory tests (e.g., hydrometer method, feel method) to determine soil texture
and classify soil samples.

KU4. Understand chemical properties like pH, salinity, and electrical conductivity and their
influence on nutrient availability and plant performance.
KU5. Recognize the role of soil biology, including microorganisms and enzymes, in nutrient cycling,
decomposition, and ecosystem functioning.
KU6. Acquire skills in laboratory and field techniques for analyzing soil properties, nutrient content,
contaminants, and microbial community structure.
