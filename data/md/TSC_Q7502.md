TSC/Q7502: Jacquard Harness Builder
Brief Job Description
Builds the structure of the harness of handloom jacquard weaving and mounts it on the loom as per as per
the loom fabric and design specifications
Personal Attributes
Needs to have an eye for detail, motor skills, vision (including near, distance, colour, and peripheral),
depth perception, alert mind and calculative. The harness builder also needs to understand the psychology
of the designers, card puncher and the weavers.
Applicable National Occupational Standards (NOS)

- 1. TSC/N7502: Build harness for handloom jacquard weaving

TSC/N7502: Build harness for handloom jacquard weaving
Description
This unit refers to the necessary skills, knowledge and personality required by jacquard harness builder to
build a harness for handloom jacquard weaving.
Scope
The scope covers the following :
Ensure proper alignment of the hooks
Build jacquard harness
Elements and Performance Criteria
Ensure proper alignment of the hooks
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
- 1. place the jacquard machine in relation to the loom with card cylinder either on the sides or in
the front or back
- 2. ensure that the long rows of the hooks are at right angle or in parallel to the length of the
comber-board when the card cylinder is on the sides or in front/back of the loom respectively
- 3. cross the harness cords with each other when passing from the neck-cords to the holes in the
comber-board if the card cylinder is on the sides of the loom London/crossed or quarter twist tie
- 4. check the harness cords do not cross each other when the card cylinder is in front/back of the
loomNorwich or straight tie
Build jacquard harness
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
- 5. identify the hook in the row nearest to the head of the cylinder as the first hook
- 6. distinguish between the types of tie as per the design specificationssingle tie and lay-over or
repeat tie
- 7. check the hooks are tied to the harness cords as per the jacquard designs
- 8. ensure the width of the comber-board is as per quality
- 9. draw the warp threads through the harness mail eyes
- 10. Place the numbered ends of the laced jacquard cards at the right side of the cylinder for
rotation in serial order and design specifications
- 11. calculate the number of harness mails per unit area
- 12. dent the warp in the reed sometimes, e.g. 200 threads, 4 split and 200 threads, 2 split
- 13. maintain uniform distribution of the harness cord on the comber-board
- 14. calculate the number of harness cord per unit length
- 15. calculate the number of harness cords to each hook
- 16. cast-out by leaving empty a portion of the mails in each repeat of the harness
- 17. ensure that the wrap threads should occupy the same width in the harness as in the reed
- 18. check the set of harness does not change when tied up

Knowledge and Understanding (KU)
The individual on the job needs to know and understand:
KU1. legislation, standards, policies, and procedures followed in the work place relevant to own
employment and performance conditions
KU2. relevant health and safety requirements applicable in the work place
KU3. importance of following health, hygiene, safety and quality standards and the impact of not
following the standards on consumers and the business
KU4. own job role and responsibilities and sources for information pertaining to employment
terms, compensation, job role and responsibilities
KU5. relevant people and their responsibilities within the work area
KU6. various categories of people that one is required to communicate and coordinate within the
organization
KU7. types of tie, e.g. single tie, lay-over or repeating tie
KU8. different parts of the handloom warp beam, rods, heddles, harnesses or shafts, treadles,
reed, lathe, etc.
KU9. different types of fabrics, yarn, count, twist, etc.
KU10. loom dimensions and other specifications
KU11. jacquard cards and jacquard machines
KU12. technical terms associated with all harness operations
KU13. types of tools and equipments usedshuttles, pirns, harness eye, harness cord, hooks, etc.
KU14. process flow involved in the harness building operations
KU15. common hazards in the work area and work place procedures for dealing with them
KU16. valid sources of receiving the harness building order, e.g. employer (with valid appointment
letter or service agreement), contractor (with valid contract), client (with signed job order
and advance payment as agreed), etc.
KU17. appropriate inspection methods that can be used
KU18. calculation of the number of harness mails per unit area is by the rate at which the rows of
the holes are formed in the comber-board and the number of holes in each row, e.g. for eight
row machine, the wrap threads are drawn through harness mails in corresponding order1 to
8 on the odd mails, 9 to 16 on even mails and so on
KU19. calculation of the number of harness cord per unit length is by multiplying the number of
rows per unit length with the number of holes per row
KU20. calculation of the number of harness cords to each hook is by multiplying the width and set
of the harness per cm and then dividing that with the number of hooks
KU21. cast-out by leaving empty a portion of the mails in each repeat of the harness and allowing
the corresponding needles, hooks and harness cords to remain idle

TSC/N9005: Maintain the work area, tools and machines
Description
This unit provides performance criteria, knowledge, understanding, skills and abilities required to
organise/maintain work areas and activities to ensure tools used for warping are maintained as per norms
Scope
The scope covers the following :
Maintain the work area, tools and machines
Elements and Performance Criteria
Maintain the work area, tools and machines
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
- 1. Handle materials and tools safely and correctly
- 2. Use correct lifting and handling procedures
- 3. Use materials in a manner to minimize waste
- 4. Maintain a clean and hazard free working area
- 5. Maintain the tools and equipment used for warping
- 6. Carry out maintenance and/or cleaning within ones responsibility
- 7. Identify damaged tools and materials and take action according to the standards followed
- 8. Ensure that the correct tools and yarn required are in place
- 9. Work with the correct posture
- 10. Use cleaning equipment and methods appropriate for the work to be carried out
