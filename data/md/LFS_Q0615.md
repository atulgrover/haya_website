#### LFS/Q0615: Chemist - Retail Pharmacy
Brief Job Description
Chemist - Retail Pharmacy is responsible for supporting pharmacy operations by ensuring valid prescription processing, accurate order fulfilment, and effective customer interaction in compliance with
regulatory standards. The role involves managing sales transactions, maintaining proper drug storage with
cold chain and expiry monitoring, and handling inventory efficiently. The job holder is also expected to
uphold the pharmacy professional image, promote responsible use of medicines, and contribute to
pharmacovigilance and safe handling of controlled substances, while understanding the basics of retail
operations within the Life Sciences industry.
Personal Attributes
The individual should have knowledge of different materials and chemicals along with the storing and
selling appropriate pharma product. He must be able to identify defects, select appropriate storage areas,
and handle inventory/stock. He should have the ability to do quality assurance, inventory control,
manufacturing dispensing .
Applicable National Occupational Standards (NOS)

Expiry Management, and Cold Chain Monitoring

fulfilment as per Regulatory Standards
5. LFS/N0660: Processing the sales transactions in cash or credit at point of sale

Handling of Controlled Substances

#### LFS/N0674: Introduction to Life Sciences sector and basics of retail
pharmacy
Description
This NOS is about introduction to Life Sciences sector and basics of retail pharmacy
Scope
The scope covers the following :
Introduction to Life Sciences Sector
Basic of retail Pharmacy
Environment Sustainability
#### Elements and Performance Criteria
Introduction to Life Sciences Sector
#### To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
- PC1. describe the key sectors and value chain of the Life Sciences industry in Indian and global
context.
- PC2. list applicable regulations and good practices such as GMP, GLP, and GDP relevant to
pharmacy operations.
- PC3. list key responsibilities and duties of a Chemist Retail Pharmacist.
Basic of retail Pharmacy
#### To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
- PC4. Explain the difference between OTC, prescription, controlled, and Schedule H/X drugs.
- PC5. identify brand name, generic name, expiry date, and batch number from packaging
- PC6. interpret basic components of a prescription including dosage form, frequency, and route of
administration.
- PC7. demonstrate correct storage of drugs as per label instructions and regulatory norms.
- PC8. respond appropriately to scenarios such as prescription denial or customer confusion
Environment Sustainability
#### To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
- PC9. list energy conservation practices relevant to pharmacy operations.
- PC10. identify actions that optimize electricity and equipment usage during daily operations
- PC11. follow practices that reduce water wastage, chemical spills, and air pollution
- PC12. segregate waste into recyclable, non-recyclable, and hazardous categories
- PC13. use appropriate color-coded containers for waste collection and disposal
- PC14. prepare and follow a waste management checklist for daily pharmacy operations

#### LFS/N0661: Perform Inventory Management   and Replenishment  in a
Retail Pharmacy
Description
This NOS outlines the procedures and responsibilities involved in managing inventory at a retail pharmacy,
including stock monitoring, buffer stock planning, expiry tracking, replenishment , documentation, safety
to ensure uninterrupted pharmacy operations.
Scope
The scope covers the following :
Inventory Monitoring and Reorder Level Setting
Stock Replenishment and Buffer Stock Management
Documentation and Safety Compliance
#### Elements and Performance Criteria
Inventory Monitoring and Reorder Level Setting
#### To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
- PC1. Use stock control software or tools to assess current inventory levels, set reorder thresholds,
and identify gaps against minimum stock requirements
- PC2. Track product consumption patterns using historical sales data and seasonal trends to
anticipate demand fluctuations.
- PC3. Review and adjust reorder levels periodically based on updated consumption data, stock
movement frequency, and shelf life.
- PC4. Monitor expiry dates regularly and ensure timely removal of near-expiry or unsaleable stock;
update records accordingly.
- PC5. Flag low-stock alerts early to ensure replenishment without stockouts or delays in patient
service.
Stock Replenishment and Buffer Stock Management
#### To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
- PC6. Maintain buffer stock for fast-moving and life-saving medications, guided by company policy
and past sales records.
- PC7. Raise purchase requisitions based on reorder level breaches, buffer stock depletion, and
supplier lead times.
- PC8. Coordinate with approved vendors or suppliers to ensure timely delivery and maintain
continuity of inventory.
- PC9. Cross-check incoming stock against invoices, batch numbers, expiry dates, and purchase
orders before updating inventory records.
Documentation and Safety Compliance
#### To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
- PC10. Handle stock safely to avoid product damage, contamination, or disruption to daily
operations.

#### LFS/N0668: Maintain Regulatory-Compliant Drug  Storage Through
Systematic Layout Planning, Expiry Management, and  Cold Chain
Monitoring
Description
This NOS focuses on maintaining proper drug storage in compliance with regulatory norms by planning
shelf layouts, implementing expiry management systems (FIFO/FEFO), and ensuring temperature control
for sensitive products.
Scope
The scope covers the following :
Organize Storage Space Using Approved Shelf Layouts
Implement FIFO/FEFO for Efficient Inventory Rotation
Monitor and Document Temperature for Sensitive Stock
#### Elements and Performance Criteria
Organize Storage Space Using Approved Shelf Layouts
#### To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
- PC1. Arrange drug products as per defined shelf layout plans, ensuring proper segregation based
on dosage forms, therapeutic classes, and storage conditions.
- PC2. Label all shelves clearly to indicate drug category, storage temperature range, and hazard
classifications where applicable.
- PC3. Ensure restricted-access drugs (e.g., narcotics, psychotropics) are stored in designated,
locked sections of the storage area.
- PC4. Maintain adequate spacing between stock units to allow airflow and prevent cross-
contamination.
- PC5. Periodically review and update shelf layouts to accommodate new inventory, expired goods
removal, or revised compliance guidelines.
Implement FIFO/FEFO for Efficient Inventory Rotation
#### To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
- PC6. Apply First-In-First-Out (FIFO) for non-perishable or general drug products to ensure older
stock is dispensed before newer stock.
- PC7. Follow First-Expiry-First-Out (FEFO) for drugs with short shelf lives, ensuring products nearing
expiry are prioritized for use or flagged.
- PC8. Check and document batch numbers and expiry dates during every inward entry and
physical stock audit.
- PC9. Train staff on visual identification of expiry risk items and assign responsibility for regular
expiry monitoring.
- PC10. Ensure expired or short-expiry goods are separated immediately and marked for disposal as
per SOPs.
Monitor and Document Temperature for Sensitive Stock

#### LFS/N0664: Ensure valid prescription processing, customer need
assessment, and accurate order fulfilment as per Regulatory Standards
Description
This NOS outlines the skills required to validate prescriptions, assess customer needs, and fulfill orders
accurately while complying with pharmacy regulations.
Scope
The scope covers the following :
Prescription Validation and Customer Need Assessment
Schedule Drug Handling and Regulatory Compliance
E-Order Processing and Fulfilment
Compliance Documentation and Data Security
Operations During Constrained/Epidemic Situation
#### Elements and Performance Criteria
Prescription Validation and Customer Need Assessment
#### To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
- PC1. identify customer needs by asking relevant and compliant questions regarding symptoms or
prescription requirements.
- PC2. identify the goods that will meet customers needs and check with customers that these are
satisfactory.
- PC3. Verify the validity of prescriptions, including the prescriber’s details, drug schedule category,
date, dosage, and duration, as per applicable legal standards.
- PC4. Ensure that the prescribed drugs are permitted for dispensing and are not expired, forged, or
falling under restricted supply without proper authorization.
- PC5. Give customers clear, accurate and complete information about the availability of goods and
the terms of supply.
Schedule Drug Handling and Regulatory Compliance
#### To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
- PC6. Identify whether requested drugs fall under Schedule H, H1, X, or other restricted categories
and follow proper protocols before processing.
- PC7. Maintain documentation such as prescription copies and pharmacist's verification records for
scheduled drugs, as per statutory norms.
- PC8. prepare accurate, clear and complete information about the order & pass this information to
