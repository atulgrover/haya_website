#### HSS/Q3006: Skills Competition Health & Social Care
Brief Job Description
A health and social care practitioner offers a range of support to individual clients and their family and has
a continuing responsibility to work professionally and interactively with the client in order to ensure their
holistic care needs are met. Whilst the health and social care practitioner may work directly for the client
they are normally employed by an organization within the health and social care sector. Health and social
care is closely associated with the medical profession.
Personal Attributes
Work organization and self-management, communication and interpersonal skills, problem solving,
innovation and creativity, the ability to understand, empathize and work with clients to improve the quality
of their life, are the universal attributes of the outstanding practitioner.
Applicable National Occupational Standards (NOS)

#### HSS/N3036: Coordinate for Work organization and management
Description
Work organization and management
Scope
The scope covers the following :
The individual needs to know and understand Health, safety, environmental and hygiene legislation,
obligations, regulations, and documentation
#### Elements and Performance Criteria
Work organization and management
#### To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
- PC1. Follow health, safety, and hygiene standards, rules, and regulations
- PC2. Take appropriate hygiene precautions for infection prevention
- PC3. • Identify and use the appropriate uniform/personal protective clothing
• including safe footwear
- PC4. Select, use effectively/efficiently, and store materials safely
- PC5. • Maintain safe and secure storage of medication in accordance with
• regulations
- PC6. Plan, schedule, and re-prioritize work as the need arises
- PC7. Ensure safe and ergonomic working practices
- PC8. Discard waste ecologically
- PC9. Work together in an efficient way with other practitioners and any person
- PC10. • Keep up-to-date with new practices and regulations e.g. safe moving and
• health and safety

- KU1. • Health, safety, environmental and hygiene legislation, obligations,
• regulations, and documentation

- KU3. The purpose of a uniform/personal protective clothing
- KU4. The purpose, safe use, care, and storage of materials
- KU5. Regulations regarding the safety and security of medication
- KU6. Techniques of time management
- KU7. The principles of ergonomics

#### HSS/N3038: Align for Problem solving, innovation, and creativity
Description
Problem solving, innovation, and creativity
Scope
The scope covers the following :
The individual needs to know and understand the common types of situations which can occur within
health and social care work
#### Elements and Performance Criteria
Problem solving, innovation, and creativity
#### To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
- PC1. • Value the individual, take a strong interest and gain a deep understanding
• of their character
- PC2. The difference between symptoms and causes of problems
- PC3. Gain the confidence of clients to discuss their problem(s)
- PC4. • Recognize problems swiftly and follow a self-managed process for
• resolving
- PC5. • Determine the root cause(s) of clients’ problem(s) through careful and
• structured discussion/questioning/observation
- PC6. Pro-actively re-prioritize clients’ problem(s) as situations demand
- PC7. • Recognize the boundaries of own expertise/authority in dealing with
• clients’ problem(s) and refer to colleagues and professionals as appropriate
- PC8. • Create, develop, and negotiate ‘new safe ways of working’ to improve the
• daily life of clients and their happiness, e.g. use of aids to support mobility
- PC9. • Recognize opportunities and pro-actively contribute ideas to improve client
• care, e.g. through a new way to use the client’s environment effectively,
• through discussions with the client at the right time
#### Knowledge and Understanding (KU)
The individual on the job needs to know and understand:
- KU1. The difference between symptoms and causes of problem
- KU2. • Why individuals may be not willing to discuss problems e.g. client and • family members, and techniques for addressing
- KU3. • The value of working with clients and respecting their wishes in resolving
• problems
- KU4. • The importance of observing clients closely and consistently to identify any

#### HSS/N3039: Assessing needs and planning client care
Description
Assessing needs and planning client care
Scope
The scope covers the following :
Principles of growth and development across the lifespan and the range of illnesses and the relevant
treatments
#### Elements and Performance Criteria
Assessing needs and planning client care
#### To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
- PC1. • Carefully assess the clients’ environments and situations to accurately
• determine care needs, recognizing boundaries of role
- PC2. Assess the clients’ capabilities and the capabilities of families
- PC3. Identify clients’ nutritional status and requirements

- PC5. Secure required resources to facilitate client care plans
- PC6. Plan how to support client rehabilitation
- PC7. Obtain consent before the delivery of care
- PC8. Refer to medical professionals as appropriate
#### Knowledge and Understanding (KU)
The individual on the job needs to know and understand:
- KU1. Principles of growth and development across the lifespan
- KU2. The range of illnesses and the relevant treatments
- KU3. • Health, social care, and rehabilitation of clients holistically across the • lifespan in a variety of daily life situations
- KU4. Ethics and law with respect to rights, discrimination, and abuse
- KU5. • Techniques for working with clients and their families to accurately
• determine the care needs of the client
- KU6. The role of nutrition and special diets
- KU7. Techniques to assess the capabilities of the patient and the family

#### HSS/N3040: Managing and  delivering client care
Description
Managing and delivering client care
Scope
The scope covers the following :
The holistic needs of clients and inclusion of capabilities
#### Elements and Performance Criteria
Managing and delivering client care
#### To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
- PC1. • Promote and assist with physical, social, and psychological well-being, s
• growth and development, caring and rehabilitation
- PC2. Respect clients and the principles of law and ethics in the delivery of care
- PC3. Create a positive environment for clients and deliver safe care
- PC4. Assist with hygiene needs as required and respect clients’ need for intimacy
- PC5. Include patients` capabilities in the delivery of care
- PC6. Use culturally appropriate measures
- PC7. • Perform medical tasks within the scope of practice e.g. first aid, wound
• care, breathing exercises
- PC8. • • Monitor different health parameters e.g. blood pressure, pulse,
• temperature, blood sugar, pain, and weight and give information about
• them
- PC9. • Take precautions for risks which are common in clients who are sick, e.g.
• pressure ulcers, pneumonia, and contractions
- PC10. • Consistently observe clients and quickly identify any new problems that
• need attention or medical referral, e.g. pressure ulcer
- PC11. • Accurately judge when immediate medical attention or support may be
• required, and start with first aid measures
- PC12. • Implement measures that promote clients’ independence within any
• limitations
- PC13. • Recommend and administer appropriate measures to support nutritional
• well-being within the scope of practice and regulations
- PC14. • Educate clients in the promotion of a healthy lifestyle, e.g. do exercise
• regularly, stop smoking
- PC15. • Advise clients on common drug groups and their side effects within the
• scope of practice and regulations, e.g. antihypertensive and analgesics
- PC16. • Support clients in the administration and storage of their medication within
• the scope of practice and regulations
- PC17. • Plan and adapt schedules to ensure each client receives the time they need,
• ensuring individuals are not rushed

#### HSS/N3041: Evaluating client care
Description
Evaluating client care
Scope
The scope covers the following :
The importance of reviewing care at regular intervals and gaining feedback from all parties including clients
#### Elements and Performance Criteria
Evaluating client care
#### To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
- PC1. Understand the clients’ perspectives through careful discussion with them
- PC2. Gain inputs from the clients’ families, colleagues, and related professionals
- PC3. Recognize what makes clients happy and what they consider to be wellbeing
- PC4. • listen carefully and respond positively to any problems, endeavouring to
• develop solutions
- PC5. Judge the extent to which care plans are helping clients
- PC6. • Agree any changes to care plans with clients, their families, colleagues, and
• related professionals
- PC7. Record outcomes of evaluations
- PC8. Develop action plans, taking into consideration the available resources
- PC9. Report and record any concerns to the relevant parties
- PC10. Reflect on feedback and evaluate own working practices
#### Knowledge and Understanding (KU)
The individual on the job needs to know and understand:
- KU1. • The importance of reviewing care at regular intervals and gaining feedback
• from all parties including clients
- KU2. The expected level of client quality of life in relation to their circumstances
- KU3. • The possibilities for the care to be changed/improved e.g. resources
• available
- KU4. Latest developments in products and services
- KU5. How to gain an outcome and assess it
