ISC/Q1801: Metal Forging Technician
Brief Job Description
The individual in this role responsible for performing forging operation and making the components as per
the forging drawing.
Personal Attributes
A forge technician should possess a combination of technical skills and personal attributes to excel in their
role. The person should be patient, organized, team-oriented and self-motivated. They must be able to
plan and prioritise tasks effectively and have an eye for detail and quality
Applicable National Occupational Standards (NOS)
Compulsory NOS:
- 1. ISC/N0946: Manage and implement health and safety practices at the work place
- 2. ISC/N0947: Manage team and others at workplace
- 3. ISC/N1801: Perform the forging operation

ISC/N0946: Manage and implement health and safety practices at the
work place
Description
This OS unit is about managing and implementing health, hygiene, safety and sustainable practices for
optimising use of resources.
Scope
The scope covers the following :
Maintain safe and secure working environment
Maintain health and hygiene
Manage housekeeping at workplace
Implement material and energy conservation practices
Elements and Performance Criteria
Maintain safe and secure working environment
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC1. identify hazardous activities and the possible causes of risks or accidents in the workplace
PC2. implement safe working practices while dealing with hazards to ensure safety of self and
others
PC3. ensure that team is following appropriate safety practices while working in and around
trenches, elevated places and confined areas
PC4. conduct regular checks of the machines with support of the maintenance team to identify
potential hazards
PC5. organise safety drills or training sessions to create awareness amongst others on the
identified risks and safety practices
PC6. fill daily check sheet to report improvements done and risks identified
PC7. ensure that relevant safety boards/signs are placed on the shop floor for the safety of self
and others
PC8. report any identified breaches in health, safety and security policies and procedures to the
designated person
PC9. ensure that team is following appropriate emergency procedures such as raising alarm, safe
evacuation etc.
Maintain health and hygiene
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC10. ensure team is aware about hygiene and sanitation regulations and following them on the
shop floor
PC11. ensure workplace, equipment, restrooms etc. are cleaned and sanitized regularly
PC12. ensure availability of running water, hand wash and alcohol-based sanitizers at the
workplace
PC13. report advanced hygiene and sanitation issues to appropriate authority

ISC/N0947: Manage team and others at workplace
Description
This OS unit is about managing team, colleagues/superiors and others, either in own work group or in other
work groups within organisation.
Scope
The scope covers the following :
Communicate effectively with team members
Interact with supervisor
Manage appropriate behaviour at work place
Elements and Performance Criteria
Communicate effectively with team members
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC1. manage and co-ordinate with team members to integrate work, as per the workload in order
to achieve team goals
PC2. implement ways to share information with team members in line with organisational
requirements
PC3. ensure that work requirements are clearly communicated to the team members through all
means including face-to-face, telephonic and written
PC4. work in a way that show respect for all team members and customers
PC5. carry out commitments made to team members
PC6. resolve conflicts within the team members at work to achieve smooth workflow
PC7. guide the team members to follow the organisation’s policies and procedures
PC8. ensure team goals are given preference over individual goals
PC9. encourage team members to rectify errors as per feedback and minimize mistakes in future
Interact with supervisor
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC10. report progress on job allocated and team performance to the superiors
PC11. escalate problems to supervisors that cannot be handled
PC12. report the completed work
PC13. train the team members to report completed work and receive feedback on work done
Manage appropriate behaviour at work place
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC14. help PwD team members to overcome the challenges, if asked
PC15. ensure team shows sensitivity towards all genders and PwD
PC16. adjust communication styles to reflect gender sensitivity and sensitivity towards person with
disability

ISC/N1801: Perform the forging operation
Description
The Metal Forging Technician training program is designed to provide knowledge and skills necessary to
work in the field of metal forging.
Scope
The scope covers the following :
Manage preparatory activities for forging
Perform forging operations
Perform post operation activities
Elements and Performance Criteria
Manage preparatory activities for forging operation
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC1. Choose the appropriate material for the forging process based on the desired properties,
such as strength, durability, and heat resistance.
PC2. Cut or shape the raw material into the initial form called a billet or blank. The size and shape
of the billet will depend on the final part's design.
PC3. Heat the billet to the appropriate forging temperature. The temperature is critical for ease of
deformation and material flow during forging. Use a furnace or other heating equipment.
PC4. Design and fabricate the forging dies or tooling required for the specific part. The dies should
be designed to shape the material according to the part's design.
PC5. Apply lubricants to the die surfaces and billet to reduce friction and facilitate material flow
during forging. Lubrication helps prevent excessive wear on the dies and enhances the
quality of the forged part.
PC6. Set up the forging equipment, such as hammers, presses, or hydraulic machinery, ensuring
they are in proper working condition and aligned correctly.
PC7. Train the operators who will be performing the forging process. Skilled operators are
essential for achieving accurate and consistent results.
PC8. Implement safety measures to protect operators from hazards associated with forging, such
as hot materials, heavy equipment, and potential flying debris. Provide appropriate safety
gear and training.
PC9. Ensure the forging workspace is clean and organized. Properly store materials, tools, and
safety equipment for efficient and safe operations.
PC10. Mark or identify the workpiece or billet with any necessary information, such as part
numbers, heat codes, or other traceability markings.
PC11. Develop and document quality control plans that outline the specific inspection and testing
procedures to ensure the final product meets the required specifications.
PC12. Establish the sequence of forging operations, including the number of strikes, rotations, and
manipulations required to achieve the desired shape.

PC13. Inspect and verify that all preparation activities have been completed according to the
forging plan and quality control requirements.
Perform Forging operations
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC14. Use appropriate Personal Protective Equipment (PPE) for safe working in work area
PC15. Carry out process safety measures as per industry specifications
PC16. Place the heated billet on an anvil or flat surface between the open dies.
PC17. Apply compressive forces to the billet using a power hammer, press, or other forging
equipment.
PC18. Deform the material and shape as it flows between the open dies.
PC19. The workpiece may need to be manipula ted and rotated between strikes to achieve the
desired shape. Skilled operators use techniques such as bending, twisting, and tapering to
shape the material.
PC20. Continue forging the workpiece in a series of strikes until the desired shape and dimensions
are achieved.
PC21. Reheating the billet if it cools down during the process.
PC22. Trim any excess material or flash from the forged part, which may have been forced out
during the forging process.
PC23. Allow the forged part to cool naturally or use controlled cooling processes to relieve residual
stresses and prevent cracking.
Perform post operation activities
