Recovery)
Brief Job Description
The Junior Supervisor - E-waste (Recycling and Resource Recovery) qualification equips individuals with the
skills to supervise daily operations in e-waste recycling facilities. The role focuses on monitoring team
activities, ensuring complaince with safety and environmental regulations, supporting workforce training,
and maintaining accurate operational records.
Personal Attributes
Attention to detail, Sense of responsibility, Problem-solving attitude, Team-oriented and dependable,
Safety-conscious and disciplined
Applicable National Occupational Standards (NOS)

SGSU/ESS/N0101: Plan and organize e-waste recycling operations
Description
Oversees the planning, coordination, and organization of e-waste collection, segregation, dismantling, and
recycling activities to ensure safe, efficient, and environmentally compliant resource recovery operations.
Scope
The scope covers the following :
Assessing and planning resource requirements
Ensuring compliance with e-waste regulations
Organising workspace and materials
Elements and Performance Criteria
Assessing and planning resource requirements
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC1. Inspect and classify incoming e-waste by type (e.g., plastics, metals, hazardous components)
for operational planning.
PC2. Evaluate the weight and volume of each e-waste category to determine processing priorities.
PC3. Identify the manpower and resource needs for daily operations, including dismantling,
segregation, and recovery tasks.
PC4. Prepare checklists for required tools and equipment based on the types and quantities of e-
waste.
PC5. Organize and document daily workflows and assign tasks to team members.
PC6. Assist in preparing contingency plans for potential bottlenecks or resource shortages.
PC7. Plan and coordinate the collection, transportation, and storage of e-waste, ensuring
adherence to safety and environmental standards as per the E-Waste (Management) Rules,
- 2022.
Ensuring compliance with e-waste regulations
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC8. Monitor team adherence to e-waste handling protocols during daily operations.
PC9. Verify that storage areas and workspaces meet safety and regulatory requirements.
PC10. Maintain records of licenses, permits, and certifications, ensuring availability for internal
reviews and audits.
PC11. Report violations of safety or regulatory standards to the concerned authority for corrective
action.
PC12. Demonstrate an understanding of key provisions under the E-Waste (Management) Rules,
2022, and other relevant Acts to guide team operations and ensure adherence to regulatory
requirements.
Organising workspace and materials
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC13. Designate and label bins and containers for hazardous and non-hazardous materials.

Knowledge and Understanding (KU)
The individual on the job needs to know and understand:
KU1. Knowledge of e-waste categories, materials types, hazardous components, and CPCB's

KU2. Understanding of the E-Waste (Management) Rules, 2022, including compliance requirements,

KU3. Knowledge of resource planning principles, including assessing manpower needs, estimating
weight/volume of incoming e-waste, and preparing tool/equipment checklists for daily
operations.
KU4. Understanding of workflow organisation and task allocation methods, including maintaining
records, preparing contingency plans, and ensuring smooth operational flow.
KU5. Knowledge of safety standards and workspace management practices, including proper
labelling, PPE requirements, hazard identification, and maintenance of approved storage
areas.
Generic Skills (GS)
User/individual on the job needs to know how to:
GS1. Communication skills to coordinate with team members, report issues, and document daily
operational activities clearly.
GS2. Numerical and analytical skills to measure weight/volume of e-waste, interpret checklists, and
understand resource requirements.
GS3. Basic digital literacy to update records, use inventory tools, and access regulatory or
operational information as required.

SGSU/ESS/N0102: Supervise E-waste Processing and Recycling
Description
Oversee dismantling, segregation, and material recovery operations to ensure safe, efficient, and
compliant e-waste recycling processes.
Scope
The scope covers the following :
Overseeing recycling processes
Managing hazardous waste handling
Ensuring safety and quality standards
Elements and Performance Criteria
Overseeing recycling processes
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC1. Supervise dismantling and segregation activities to ensure adherence to quality and safety
standards.
PC2. Monitor the operation of tools and equipment to identify and report malfunctions.
PC3. Track and document material recovery rates, reporting anomalies to the concerned authority
PC4. Guide team members in handling complex or unusual waste components.
Managing hazardous waste handling
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC5. Identify hazardous materials, such as batteries and CRTs, during sorting and segregation.
PC6. Ensure proper labelling, containment, and storage of hazardous materials to prevent
contamination.
PC7. Record quantities and types of hazardous waste for disposal and submit reports to the
concerned authority.
Ensuring safety and quality standards
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC8. Conduct pre-shift briefings to reinforce safety protocols.
PC9. Inspect workspaces regularly for adherence to safety and quality guidelines.
PC10. Address minor safety violations on the spot and escalate serious issues to the concerned
authority.
Knowledge and Understanding (KU)
The individual on the job needs to know and understand:
KU1. Knowledge of e-waste processing and recycling methods, including dismantling, segregation,
recovery, and safe material flow practices.

SGSU/ESS/N0103: Support Workforce Training and Awareness
Description
Assist in organising training sessions and promoting environmental awareness among workers to enhance
safety, skill, and sustainability practices.
Scope
The scope covers the following :
Assisting in workforce training
Promoting environmental awareness
Elements and Performance Criteria
Assisting in workforce training
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC1. Facilitate training logistics, such as setting up equipment and distributing training materials.
PC2. Support trainers by assisting participants during practical demonstrations.
PC3. Monitor team members’ performance during and after training, providing constructive
feedback to the trainers and management
PC4. Maintain attendance records for training sessions and report team performance
improvements.
Promoting environmental awareness
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC5. Distribute awareness materials on the environmental impact of improper e-waste disposal.
PC6. Organize team discussions to identify sustainable practices and areas for improvement.
PC7. Report team suggestions for process improvements to the management for review.
Knowledge and Understanding (KU)
The individual on the job needs to know and understand:
KU1. Knowledge of workforce training methods and procedures, including preparation, facilitation,
and post-training evaluation.
KU2. Understanding of communication and feedback techniques for effectively supporting trainers
and engaging participants.
KU3. Knowledge of environmental sustainability principles related to e-waste recycling and
resource recovery.
KU4. Understanding of documentation and reporting protocols for maintaining attendance,
performance, and feedback records.
KU5. Knowledge of awareness-building approaches such as group discussions, demonstrations, and
dissemination of educational materials.

Description
Maintain operational records, track expenses, and support budgeting activities to ensure transparency,

Scope

Maintaining accurate records

Elements and Performance Criteria

PC1. Log daily e-waste inputs and outputs, categorized by type.
PC2. Record quantities of hazardous and non-hazardous materials processed and stored.
PC3. Maintain logs of tool usage, maintenance schedules, and inventory levels.
PC4. Ensure records are secure and accessible for audits and internal reviews.
Supporting in budget and expenditure tracking
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC5. Track daily operational expenses, such as fuel, consumables, and equipment usage.
PC6. Identify cost overruns or inefficiencies and report them to the concerned authority
PC7. Suggest cost-saving measures for routine activities without compromising safety or quality

KU1. Knowledge of record-keeping systems and documentation procedures used for tracking e-
waste inflow, outflow, and storage.

regulatory compliance.
KU3. Knowledge of basic budgeting principles, including expense categorisation, cost tracking, and
variance reporting.
KU4. Understanding of financial control measures to identify inefficiencies, wastage, or cost
overruns in daily operations.
KU5. Knowledge of digital tools or spreadsheet applications used for maintaining logs, expense
reports, and inventory data.
