CUTM/AGR/Q0201: Post-harvest Commodity Test and Storage Supervisor
Brief Job Description
The Individual is responsible to plan post-harvest of different commodities, plan for Testing methods for
product Certification, Monitor Pests and diseases in storage and their management, Grain deterioration
and their management, Safe storage of fruits and vegetables, Hands-on practice on safe storage
techniques.
Personal Attributes
A Post-Harvest Commodity Test and Storage Supervisor should be analytical, detail-oriented, and
responsible. They must possess strong knowledge of post-harvest handling, storage systems, and disease
prevention. Effective communication, decision-making skills, and the ability to implement quality standards
are essential for ensuring the safety and longevity of agricultural produce.
Applicable National Occupational Standards (NOS)
Compulsory NOS:
- 1. CUTM/AGR/N0206: Suggest suitable post-harvest technology
- 2. CUTM/AGR/N0207: Suggest suitable storage methods

CUTM/AGR/N0206: Suggest suitable post-harvest technology
Description
This NOS covers identifying and implementing appropriate post-harvest technologies to maintain the
quality of agricultural commodities. It includes understanding maturity indices, selecting chemical and
physical treatment methods, ensuring proper storage conditions, and using modern preservation
techniques to minimize losses and enhance market value.
Scope
The scope covers the following :
The scope of this NOS includes evaluating commodity ripeness, applying post-harvest treatments,
and ensuring proper storage. It covers grading, sorting, packaging, and the application of
preservation techniques such as controlled atmosphere storage, chemical treatment, and eco-
friendly methods to extend shelf life and maintain quality.
Elements and Performance Criteria
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC1. Understand the composition and physiology of Fruits, Vegetables, Seeds and Grains.
PC2. Understand the maturity indices and factors related to ripening and quality of horticulture
produce.
PC3. Clearly understand the application of chemicals used to control spoilage.
PC4. Understand detailed information on export-oriented quality grading of fruits and vegetables.
PC5. Knowledge on hurdle technologies and minimal processing.
PC6. Know post-harvest waste utilization and ecofriendly packaging techniques.
PC7. Know about application of waxing, absorbers and scrubbers.
PC8. Understand the working and factors related to Cellar storage, Cold storage, MAP, CAS and
Hypobaric storage system.
PC9. Design and construct Zero Energy Cooling Chamber (ZECC) and knowledge of its working
principle.
PC10. Understand the relation of data loggers (Time, temperature, relative humidity)
PC11. Know about different types of packaging materials and methods. Modern packaging systems.
Knowledge and Understanding (KU)
The individual on the job needs to know and understand:
KU1. the composition and physiology of fruits, vegetables, seeds, and grains.
KU2. maturity indices and factors affecting ripening and quality.
KU3. chemical treatments used to control spoilage and extend shelf life.
KU4. post-harvest biochemistry and its role in commodity preservation.

CUTM/AGR/N0207: Suggest suitable storage methods
Description
This NOS covers selecting appropriate storage methods for different commodities, ensuring safe storage
conditions, and minimizing losses. It involves applying modern and traditional storage techniques,
controlling pests and moisture, and maintaining compliance with quality standards.
Scope
The scope covers the following :
The scope includes evaluating commodity-specific storage requirements, implementing fumigation
techniques, controlling environmental factors such as temperature and humidity, and ensuring safe
handling practices. It also covers record-keeping, inventory management, and application of modern
storage technologies.
Elements and Performance Criteria
Suggest suitable storage methods
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC1. Suggest suitable storage methods for Vegetables.
PC2. Suggest suitable storage methods for Cereals.
PC3. Suggest suitable storage methods for Fruits.
Understanding the different seed borne pathogens associated with seeds
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC4. Important seed-borne fungi, viruses, bacteria and nematode in storage condition.
PC5. Important micro-organisms infect seed in storage conditions and toxins produced by them.
Knowledge on various detection methods of seed borne pathogens and management
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC6. Detection, Mechanism of transmission, and management of seedborne pathogen (special
focus on stored seed pathogen)
PC7. Understand Chemical treatment procedure and Equipment used for seed treatment.
PC8. Management of seed-borne disease in an organic manner.
PC9. Quarantine
PC10. Seed certification standards.
Knowledge and Understanding (KU)
The individual on the job needs to know and understand:
KU1. different storage methods for vegetables, cereals, and fruits.
KU2. storage conditions required for different commodity types.
KU3. pest infestation risks and preventive measures in storage.

CUTM/AGR/N0208: Management of diseases affecting seed.
Description
This NOS covers the identification, prevention, and management of diseases affecting stored seeds. It
includes seed treatment methods, quarantine measures, integrated disease management, and ensuring
compliance with seed health regulations. The focus is on maintaining seed viability, quality, and safety for
long-term storage and distribution.
Scope
The scope covers the following :
The scope includes monitoring seed-borne diseases, applying biological and chemical treatments,
implementing sanitation practices, and ensuring compliance with phytosanitary regulations. It also
covers seed health testing, record-keeping, and integrating disease-resistant seed varieties to
enhance storage quality and reduce post-harvest losses.
Elements and Performance Criteria
Understanding the different insect-pests and non-insect-pests associated with stored commodities
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC1. Important stored grain pests (Various internal and External Feeders), Management Practices.
PC2. Non-Insect pests and Their management practices.
Knowledge on various detection methods of Hidden infestation
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC3. Detection, Mechanism of Chitosan Tests.
PC4. Ninhydrin Tests for pests’ infestation detection.
PC5. Preparation of Baits for various species of rodents.
PC6. Quarantine
PC7. Safe storage structures for the long-term storage of Seeds.
Knowledge and Understanding (KU)
The individual on the job needs to know and understand:
KU1. common seed-borne diseases and their impact on seed quality.
KU2. factors contributing to seed infections, such as humidity, temperature, and storage conditions.
KU3. biological, chemical, and physical seed treatment methods.
KU4. quarantine regulations and phytosanitary measures for disease prevention.
KU5. seed certification standards and legal compliance for seed storage and distribution.
Generic Skills (GS)
User/individual on the job needs to know how to:

CUTM/VSQ/N7804: Ensure organizational health, safety and security for
self and others
Description
This NOS focuses on maintaining personal hygiene, workplace safety, and environmental responsibility. It
outlines procedures for disease prevention, safe equipment handling, waste disposal, and emergency
response. The standard ensures that individuals follow preventive measures and safety protocols to create
a clean, hazard-free, and health-conscious work environment.
Scope
The scope covers the following :
The scope covers adherence to sanitation and hygiene norms, safe use of tools and chemicals,
proper waste management, first aid administration, and compliance with government and workplace
advisories. It aims to prepare individuals to work safely under routine and emergency situations,
reducing risks to themselves, coworkers, and the environment.
Elements and Performance Criteria
Maintain personal hygiene
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC1. wash hands, legs and face with soap/alcohol based sanitizer at reasonable intervals
PC2. wash the worn clothes with soap and sundry before use next time
PC3. ensure the face is covered with mask or three layers of cloth-piece
PC4. follow the workplace sanitization norms including distancing from sick people
Maintain clean and safe workplace
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC5. carry out basic safety checks before operation of all tools, implements, and machinery and
report identified hazards to the supervisor
PC6. follow the instructions mentioned on the labels of chemicals/pesticides/fumigants etc to
avoid hazards
PC7. sanitize equipment, tools and machinery before and after use
PC8. dispose waste safely and correctly in the designated area
PC9. work in a manner which minimizes environmental damage, ensuring all procedures and
instructions for controlling risks are followed
PC10. follow government / workplace advisories in case of outbreak of any disease/disaster
Administer appropriate emergency procedures
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC11. follow procedures for dealing with accidents, fires and emergencies, including
communicating location and directions to the location of emergency, as per the workplace
requirements
