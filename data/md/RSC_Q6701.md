RSC/Q6701: Latex Harvest Junior Technician (Tapper)
Brief Job Description
A Latex Harvest Junior Technician (Tapper) prepares for tapping operations, performs scientific tapping of
rubber trees, collects latex efficiently, and ensures post-tapping hygiene to maintain tree health and yield.
The role is labour-intensive, requiring knowledge of rubber tree physiology, safe tool handling, sustainable
harvesting techniques, and hygiene practices. The technician works under supervision of senior staff/field
supervisor in plantations or smallholder farms.
Personal Attributes
The individual in this job must have good health to tap around 300-400 rubber trees everyday early in the
morning. The individual should be punctual to perform activities on time.
Applicable National Occupational Standards (NOS)
Compulsory NOS:
- 1. RSC/N6127: Prepare for tapping activities
- 2. RSC/N6701: Perform scientific tapping of rubber trees followed by latex collection
- 3. RSC/N5901: Maintain tree health and post tapping hygiene
- 4. DGT/VSQ/N0101: Employability Skills (30 Hours)

RSC/N6127: Prepare for tapping activities
Description
This NOS covers site and tree preparation activities before tapping operations, including tool maintenance,
tapping panel identification, marking, and ensuring readiness for latex collection.
Scope
The scope covers the following :
Prepare tapping tools and equipment
Identify and mark tapping panel
Prepare tree for tapping
Ensure pre-tapping readiness
Elements and Performance Criteria
Prepare tapping tools and equipment
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC1. check sharpness and alignment of tapping knives
PC2. sharpen knives to prescribed angle using stone
PC3. disinfect knives to avoid disease transfer
PC4. check availability and condition of spouts, cups, buckets, and collection pots
PC5. clean collection vessels before use
PC6. arrange tapping kit systematically for field use
PC7. ensure personal protective gear (gloves, boots, cap) is in place
Identify and mark tapping panel
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC8. identify trees ready for tapping based on girth
PC9. select correct tapping panel based on tapping cycle
PC10. mark tapping line at recommended slope (30°)
PC11. mark depth and length according to tapping system
PC12. prepare alternate panel if old panel is exhausted
PC13. avoid marking over diseased or injured bark
Prepare tree for tapping
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC14. clear moss, algae, or dirt from panel area
PC15. clean trunk around tapping zone to prevent contamination
PC16. fix spouts at correct angle for latex flow
PC17. place collection cups under spouts securely
PC18. apply preservative solution in cups before use
Ensure pre-tapping readiness

RSC/N6701: Perform scientific tapping of rubber trees followed by latex
collection
Description
This NOS covers scientific tapping practices, including controlled incision, depth maintenance, latex
collection, and preservative use to maximize yield without harming trees.
Scope
The scope covers the following :
Perform tapping cut
Collect latex
Handle tools and materials
Elements and Performance Criteria
Perform tapping cut
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC1. hold tapping knife firmly with correct grip
PC2. maintain slope of 30° while cutting
PC3. keep depth within 1–1.5 mm avoiding cambium damage
PC4. maintain smooth and continuous cut
PC5. remove shavings uniformly without rough edges
PC6. follow prescribed tapping frequency (d2, d3)
PC7. adjust cut length depending on tapping system
PC8. avoid over-tapping or premature tapping
PC9. ensure uniform bark consumption across cycles
PC10. renew cut when panel becomes irregular
Collect latex
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC11. ensure latex flows without obstruction into cups
PC12. collect latex into buckets after sufficient accumulation
PC13. sieve latex to remove impurities
PC14. measure latex volume from each section
PC15. add ammonia preservative in prescribed proportion
PC16. record preservative usage
PC17. stir latex properly for uniform preservation
PC18. transfer latex to larger collection vessels in shed
PC19. prevent spillage and wastage during transfer
PC20. clean cups and buckets post collection
Handle tools and materials

RSC/N5901: Maintain tree health and post tapping hygiene
Description
This NOS involves maintaining tree health after tapping through proper bark management, disease control,
rest marking, and hygiene practices.
Scope
The scope covers the following :
Post-tapping hygiene
Maintain tree health
Sustainable practices
Elements and Performance Criteria
Post-tapping hygiene
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC1. clean tapping panel of residual latex
PC2. remove bark shavings and debri
PC3. disinfect spouts, cups, and buckets
PC4. clean moss and algae around panel area
PC5. apply stimulants or fungicides if instructed
PC6. record post-tapping hygiene activities
Maintain tree health
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC7. observe signs of TPD (tapping panel dryness)
PC8. detect brown bark disease or fungal infections
PC9. report abnormal bark conditions
PC10. mark resting trees and avoid further tapping
PC11. assist in bark renewal when instructed
PC12. mulch soil around stressed trees
PC13. apply organic manure where required
Sustainable practices
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC14. dispose latex waste safely
PC15. ensure safe disposal of used chemicals
PC16. conserve water during cleaning
PC17. follow eco-friendly practices in plantation
PC18. assist in maintaining biodiversity around plantation
Knowledge and Understanding (KU)
