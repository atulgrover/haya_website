SSC/Q8903: 3D Printing Modelling Engineer
Brief Job Description
Individuals at this job are responsible for creating design, conceptualization, development of 3d models in
the design software programs and generation of the G-codes for printing the product
Personal Attributes
This job requires the individual to have thorough knowledge of various technology trends and processes as
well as have updated knowledge about IT initiatives. He/she should be highly motivated and energetic with
the ability to self-direct daily activities.
Applicable National Occupational Standards (NOS)
Compulsory NOS:
- 1. SSC/N8912: Design and develop finalized 3D models and product prototypes using CAD/CAM
programs
- 2. SSC/N8913: Structure and conduct appropriate simulation tests, design optimization techniques to
validate and finalize the 3D CAD models.
- 3. SSC/N8914: Export print-ready digital design files and generate tools paths for 3D printers in the
form of G-code instructions
- 4. DGT/VSQ/N0102: Employability Skills (60 Hours)

SSC/N8912: Design and develop finalized 3D models and product
prototypes using CAD/CAM programs
Description
This unit is about incorporation of Dfam principles to optimize the design methodologyand process
optimization
Scope
The scope covers the following :
Fundamentals of 3D Printing and CAD Design,Design Principles and Optimization
Techniques,Mechanical Properties and Strength Analysis,Printing Strategy and Support
Optimization,Software Tools and Automation,Creative Design Elements,Market and User
Considerations,Quality and Compliance Standards
Elements and Performance Criteria
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC1. Incorporate DfAM (Design for Additive Manufacturing) principles to optimize the design
methodology based on 3D printing technology.
PC2. Evaluate part consolidation, geometric freedom, and biomimicry to enhance design
capabilities.
PC3. Consider key process-specific, material-driven design guidelines, rules, and considerations.
PC4. Consider limitations of 3D printing parts, pre-defined design allowables in line with industry
standards to comprehend design feasibility
PC5. Include suggested material types, colors, infill levels, orientations, and finishing methods in
the 3D design.
PC6. Evaluate the constraints in physical properties of materials, including supports for overhangs,
walls, and corners
PC7. Determine shapes of components with fewer stress points, smaller footprints, and superior
mechanical properties
PC8. Decide on the minimum level of detail required based on the specific 3D printing technology.
PC9. Quantify and prioritize necessary mechanical properties, standards, and loads for the parts.
PC10. Refer to industry standards for applicable mechanical properties, including tensile,
compression, flexural strength, and wear resistance
PC11. Analyze loading conditions to design parts such that largest forces traverse planes parallel to
the print bed for optimal strength
PC12. Identify critical dimensions and features, including minimum wall thickness and support
variables, adjusting for printer precision
PC13. Maximize print bed contact to minimize the need for supports and improve bed adhesion.
PC14. Orient the part optimally so the largest face lies on the print bed unless dictated otherwise
by strength or geometry

SSC/N8913: Structure and conduct appropriate simulation tests, design
optimization techniques to validate and finalize the 3D CAD models.
Description
This unit is about conducting simulation tests and validation of the design models developed
Scope
The scope covers the following :
Simulation Planning and Setup,Finite Element Analysis (FEA) Execution,Data Output and
Analysis,Results Verification and Redesign,Topology Optimization (TO),Implementation and
Continuous Improvement
Elements and Performance Criteria
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC1. Perform multiple simulation runs to ensure the design is compatible with the chosen
manufacturing processes
PC2. Aim to enhance the maximum pre-print accuracy of the CAD models by creating various test
scenarios in iterations
PC3. Start with simple models and gradually modify them as the simulations present plausible
results.
PC4. Create a detailed project plan to accommodate multiple iterations of FEA and TO (Topology
Optimization) simulations coherently
PC5. Establish a clear set of success criteria for simulations based on project goals and
performance targets to ensure results align with intended outcomes.
PC6. Conduct Finite Element Analysis (FEA) to understand and quantify the effects of real-world
conditions on a part or assembly
PC7. Research and select from the most advanced FEA simulation software packages best suited
for the analysis requirements.
PC8. Determine and prioritize various types of FEA simulations like structural, thermal,
computational fluid dynamics (CFD), etc., based on the process, materials, and necessary
performance indicators.
PC9. Generate a mesh model and refine the mesh using an iterative process of the FEA theory.
PC10. Define element type, choose material data set, and assign all the properties required
PC11. Enter sections and assign all the loads, constants, and boundary conditions according to the
type of simulation
PC12. Request the data output according to the variables you want to study, i.e., forces,
displacements, stresses, strengths, etC
PC13. Analyze the data by plotting contours of the requested variables, evaluating the deformed
and undeformed shapes plots in the form of custom tabular reports.
PC14. Obtain a detailed visualization of where structures bend or twist, and the distribution of
stresses and displacements as results.

PC15. Utilize sensitivity analysis to understand how variations in input parameters affect simulation
outcomes.
PC16. Verify if the upper limits of failure meet the required mechanical properties, standards, and

PC17. Consider redesigning the models based on the results for further analysis.
PC18. Incorporate advanced simulation techniques such as multi-physics simulations that combine
thermal, structural, and fluid dynamics analyses when necessary
PC19. Validate simulation results with physical testing of prototypes to verify accuracy and refine
models based on observed discrepancies
PC20. Perform Topology Optimization (TO) after the initial FEA simulation results check out for
optimal material distribution layout.
PC21. Select from the available list of specific TO software solutions or the usual CAD packages.
PC22. Conduct topology optimization using the Finite Element Method (FEM).
PC23. Aim to optimize material layout within a predefined set of loads, conditions, and constraints.
PC24. Maximize the performance and efficiency of design by removing redundant material from
areas that do not need to carry significant loads.
PC25. Solve design challenges like reducing unnecessary weight, resonance, and thermal stresses
(e.g., lattice structures)
PC26. Determine the minimum allowable design space necessary for shape optimization of the
product
PC27. Use TO software to test the structural integrity and identify unnecessary material under
various simulated stress scenarios.
PC28. Implement the design modifications continuously as indicated by the simulation test
outcomes
PC29. Analyze cost implications associated with different design optimizations to ensure that
performance improvements do not significantly increase production costs.
PC30. Train and mentor team members on best practices in simulation methods and software
usage to enhance overall team capabilities

SSC/N8914: Export print-ready digital design files and generate tools
paths for 3D printers in the form of G-code instructions
Description
This unit is about finalising and exporting the design model to be printed in the 3D printer
Scope
The scope covers the following :
File Preparation and Export,Slicing and Tool Path Generation,G-code Management and Optimization
Elements and Performance Criteria
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC1. Transform the raw design files and renders into 3D printable digital file formats as Polygon
Mesh models.
PC2. Select from different file formats to export like STL, STEP, IGES or IGS, OBJ, 3MF, or DXF, with
STL being the most commonly preferred format
PC3. Employ appropriate methods to export based on the CAD program being used.
PC4. Consider the parameters used for outputting an STL file and their effect on the faceting of
the mesh model.
PC5. Understand that smoother/less faceted surfaces (higher poly count or triangulation) result in
larger STL file sizes
PC6. Determine factors like chordal tolerance or deviation, angle control, and minimum triangle
side length.
PC7. Strike a balance between the model, desired surface fidelity, and the 3D printing process of
choice, as 3D printing can only accept a certain size
PC8. Adjust the values of the chordal and angular tolerance to generate the optimal file size and
mesh faceting without compromising on resolution.
PC9. After generating the mesh, ensure supports are created for the mesh model intelligently
PC10. Use mesh repair tools to check, repair, and optimize the mesh before slicing
PC11. Process the STL file using a slicer or slicing software to generate automated tool paths.
PC12. Convert the model into a series of thin layers through the slicer, producing a G-code file
(.gcode) containing instructions tailored to a specific type of 3D printer
PC13. Determine optimal printing build parameters in the slicing software with regard to necessary
mechanical properties, standards, and load requirements.
PC14. Input parameters like orientation, fill pattern, layer thickness, print speed, temperature, and
other printer-specific settings.
PC15. If needed, modify model specifications partially through scaling features in the slicer before
processing
PC16. Select the right slicing software based on organizational preferences, as the choice of slicer
will impact the final print quality.
