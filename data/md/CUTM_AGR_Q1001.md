CUTM/AGR/Q1001: Intensive Aquaculture Supervisor
Brief Job Description
The supervisor is responsible for various farming system which involve pond culture, polythene line pond,
raceways, in pond raceway system, cage and pen culture etc. it gives practical exposure about design,
layout, standard operating procedures, water quality management, feed management, health
management, species selection.
Personal Attributes
An Intensive Aquaculture Supervisor should be observant, detail-oriented, and proactive in managing
aquatic systems. Strong leadership, problem-solving, and decision-making skills are essential. They must
demonstrate adaptability, patience, and responsibility while ensuring sustainability and productivity. Good
communication, teamwork, and commitment to maintaining water quality, fish health, and operational
efficiency are vital.
Applicable National Occupational Standards (NOS)
Compulsory NOS:
- 1. CUTM/AGR/N1001: Study and Selection of Appropriate Intensive Culture Systems
- 2. CUTM/AGR/N1002: Knowledge of Aquaculture Farming using Biofloc Technology

CUTM/AGR/N1001: Study and Selection of Appropriate Intensive Culture
Systems
Description
The NOS "Study and Selection of appropriate Intensive culture systems" under the Intensive Aquaculture
Supervisor qualification focuses on enabling trainees to understand and apply modern intensive
aquaculture practices. It involves identifying and selecting suitable sites, analyzing water quality,
managing feed and fish nutrition, and choosing appropriate culture species. The NOS also emphasizes
designing layouts, developing standard operating procedures (SOPs), and implementing effective health
management practices. Through both theoretical knowledge and hands-on training, learners gain the
ability to plan, establish, and maintain intensive aquaculture systems such as ponds, raceways, and cage
culture with proper record-keeping and sustainability measures.
Scope
The scope covers the following :
The NOS Scope for Study and Selection of Appropriate Intensive Culture Systems under the Intensive
Aquaculture Supervisor qualification focuses on equipping learners with knowledge and skills to
plan, implement, and manage intensive aquaculture systems. It covers site selection, water quality
analysis, feed and species management, and health monitoring to ensure sustainable production.
The scope also includes designing layouts, developing SOPs, and maintaining systematic records for
different culture systems such as ponds, raceways, and cages. Emphasis is placed on applying
scientific, technical, and managerial practices to enhance productivity while ensuring quality and
hygiene standards.
Elements and Performance Criteria
Study and selection of appropriate Intensive culture systems
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC1. Site selection criteria for intensive culture system.
PC2. Maintenance of water quality parameters.
PC3. Suitable culture species.
PC4. Fish nutrition and feed management.
PC5. Health management.
PC6. Design and construction of intensive aquaculture models.
PC7. Engineering aspects of intensive aquaculture system.
Knowledge and Understanding (KU)
The individual on the job needs to know and understand:
KU1. different models of intensive aquaculture (e.g., tanks, cages, ponds, RAS) and their
applications in commercial farming.

CUTM/AGR/N1002: Knowledge of Aquaculture Farming using Biofloc
Technology
Description
The NOS focuses on equipping learners with the knowledge and practical skills required for aquaculture
farming using Biofloc technology. It covers the principles of floc formation, maintenance of optimum water
quality, and management of the carbon-to-nitrogen (C:N) ratio for microbial balance. Trainees learn
species selection, nutrition, and feed management practices suited to biofloc systems. The NOS also
emphasizes health management, use of probiotics, and engineering aspects such as design, aeration, and
construction of biofloc units. Additionally, it integrates economic feasibility, standard operating procedures
(SOPs), and record-keeping for sustainable and cost-effective aquaculture operations.
Scope
The scope covers the following :
The NOS Scope for Knowledge of Aquaculture Farming using Biofloc Technology under the Intensive
Aquaculture Supervisor qualification focuses on equipping learners with practical and theoretical
competencies in sustainable aquaculture practices. It covers identification of biofloc production
systems, engineering and design of biofloc units, and management of water quality and feed
through microbial growth, probiotics, and carbon - nitrogen balance. The scope also includes species
selection, fish health management, floc formation techniques, aeration systems, and economic
evaluation of biofloc farming. Learners are trained to develop SOPs, maintain records, and ensure
optimized productivity with minimal environmental impact.
Elements and Performance Criteria
Knowledge of aquaculture farming using Biofloc technology
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC1. Floc formation techniques.
PC2. Maintenance of water quality parameters.
PC3. Suitable culture species.
PC4. Fish health, nutrition and feed management.
PC5. C: N Ratio management and carbon resource utilization
PC6. Design, construction and aeration facilities for biofloc culture systems
PC7. Economics of biofloc based farming
Knowledge and Understanding (KU)
The individual on the job needs to know and understand:
KU1. the basic concepts, benefits, and evolution of biofloc in sustainable aquaculture.
KU2. the layout, aeration systems, and construction of biofloc units to maintain productivity.
KU3. floc development, maintenance of microbial growth, and role of probiotics and prebiotics.

CUTM/AGR/N1003: Understanding of Aquaculture Farming using
Aquaponics Technology
Description
The Understanding of Aquaculture farming using Aquaponics technology NOS under the Intensive
Aquaculture Supervisor qualification emphasizes integrating aquaculture with hydroponic farming to create
a sustainable and symbiotic system. It covers design and construction of aquaponics setups, selection of
suitable fish and plant species, maintenance of water quality, and management of nutrition cycles.
Learners gain skills in fish health management, application of fertilizers and minerals, and ensuring
optimum feed practices. The NOS also focuses on documenting processes, maintaining quality standards,
and understanding the economic aspects of aquaponics systems.
Scope
The scope covers the following :
The NOS Scope for Understanding of Aquaculture farming using Aquaponics technology under the
Intensive Aquaculture Supervisor qualification focuses on integrating fish farming with hydroponic
plant cultivation to create sustainable, symbiotic systems. It includes designing and constructing
aquaponics units, maintaining water quality, managing fish health, nutrition, and feed, and selecting
suitable aquaculture and plant species. The scope also covers application of fertilizers and minerals,
understanding nutritive cycles, and evaluating the economics of aquaponics systems. This NOS
ensures that learners can apply scientific and technical knowledge for efficient, eco-friendly
aquaculture farming using aquaponics technology.
Elements and Performance Criteria

CUTM/AGR/N1004: Knowledge of Aquaculture Farming for Ornamental
Fish
Description
The "Knowledge of Aquaculture farming for Ornamental Fish" under the Intensive Aquaculture Supervisor
qualification focuses on the specialized skills required to manage ornamental fish farming systems. It
covers identification, procurement, and breeding of exotic, indigenous, and endemic ornamental fish
species, along with aquatic plants. The NOS emphasizes design, construction, and operation of aquariums,
hatcheries, and advanced aquaculture systems, including filtration and aeration. Learners gain knowledge
of feed management, health care, water quality monitoring, mass production technologies, and live food
organisms. It also integrates aspects of marketing, economics, and record-keeping to ensure sustainable
ornamental fish farming practices.
Scope
The scope covers the following :
The NOS Scope for Knowledge of Aquaculture farming for Ornamental fish under the Intensive
Aquaculture Supervisor qualification focuses on providing skills and knowledge for managing
ornamental fish production systems. It covers identification and procurement of exotic, indigenous,
and endemic species, designing and constructing aquariums, hatcheries, and ornamental units, and
maintaining water quality, feed, and health management. Learners also gain expertise in breeding
techniques, installation of aeration and filtration systems, and mass production of ornamental fish
and live food organisms. The scope further extends to understanding economics, marketing
channels, and record-keeping for sustainable ornamental aquaculture operations.
Elements and Performance Criteria
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC1. Design and construction of different types of aquaculture systems.
PC2. Maintenance of water quality parameters.
PC3. Selection of suitable ornamental and aquatic plant species.
PC4. Fish health, nutrition and feed management.
PC5. Installation and operation of filtration, aeration and aquarium decorative.
PC6. Understanding the mass production technologies for ornamental fishes and live food
organisms.
PC7. Understanding the economics of ornamental farming systems and its marketing.
Knowledge and Understanding (KU)
The individual on the job needs to know and understand:
KU1. exotic, indigenous, and endemic ornamental fish species, along with their classification and
characteristics.

Assessment Guidelines and Assessment Weightage
Assessment Guidelines
- 1. Assessment System Overview:
• Batches assigned to the assessment agencies for conducting the assessment on SIDH or email
• Assessment agencies send the assessment confirmation to TP looping AB/AA to accept in SIDH
• Assessment agency deploys the ToA certified Assessor for executing the assessment
• AB monitors the assessment process & records
- 2. Testing Environment:
• Check the Assessment location, date and time
• If the batch size is more than 30, then there should be 2 Assessors.
• Check that the allotted time to the candidates to complete Theory & Practical Assessment is correct.

| Sub-sector | Sub-sector is derived from a further breakdown based on the characteristics and interests of its components. |
| Occupation | Occupation is a set of job roles, which perform similar/ related set of functions in an industry. |
| Job role | Job role defines a unique set of functions that together form a unique employment opportunity in an organisation. |
| Occupational Standards (OS) | OS specify the standards of performance an individual must achieve when carrying out a function in the workplace, together with the Knowledge and Understanding (KU) they need to meet that standard consistently. Occupational Standards are applicable both in the Indian and global contexts. |
| Performance Criteria (PC) | Performance Criteria (PC) are statements that together specify the standard of performance required when carrying out a task. |
| National Occupational Standards (NOS) | NOS are occupational standards which apply uniquely in the Indian context. |
| Qualifications Pack (QP) | QP comprises the set of OS, together with the educational, training and other criteria required to perform a job role. A QP is assigned a unique qualifications pack code. |
| Unit Code | Unit code is a unique identifier for an Occupational Standard, which is denoted by an ‘N’ |
| Unit Title | Unit title gives a clear overall statement about what the incumbent should be able to do. |

| Scope | Scope is a set of statements specifying the range of variables that an individual may have to deal with in carrying out the function which have a critical impact on quality of performance required. |
