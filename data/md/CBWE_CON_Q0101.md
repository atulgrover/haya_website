CBWE/CON/Q0101: Retrofitting Technician (Construction Sector)
Brief Job Description
The Retrofitting Technician at NSQF Level 4 is skilled in performing complex retrofitting operations to
repair and strengthen existing structures. This qualification is tailored to individuals involved in building
repair and re-strengthening, utilizing techniques such as structural repairs, seismic retrofitting, crack
injections, and strengthening with steel, concrete, and other materials. The role requires in-depth technical
knowledge, practical expertise, and adherence to safety standards to ensure that buildings are restored to
a condition that enhances their durability and load-bearing capacity.
Personal Attributes
The individual should be physically fit to work for sustained durations in varying site conditions and
environments. The person should be able to interpret basic structural drawings, follow technical
instructions, and work in coordination with structural engineers and other team members. The individual
should have a safety-conscious attitude, sound manual dexterity, and adequate verbal communication
skills to report observations accurately.
Applicable National Occupational Standards (NOS)
Compulsory NOS:
- 1. CBWE/CON/N0101: Assess Building Condition and Identify Repair & Strengthening Requirements
- 2. CBWE/CON/N0102: Conduct Structural Repairs (Masonry, Plastering, Waterproofing, etc.)
- 3. CBWE/CON/N0103: Perform Crack and Joint Repairs (Epoxy, Polyurethane Injection)
- 4. CBWE/CON/N0104: Apply Structural Re-Strengthening Techniques (Steel, Concrete, FRP)
- 5. CBWE/CON/N0105: Implement Seismic Retrofitting Solutions
- 6. CBWE/CON/N0106: Execute Quality Control and Safety Standards in Retrofitting Work
- 7. DGT/VSQ/N0102: Employability Skills (60 Hours)

CBWE/CON/N0101: Assess Building Condition and Identify Repair &
Strengthening Requirements
Description
This unit describes the skills and knowledge required to inspect and evaluate building structural elements,
determine root causes of structural weaknesses, review relevant drawings, and select appropriate repair
and strengthening techniques in alignment with national standards.
Scope
The scope covers the following :
Inspect and evaluate structural condition of buildings
Review documentation and select repair and strengthening strategies
Elements and Performance Criteria
Inspect and evaluate structural condition of buildings
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC1. inspect and evaluate building structural elements (e.g., beams, columns, foundations) to
identify visible signs of damage such as cracks, corrosion, and decay
PC2. determine the root causes of structural weaknesses, such as water leakage, settlement, or
wear and tear
Review documentation and select repair and strengthening strategies
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC3. review architectural and structural drawings to understand original design and current
condition
PC4. select appropriate repair and strengthening techniques based on the severity and type of
damage
PC5. ensure that the assessment aligns with relevant national standards and codes for building
repair and strengthening
Knowledge and Understanding (KU)
The individual on the job needs to know and understand:
KU1. load-bearing functions and structural behaviour of beams, columns, slabs, foundations, and
load-bearing walls
KU2. effect of deterioration or damage in structural elements on the stability and integrity of the
overall building
KU3. common signs and causes of structural damage, including cracks, spalling, corrosion,
settlement, water ingress, and material fatigue
KU4. types of architectural and structural drawings including plan drawings, elevation drawings,
and structural detail drawings

CBWE/CON/N0102: Conduct Structural Repairs (Masonry, Plastering,
Waterproofing, etc.)
Description
This unit describes the skills and knowledge required to prepare damaged surfaces, apply appropriate
repair materials and coatings, follow specific repair methodologies based on material type, and monitor
the curing and setting process to ensure structural repair work is completed to standard.
Scope
The scope covers the following :
Prepare surfaces for structural repair work
Apply repair materials and monitor curing process
Elements and Performance Criteria
Prepare surfaces for structural repair work
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC1. prepare the surface of damaged walls, ceilings, or floors by cleaning and removing loose
material
Apply repair materials and monitor curing process
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC2. apply repair materials such as mortar, plaster, and waterproof coatings to restore the
structural integrity of the building
PC3. follow specific repair methodologies based on material types (e.g., plaster repairs, brickwork
repairs, waterproofing applications)
PC4. monitor the curing and setting process to ensure the repair work is completed to standard
Knowledge and Understanding (KU)
The individual on the job needs to know and understand:
KU1. the types and properties of repair materials including cement mortar, polymer-modified
mortars, plaster compounds, waterproofing membranes, and surface coatings
KU2. surface preparation techniques for different substrates including concrete, masonry, and
plastered surfaces, such as hacking, grinding, and wire brushing
KU3. standard methodologies for masonry repair including tuck pointing, repointing, and brick or
block replacement procedures
KU4. types of waterproofing systems including crystalline waterproofing, cementitious coatings,
bituminous membranes, and injection grouting
KU5. plastering techniques for repair work including the application of scratch coat, brown coat,
and finish coat
KU6. curing requirements, methods, and duration for different categories of repair materials

CBWE/CON/N0103: Perform Crack and Joint Repairs (Epoxy, Polyurethane
Injection)
Description
This unit describes the skills and knowledge required to identify and assess cracks and joints in structures,
select and prepare appropriate injection materials, inject materials to restore structural integrity, and
monitor the curing process to ensure effective sealing and bonding.
Scope
The scope covers the following :
Identify and assess cracks and joints requiring repair
Execute crack injection repair and monitor curing
Elements and Performance Criteria
Identify and assess cracks and joints requiring repair
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC1. identify cracks and joints that need repair and assess their depth, width, and severity
PC2. select and prepare appropriate materials (epoxy, polyurethane, etc.) for crack injection
Execute crack injection repair and monitor curing
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC3. inject the chosen material into the cracks to restore the structural integrity of the building
PC4. monitor the curing process to ensure effective bonding and sealing of the cracks
Knowledge and Understanding (KU)
The individual on the job needs to know and understand:
KU1. types of cracks in concrete and masonry structures, including shrinkage cracks, structural
cracks, and settlement cracks, and their typical causes
KU2. crack severity assessment criteria including crack width classification, depth measurement
using gauges and probes, and activity monitoring using tell-tales
KU3. properties and structural applications of epoxy resin systems for crack repair and structural
bonding
KU4. properties and applications of polyurethane injection materials for crack sealing, water stop,
and flexible joint filling
KU5. equipment and tools used for crack injection including low-pressure injection ports, packers,
high-pressure injection equipment, and hand-operated grout pumps
KU6. stages of the crack injection process including port placement, injection sequence, and
completion indicators
KU7. curing requirements and post-injection inspection and monitoring procedures

CBWE/CON/N0104: Apply Structural Re-Strengthening Techniques (Steel,
Concrete, FRP)
Description
This unit describes the skills and knowledge required to evaluate the need for structural strengthening,
prepare surfaces for the application of strengthening materials, install and secure reinforcement materials
including steel plates and fiber-reinforced polymers (FRP), and ensure that re-strengthening work
integrates seamlessly with existing structures and complies with engineering standards.
Scope
The scope covers the following :
Evaluate strengthening requirements and prepare surfaces
Install reinforcement materials and ensure compliance
Elements and Performance Criteria
Evaluate strengthening requirements and prepare surfaces
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC1. evaluate the need for structural strengthening based on load-bearing requirements or damage
analysis
PC2. prepare the surfaces for the application of strengthening materials (e.g., steel plates, carbon
fiber wraps)
Install reinforcement materials and ensure compliance
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC3. install and secure reinforcement materials, including steel plates or fiber-reinforced polymers
(FRP), as per engineering specifications
PC4. ensure the re-strengthening work is integrated seamlessly with existing structures and
complies with building codes
Knowledge and Understanding (KU)
The individual on the job needs to know and understand:
KU1. structural strengthening methods including externally bonded steel plates, externally
bonded FRP systems, and reinforced concrete jacketing
KU2. properties and characteristics of structural strengthening materials including structural steel,
CFRP, GFRP, and AFRP
KU3. surface preparation requirements for bonding strengthening materials including grinding,
sandblasting, and priming
KU4. process of steel plate bonding including adhesive selection, application sequence, and
mechanical anchoring
