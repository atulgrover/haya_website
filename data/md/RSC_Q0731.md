RSC/Q0731: Material Handling and Storage Assistant (Rubber & Polymer)
Brief Job Description
A Material Handling and Storage Assistant (Rubber & Polymer) is responsible for supporting production by
handling, sorting, and storing raw materials, chemicals, and finished products. The individual applies
safety and environmental guidelines while managing rubber and polymer materials, maintains stock
records, and ensures timely movement of materials to and from storage areas to meet production
requirements.
Personal Attributes
The individual should be attentive to detail, physically fit, and able to work in a plant/warehouse
environment. They must possess alertness, discipline, and adherence to safety standards while handling
rubber, polymers, and chemicals. The role requires basic communication skills, teamwork, time
management, and the ability to follow instructions accurately to ensure smooth material movement and
storage.
Applicable National Occupational Standards (NOS)
Compulsory NOS:
- 1. RSC/N1621: Prepare to receive and inspect incoming materials
- 2. RSC/N1622: Perform storage and stacking activities
- 3. RSC/N1623: Carry out material handling equipment operations
- 4. RSC/N5007: Follow health & safety protocol
- 5. DGT/VSQ/N0101: Employability Skills (30 Hours)

RSC/N1621: Prepare to receive and inspect incoming materials
Description
This NOS involves checking documentation, identifying materials, and arranging tools or equipment
required for inspection. It ensures readiness to receive, verify, and handle materials as per safety and
quality standards in rubber and polymer industries.
Scope
The scope covers the following :
Check delivery documents for material match
Arrange tools and safety gear
Confirm storage and unloading
Elements and Performance Criteria
Understanding Documentation and Material Verification
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC1. identify and verify the accompanying documents such as purchase orders, invoices, delivery
challans, and material safety data sheets (MSDS) for incoming materials
PC2. check and match the details on the documentation with the actual material received
including quantity, material code, batch number, and supplier information
PC3. ensure that all materials are labelled properly and match the specifications mentioned in the
documentation and the purchase order
PC4. report any mismatch or missing documentation to the supervisor or relevant authority as per
the organization’s standard operating procedure (SOP)
PC5. record the receipt of materials accurately in manual registers or digital inventory systems as
instructed
PC6. segregate and place materials in the designated inspection area until quality checks are
completed
PC7. maintain proper documentation of accepted and rejected materials for traceability and audit
purposes
PC8. follow the company’s procedures for handling, checking, and documentation of incoming
materials in compliance with safety and quality guidelines
Performing Visual and Dimensional Inspection
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC9. perform visual inspection of incoming materials for any physical damage, contamination,
incorrect labeling, or packaging defects
PC10. identify surface defects such as cracks, discoloration, warping, or deformation using standard
visual inspection methods
PC11. use appropriate measuring instruments like vernier calipers, measuring tapes, gauges, or
scales to check dimensional accuracy of the materials

RSC/N1622: Perform storage and stacking activities
Description
This NOS covers the competencies required to systematically store and stack materials in designated
locations while ensuring safety, accessibility, and inventory accuracy. It includes organizing materials
based on type, size, and usage frequency, and following standard procedures to prevent damage or
contamination.
Scope
The scope covers the following :
Understand and Identify Storage Requirements
Perform Organized Stacking and Labeling Activities
Maintain Inventory Documentation and Clean Storage Environment
Elements and Performance Criteria
Understand and Identify Storage Requirements
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC1. identify different types of rubber and polymer materials and their specific storage needs,
such as temperature control, moisture protection, and segregation
PC2. understand and explain material compatibility and the importance of storing hazardous
materials separately to avoid contamination or reactions
PC3. state the storage guidelines for perishable or sensitive materials based on batch number,
expiry date, or shelf life
PC4. recognize and point out the designated storage zones (e.g., raw material zone, finished
goods zone, quarantine area) as per workplace layout and material classification
PC5. list out and define the requirements for racking systems, pallets, and storage containers
suitable for different types of rubber and polymer products
PC6. prepare and maintain basic storage checklists to ensure correct stacking, placement, and
compliance with safety regulations
PC7. discuss the role of FIFO (First-In-First-Out) or FEFO (First-Expiry-First-Out) principles in
maintaining material quality during storage
PC8. summarize the effects of improper storage on material degradation, waste, or safety
hazards, and elaborate on preventive actions
Perform Organized Stacking and Labeling Activities
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC9. perform systematic stacking of rubber and polymer materials using appropriate tools and
equipment, ensuring stability and safety
PC10. ensure that materials are stacked as per designated categories such as type, grade, batch
number, and production/expiry date
PC11. label stacked items clearly with relevant information including material code, lot number,
quantity, and hazard symbols if applicable

RSC/N1623: Carry out material handling equipment operations
Description
This NOS outlines the skills and knowledge required to operate material handling equipment such as
forklifts, pallet jacks, and trolleys safely and efficiently. It includes loading, unloading, moving, and placing
materials as per work instructions while following safety standards and equipment maintenance protocols.
Scope
The scope covers the following :
Understand and Identify Types of Material Handling Equipment (MHE)
Perform Pre-Operational Checks and Safe Operation of MHE
Support Smooth and Timely Material Movement Across Work Areas
Elements and Performance Criteria
Understand and Identify Types of Material Handling Equipment (MHE)
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC1. identify different types of material handling equipment such as pallet trucks, forklifts, hand
trolleys, conveyor belts, and cranes used in rubber and polymer operations
PC2. explain the primary functions, capabilities, and limitations of various MHE relevant to
movement and storage of raw materials, compounds, and finished rubber products
PC3. classify MHE based on manual, semi-automatic, and automatic operations and their
suitability for specific handling tasks
PC4. state the importance of choosing the right MHE based on material type, weight, dimensions,
and handling frequency
PC5. differentiate between MHE used for vertical movement (like hoists or forklifts) and horizontal
movement (like pallet jacks or trolleys)
PC6. describe the ergonomic and safety features to look for while operating or assisting with MHE
PC7. point out risks associated with incorrect equipment selection, such as product damage,
operator injury, or workflow disruption
PC8. list out basic maintenance and operational indicators for commonly used MHE (e.g., brake
condition, wheel alignment, battery status, etc.)

fueled or charged adequately before operation
