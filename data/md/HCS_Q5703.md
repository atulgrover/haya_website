HCS/Q5703: Kota Doria Artisan
Brief Job Description
A Kota Doria artisan specialises in crafting lightweight, translucent fabrics woven with cotton and silk
yarns, known for their distinctive square-check pattern and delicate texture. These skilled artisans often
work on traditional pit looms, preserving a centuries-old weaving heritage.
Personal Attributes
Kota Doria artisans are known for their patience, precision, and deep-rooted dedication to traditional
craftsmanship. They possess a keen eye for detail, strong hand-weaving skills, and a commitment to
preserving cultural heritage through their meticulous work.

HCS/N5720: Understand the heritage and characteristics of Kota Doria
Weaving
Description
This unit covers the heritage, evolution, and geographical roots of Kota Doria weaving, highlighting its
signature checkered design, traditional craftsmanship, and cultural significance in Rajasthan. It also
explores artisan techniques, fabric characteristics, and the adaptation of this lightweight textile in
contemporary fashion and sustainable practices.
Scope
The scope covers the following :
Historical and Geographical Context of Kota Doria
Identify the Key Characteristics of Kota Doria Fabric
Recognise the Role of Artisans in Kota Doria Weaving
Understand the Significance of Kota Doria in Modern Fashion and Culture
Design Conceptualisation and Translation
Elements and Performance Criteria
Historical and Geographical Context of Kota Doria
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC1. Describe the historical origins by identifying roots in Kota, Rajasthan, and outlining the
evolution of the weaving tradition over time.
PC2. Explain the cultural importance by highlighting its royal associations and its continued role in
traditional regional attire.
PC3. Identify the Hadoti region Kota, Bundi, and Baran as the primary production area, linking
geographical setting to craft development.
PC4. Explain how the migration of weaving communities and support from royal households
contributed to the refinement and preservation of the techniques.
PC5. Explore how local climate, geography, and customs influenced patterns, texture, and overall
weaving style.
Identify the Key Characteristics of Kota Doria Fabric
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC6. Define the term “Doria” and identify the hallmark of the fabric, the small square checks
woven into it, commonly known as “Kota khat” or “Kota Doria checks.”
PC7. Outline the traditional use of cotton yarns and explain how contemporary variations blend
silk and synthetics to enhance strength and sheen.
PC8. Describe the integration of Zari (gold or silver thread) into both heritage and modern
versions, contributing to the fabric’s decorative elegance.
PC9. Explain the fabric’s lightweight and sheer nature, created by the use of fine yarns and an
open weave, making it ideal for hot and humid climates.

HCS/N5721: Identify and handle tools, equipment and raw materials
Description
This unit equips learners with the knowledge and skills to identify, handle, and maintain traditional and
modern handloom weaving tools and raw materials. It covers safe handling practices, pre-loom operations,
material assessment, equipment upkeep, and workplace safety to ensure smooth and efficient weaving
processes.
Scope
The scope covers the following :
Identify and Maintain Weaving Tools and Equipment
Safe Operation and Storage of Handloom Equipment
Identify and Assess Raw Materials
Handle and Prepare Raw Material for Weaving
Ensure Workplace Safety and Preventive Maintenance
Elements and Performance Criteria
Identify and Maintain Weaving Tools and Equipment
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC1. Identify traditional and modern tools used in handloom weaving, including pit looms, reeds,
heddles, shuttles, beaters, and measuring implements.
PC2. Distinguish between types of looms and their components, recognising their role in achieving
specific weave patterns.
PC3. Inspect all tools for signs of wear or damage before use, ensuring functionality and safety.
PC4. Maintain tools in good working condition through regular cleaning and lubrication of moving
parts.
PC5. Record and report any faulty tools or malfunctioning equipment to ensure timely repair or
replacement.
Safe Operation and Storage of Handloom Equipment
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC6. Demonstrate safe handling of handloom components and sharp tools, such as scissors and
cutting blades, during weaving processes.
PC7. Follow standard procedures for assembling and dismantling weaving tools, avoiding damage
or misalignment.
PC8. Use personal protective equipment (PPE) such as gloves or eyewear when handling tools that
pose physical risk.
PC9. Maintain a clean and hazard-free workspace, including clear pathways and safe storage of
equipment.
PC10. Store tools in designated areas after use to prevent loss, rusting, or accidental injury.
Identify and Assess Raw Materials
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:

PC25. Keep maintenance logs for looms and tools, documenting cleaning schedules and repairs to
ensure consistent performance.
Knowledge and Understanding (KU)

KU1. Knowledge of traditional and modern weaving tools such as pit looms, reeds, heddles,

KU3. Awareness of the need to inspect tools and equipment for wear or damage before use.
KU4. Understanding the importance of regular maintenance, cleaning, and lubrication of weaving
tools.
KU5. Knowledge of how to report faulty or malfunctioning tools for timely repair or replacement.

HCS/N5722: Prepare and set up the Loom for Kota Doria Weaving
Description
This unit covers the comprehensive process of preparing and setting up the loom for Kota Doria weaving. It
includes inspecting and maintaining loom components, preparing warp and weft mechanisms, interpreting
design layouts, ensuring ergonomic and safety conditions, and conducting trial weaving to achieve high-
quality fabric production while enabling creative design adaptations.
Scope
The scope covers the following :
Inspect and Maintain Loom Components
Prepare the Warp and Beam the Loom
Set Up the Weft Insertion and Shuttle Mechanism
Configure Loom Settings and Conduct Trial Weaving
Interpret Design Instructions and Weaving Layout
Ensure Safety and Ergonomic Conditions
Calibrate and Adjust Tension and Settings
Adaptation and Innovation in Kota Doria Design
Elements and Performance Criteria
Inspect and Maintain Loom Components
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC1. Examine the loom thoroughly for signs of wear, misalignment, or damage prior to use,
ensuring safe and efficient operation.
PC2. Clean key components -reed, heddles, shafts, beams and removing accumulated dust, lint,
and old yarn residues.
PC3. Use soft brushes or cloth to eliminate debris that may hinder weaving quality or machine
movement.
PC4. Apply appropriate lubrication to moving parts as per maintenance protocol to ensure smooth
mechanical function.
PC5. Report any issues beyond routine upkeep, such as broken gears or loosened joints to the
supervisor for prompt action.
Prepare the Warp and Beam the Loom
To be competent, the user/individual on the job must be able to:
PC6. Mount the warp beam securely onto the loom frame, ensuring it is properly aligned for
balanced rotation.
PC7. Draw warp threads accurately through lease rods, heddles, and the reed according to the
threading plan.
PC8. Maintain even spacing and uniform tension across the warp to avoid weaving defects.
PC9. Securely tie warp ends to the cloth beam using standard knotting techniques suitable for
Kota Doria weaving.

| Sector | Sector is a conglomeration of different business operations having similar business and interests. It may also be defined as a distinct subset of the economy whose components share similar characteristics and interests. |
| :--- | :---: |
| Sub-sector | Sub-sector is derived from a further breakdown based on the characteristics and interests of its components. |
| Occupation | Occupation is a set of job roles, which perform similar/ related set of functions in an industry. |
| Job role | Job role defines a unique set of functions that together form a unique employment opportunity in an organisation. |
| Occupational Standards (OS) | OS specify the standards of performance an individual must achieve when carrying out a function in the workplace, together with the Knowledge and Understanding (KU) they need to meet that standard consistently. Occupational Standards are applicable both in the Indian and global contexts. |
| Performance Criteria (PC) | Performance Criteria (PC) are statements that together specify the standard of performance required when carrying out a task. |
| National Occupational Standards (NOS) | NOS are occupational standards which apply uniquely in the Indian context. |
